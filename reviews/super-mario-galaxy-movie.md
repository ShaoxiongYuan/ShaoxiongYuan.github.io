There is a reliable comfort in watching Mario do what Mario does, and *The Super Mario Galaxy Movie* delivers that comfort competently. The galaxy setting gives the animation team genuine room to play, and the visual imagination applied to floating planetoids and cosmic spectacle produces some genuinely lovely sequences. The world is bigger this time, the story more complicated, and the film wears that scale with reasonable confidence.

The added plot layers give the film a sense of appropriately cosmic stakes, but the narrative never quite finds the emotional specificity to make the complication feel meaningful rather than simply busy. The villain is taken down. Good triumphs. The credits roll.

For families with younger children, it does everything it needs to. For everyone else, it sits comfortably in the category of perfectly watchable without being particularly memorable. Mario has saved the universe now. The franchise’s next question is whether it wants to grow deeper or simply keep growing larger.
