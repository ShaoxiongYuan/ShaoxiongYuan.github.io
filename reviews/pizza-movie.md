Sometimes you just want to watch something completely unhinged, and *Pizza Movie* delivers that specific pleasure without apology or pretension. This is my second pure fantasy film of the year after *Good Luck, Have Fun, Don't Die*, and while the comparison is inevitable, *Pizza Movie* occupies a slightly different register: lighter, sillier, and considerably less interested in saying anything meaningful about the human condition. That is both its greatest charm and the reason it sits a notch below.

The premise is exactly as absurd as it sounds. A pill. Six hallucinatory phases. A school setting that the film uses as a playground for exaggerating every recognizable campus experience to its logical extreme: automated robots, invasive room checks, petty bullying, school romances, the social calculus of which party to attend and which friend group to betray for the night, peculiar pets, and dormitory conditions that anyone who has ever lived in student housing will recognize with painful accuracy. The film knows these environments and inflates them with gleeful precision, as if someone asked what would happen if every minor institutional absurdity were turned up to its maximum volume simultaneously.

The squad's central quest, descend two flights of stairs, eat the pizza, end the hallucinations, is a beautifully stupid MacGuffin, and the film commits to it with complete sincerity. What makes it work is that the logic, however surreal, is internally consistent. Every small question the opening raises gets answered. The pieces fit together in ways that reward attention, which is more than most pure comedy films bother to attempt.

The cleverest touch is the inclusion of the directors discussing how to shoot specific scenes, a meta layer that arrives as a side effect of taking more than one dose of the pill. It is exactly the kind of self-aware structural joke that could easily feel smug but lands as genuinely funny because the film has already established enough goodwill to pull it off. The cast are uniformly well-suited to this register: committed, energetic, and unafraid of looking ridiculous, which is the only way this kind of material survives contact with actual human beings.

The honest limitation is that *Pizza Movie* is exactly what it appears to be and nothing more. Where *Good Luck, Have Fun, Don't Die* hid something worth thinking about beneath its fantasy surface, *Pizza Movie* is content to be a very good time that evaporates pleasantly on the way home. There is no shame in that. Not every film needs to leave a mark.

This one leaves a smile. On a good day, that is more than enough.
