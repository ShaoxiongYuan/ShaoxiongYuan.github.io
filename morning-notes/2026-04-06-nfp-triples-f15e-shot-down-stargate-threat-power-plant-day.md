## What You Missed: The 65-Hour Gap

Markets closed Thursday at 4 PM ET and reopened this morning. Here is everything, in order.

### Friday, April 4: NFP Triples Expectations

The March nonfarm payrolls report, released at 8:30 AM Friday while markets were closed for Good Friday, was a shock:

- **+178,000 jobs** vs. 59,000 consensus, three times expectations.
- **Unemployment**: 4.3%, down from February. But the decline was partly driven by a sharp reduction in the labor force: people stopping their job search, not getting hired.
- **Healthcare**: +76,000. The Kaiser Permanente strike that depressed February's numbers unwound. This was the largest single-sector contribution.
- **Revisions**: February revised down by 41,000 (to -174K); January revised up by 34,000 (to +160K). Three-month average: ~68,000, still well below the ~150K needed to keep pace with population growth.
- **10Y reaction**: Within minutes of the release, the 10-year Treasury yield surged past 4.30% and settled at 4.35%. Bond traders couldn't trade equities but they could trade bonds, and they sold them. The message: a strong labor market + $112 oil = no rate cuts, possibly hikes.

**What it means:** The headline looks great, but the composition is weak. Strip out healthcare's Kaiser bounce and you get ~100K across all other sectors. The labor force shrank. The three-month average is 68K. This is a labor market that looks resilient on the surface but is cooling underneath. The bond market's reaction is the tell: yields surged because the Fed can't cut into a 178K print with oil at $112. The Warsh confirmation hearings in mid-April just got more interesting. He inherits an economy where labor is strong enough to justify hawkishness but fragile enough that hawkishness could break it.

### Friday Night–Saturday: F-15E Shot Down — The Most Dramatic 48 Hours of the War

On Friday, April 3, an American F-15E Strike Eagle was shot down by enemy fire over Iran. What followed was the most gripping military drama since the war began:

- **The shoot-down**: The F-15E was struck during an air campaign sortie. Both crew members, the pilot and weapons systems officer (WSO), ejected.
- **Pilot rescued**: The pilot was recovered by US forces within hours. Standard combat search-and-rescue.
- **WSO trapped in the Zagros Mountains**: The weapons systems officer, whom Trump later described as a "highly respected Colonel," landed in a mountain crevice in the Zagros range. He spent more than 24 hours evading capture while the IRGC, SEAL Team Six, and local nomadic tribesmen all raced to find him.
- **The rescue operation**: Hundreds of special operations forces, dozens of warplanes and helicopters were deployed. Two Black Hawk helicopters came under fire. Two US transport planes suffered critical problems and had to be destroyed on the ground to prevent capture. The US effectively built an improvised airfield inside Iran to extract the airman.
- **"SAFE and SOUND"**: Trump announced just after midnight Sunday that the WSO was rescued. The White House had maintained complete silence during the operation to avoid compromising it.

**What it means:** The F-15E shoot-down is the most significant US aircraft loss of the war, the first manned combat aircraft downed since operations began. It demonstrates that Iran's air defense network, while degraded, is not destroyed. The rescue operation's complexity (airfield construction inside Iran, multiple aircraft under fire, 24+ hours of evasion) underscores that this is a real shooting war with real risk to American servicemembers. Fifteen US service members have now been killed. The political dynamic is shifting: the rescue dominated weekend news coverage and made the war feel personal and dangerous in a way that air strike videos don't.

### Saturday–Sunday: The Ceasefire That Wasn't

While the rescue operation dominated headlines, the diplomatic track hit a wall.

**The 45-Day Proposal:**
- Mediators, led by Pakistan's Field Marshal Asim Munir, circulated a 45-day ceasefire proposal tentatively dubbed the "Islamabad Accord." Munir was in contact "all night long" with VP JD Vance, envoy Steve Witkoff, and Iranian FM Araghchi.
- The framework: 45-day ceasefire as phase one, leading to a permanent peace deal. Regional framework for Strait of Hormuz. Final in-person talks in Islamabad.

**Iran Rejected It:**
- Iran's state news agency IRNA confirmed Tehran rejected the temporary ceasefire.
- Iran responded with its own 10-clause counter-proposal demanding: (1) a permanent end to the war, not a 45-day pause; (2) a protocol for safe Hormuz passage under Iranian sovereignty; (3) lifting all sanctions; (4) ending regional hostilities including Lebanon; (5) reconstruction assistance; and several other conditions.
- A US official described Iran's demands as "maximalist."

**Trump at the Easter Egg Roll:**
- When asked about Iran's response: "It's not good enough, but it's a very significant step."
- This is the most conciliatory Trump has been about any Iranian diplomatic communication. He acknowledged Iran's counter-proposal as meaningful, even while rejecting its terms.

**What it means:** The 45-day ceasefire is dead in its proposed form. But something important happened: Iran submitted a structured 10-clause counter-proposal. That's not rejection; that's negotiation. Iran is saying "we won't accept your terms, but here are ours." The gap between the US position (temporary ceasefire, Hormuz reopens first) and Iran's position (permanent peace, sanctions lifted, reconstruction) is enormous. But both sides are now negotiating through Pakistan. The Islamabad Accord framework is alive even if the first draft is dead.

### Sunday–Monday: Trump Sets Tuesday Deadline. "Power Plant Day."

Trump escalated his rhetoric to the most extreme of the war:

- **"Power Plant Day and Bridge Day"**: Trump posted that Tuesday will be "Power Plant Day, and Bridge Day, all wrapped up in one, in Iran."
- **The quote**: "Open the f***in' Strait, you crazy bastards, or you'll be living in Hell. JUST WATCH!"
- **Deadline**: Tuesday, April 7, 8 PM ET. Trump said he is "highly unlikely" to postpone.
- **"Every bridge in Iran will be decimated"**: An explicit threat to destroy civilian transportation infrastructure.
- **"Taken out in one night"**: Trump said Iran could be "taken out in one night," implying the US has the capability to simultaneously strike all critical infrastructure.
- **The contradiction**: When asked directly, Trump said he "doesn't know" if he's winding down or escalating the war. This is the most candid admission yet that even the president isn't sure how this ends.

**What it means:** Tuesday at 8 PM ET is now the most important moment of the war. If Trump follows through with simultaneous strikes on power plants, bridges, and oil infrastructure, Iran faces a humanitarian catastrophe. 88 million people without electricity. The international backlash would be severe. Iran has already announced plans for "human chains" around power plants. If Trump blinks with another postponement like March 23, his credibility on deadlines evaporates and Iran's negotiating position strengthens. The market needs to price both outcomes over the next 36 hours.

### Monday: Israel Destroys South Pars. Sharif University Hit. IRGC Intelligence Chief Killed.

Israel continued its independent escalation on Monday morning:

- **South Pars petrochemical plant**: Israel's Defense Minister Katz confirmed a "powerful strike on the largest petrochemical facility in Iran," the South Pars complex, responsible for 50% of Iran's petrochemical production. The gas field, shared with Qatar, is the world's largest, sitting under the Persian Gulf.
- **Sharif University of Technology**: An airstrike hit the campus in Tehran, near Azadi Square. 34 killed. The university has been sanctioned by multiple countries for its work on Iran's ballistic missile program with the IRGC. This is the first confirmed strike on a university campus.
- **IRGC Intelligence Chief killed**: Major General Majid Khademi, head of IRGC intelligence, was killed in the Tehran strikes. This follows the assassinations of Khamenei and senior leadership on Day 1; the IRGC's command structure is being systematically dismantled.
- **Iran responded**: Missile fire on Israel and Gulf Arab neighbors following the strikes.

**What it means:** Israel is accelerating. Hitting South Pars (50% of petrochemical production), a university, and the IRGC intelligence chief on the same day, while ceasefire negotiations are active, is a deliberate statement: Israel is not part of any ceasefire and will continue destroying Iran's capabilities regardless. The South Pars strike affects Qatar's shared gas field, potentially drawing Gulf states further into the diplomatic effort. The university strike will generate international condemnation. Israel is racing to inflict maximum damage before any ceasefire framework constrains it.

### Monday: IRGC Threatens to "Annihilate" OpenAI's $30B Stargate Data Center

The IRGC's escalation against tech infrastructure went to a new level:

- **The threat**: IRGC spokesperson Ebrahim Zolfaghari released a video vowing "complete and utter annihilation" of OpenAI's $30 billion Stargate AI data center in Abu Dhabi if the US strikes Iran's power plants.
- **The target**: Stargate is a joint venture between OpenAI, SoftBank, and Oracle, the flagship AI infrastructure project in the Middle East.
- **The broader threat**: "All power plants, energy infrastructure, and information and communications technology of the Zionist regime, and all similar companies within the region that have American shareholders shall face complete and utter annihilation."
- **Precedent**: The IRGC already struck AWS data centers in Bahrain and an Oracle data center in Dubai earlier in the war.

**What it means:** This is a direct escalation ladder. Trump threatens Iran's power plants, so the IRGC threatens the Stargate data center. Tit-for-tat infrastructure targeting. The Stargate facility is a $30 billion asset involving OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle, and NVIDIA. An attack would be the most significant destruction of tech infrastructure in the history of warfare. It would immediately rerate every US tech company with Middle East operations. The market hasn't priced this because it seems too escalatory, but the IRGC already hit AWS. They have the capability and the stated intent.
