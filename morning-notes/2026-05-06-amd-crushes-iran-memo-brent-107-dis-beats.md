Wednesday pre-market, 7:00 AM ET. The tape this morning has flipped from war premium to peace premium in roughly fourteen hours, and the read across is everywhere. AMD delivered the cleanest semi print of the cycle after Tuesday's close, with $10.25 billion in revenue (+38% year over year), a $5.8 billion data center segment (+57%), and a Q2 revenue guide of $10.9 to $11.5 billion that runs $400 million above the Street. The stock is +18% to +20% premarket and the AI capex narrative just got its second wind. Then at roughly 9:30 PM ET, Axios broke that the White House is "closing in" on a one-page, 14-point memorandum of understanding with Iran, negotiated by Witkoff and Kushner, that would declare the war over and open a 30-day window for nuclear and Hormuz technicals. Brent has crashed from Monday's $114 high to $106.52 (off about 6% on the day, off 8% from the overnight peak), WTI is at $102.76, the VIX has unwound to 16.73, and S&P futures are +0.77% with the Nasdaq +1.28% on the AMD halo. We are constructive on the day but cautious on the durability of the Iran memo, because the same 14-point framework was the basis of Sunday's proposal that became Tuesday's UAE drone strike on Fujairah. The market is pricing 70% probability the memo lands by Friday. We are at 55%.

The information sequence is what matters today. AMD ran into the print up 64% in April, and the binary that desks were trading was gross margin. Lisa Su delivered a Q1 GM at 54.1%, in line, with a Q2 guide at 54.5%, fifty basis points above consensus, and the data center sub-segment growing 57% on a comp that was already 100%+ a year earlier. The MI355 ramp commentary was unambiguous, and Su flagged "second-half shipments of Meta chips" with the OpenAI 6GW commitment recompiled into the FY27 build plan rather than backloaded. SMCI separately reported earlier in the AMC slate, with non-GAAP EPS at $0.84 against $0.62 consensus and Q4 revenue guide at $11 to $12.5 billion (well above $9.8 billion expected), driving the stock +18% after-hours. Read across, the AI infrastructure print is doing the structural work: AMD's data center revenue is now nearly $24 billion annualized at 57% growth, SMCI is annualized at $44 billion, and ARM reports tonight after the close. The entire AI capex worry that desks carried into Q1 (whether DeepSeek-style efficiency had pulled forward demand) is now off the table for two full quarters. Our 12-month AMD PT moves from $215 to $260, the call vertical we recommended last week is +110% mark-to-market, and we are letting the spread run to the June expiry rather than rolling.

Three things into Wednesday:

1. **AMD is the print of the year for semis, and the multiple has room to expand.** Q1 revenue $10.25B (+38% y/y), data center +57%, Q2 guide $11.2B (+18% above $9.5B Q1), full-year revenue trajectory now firmly above $43B. The stock has run 64% in April but the multiple is still 38x forward EPS against NVDA at 35x, and the relative value case has just been argued by Lisa Su's GM trajectory. We hold the long, lift the short put leg of the May 16 $200 strike, and roll the June $215/$240 call vertical out to July $250/$280. The MI400 commentary in 2H gives the story a second leg into NVDA's May 28 print.

2. **The Iran memo is the single biggest peace-versus-war binary the tape has seen since April 8.** A signed memo by Friday means Brent goes to the upper $90s, the energy long book unwinds 8% to 10%, and the defense basket gets repriced 4% to 6% lower. A failed memo (third 14-point framework collapse in three weeks) means Brent re-tests $115 and the VIX is back through 20. The asymmetry sits with a 30-day standstill that locks neither side, leaving Hormuz transit fragile and the $108 to $112 range as the new norm. We are trimming half the calendar-spread reversal we put on Tuesday and replacing it with a Brent September $95 / $115 strangle, paying about $4.20.

3. **The hyperscaler capex print has flipped the equity tape constructive.** With AMD's beat coming after MSFT, GOOGL, META, AMZN, and ORCL all confirming FY26 capex above $710 billion in aggregate, and now PLTR's $7.66B FY guide and AMD's $11.2B Q2 guide layering on top, the AI infrastructure thesis has its strongest week of confirmation since November 2024. Position adds: long basket of AMD, NVDA, AVGO, ARM (into tonight), MU, with funding from a partial trim in defense.

## The Iran Memo and Hormuz Tape Day 3

The memo language reported by Axios overnight is the most forward US-Iran framework since the war started in late February. The 14 points include: a public declaration the war is over; a 30-day negotiation window on a final agreement; an Iranian moratorium on nuclear enrichment; a US commitment to lift sanctions and release frozen assets; bilateral lifting of restrictions on Strait of Hormuz transit; and a structured diplomatic track through Geneva starting around May 22. Witkoff and Kushner are the negotiators. Pakistan-mediated technicals are layered on top. The US expects Iranian responses on key points "in the next 48 hours."

The market is pricing this at roughly 70% probability of a signed memo by Friday. We are at 55%, for three reasons. First, the Sunday 14-point proposal (which became Tuesday's Fujairah strike) and the new memo share roughly nine of fourteen points, which means Iran's response is repackaging an existing framework. The new five points are the binding ones. Second, the kinetic clock has not stopped. CENTCOM's six-boat sinking on Tuesday and a continuing Project Freedom escort schedule means the memo has to survive any further Iranian provocation through Friday. Third, the UAE airspace closure earlier this week and the ADNOC Fujairah outage mean the GCC has not yet been brought into the memo's framework, which is operationally awkward.

The base case is the memo is signed in some form, but contingent rather than binding. The market is treating it as binding. This is why Brent has crashed from $114 to $106.52 (a roughly $8 move) and the front-month curve has steepened sharply in backwardation. We are reversing half the calendar-spread reversal we put on Tuesday morning and replacing with a strangle, because the binary tail risk both ways is now worth more than directional exposure.

**Two market-moving headlines we expect today:**
- White House readout on Iranian response, expected mid-morning ET (10:00 to 12:00)
- Trump statement on the memo, tone matters more than substance. A "Iran is being smart" framing is bullish risk; an "Iran has 24 hours" framing is bearish risk.
