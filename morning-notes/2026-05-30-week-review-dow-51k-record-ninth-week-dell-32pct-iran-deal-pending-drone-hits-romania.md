Saturday wrap, 9:00 AM ET, with the cash tape closed for the weekend and the week that ended Friday standing as one of the most structurally constructive five-session stretches of the year. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,580.08 (up 0.22 percent Friday, up 1.4 percent on the week) for its ninth consecutive weekly gain, the longest such streak since 2023. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 363 points or 0.72 percent to 51,032.46, closing above 51,000 for the first time in history. The Nasdaq Composite finished at 26,972.62 (up 0.26 percent Friday), capping an 8 percent gain for the month of May. The Russell 2000 was the notable laggard, closing down 0.59 percent at 2,919.34 on Friday as small caps failed to participate in the large-cap record-setting session. On the month, the S&P gained 5.5 percent, the Nasdaq 8 percent, and the Dow 2.5 percent, making May 2026 one of the strongest months for risk assets on record.

Three forces drove the week to fresh records:

1. **Dell Technologies posted the cleanest single AI-infrastructure beat of the cycle on Thursday after the close, then surged 32 percent on Friday for its best single-day performance ever.** Revenue came in at $43.8 billion, up 88 percent year on year and an $8 billion beat over the $35.5 billion consensus. AI-optimized server revenue hit $16.1 billion, up 757 percent year on year. The company booked $24.4 billion of AI orders in the single quarter, raised the FY27 AI server revenue guide to $60 billion from the prior $50 billion, and lifted total FY27 revenue guidance to $167 billion versus the $142.1 billion consensus. The read-through cascades through the entire AI-infrastructure stack. Micron earlier in the week briefly crossed the $1 trillion market capitalization threshold on the back of a UBS upgrade to a $1,625 price target, closing up 19.3 percent on Monday alone, as the HBM (high-bandwidth memory) shortage continues to accelerate pricing power. The AI capex cycle has not stalled. It is in fact accelerating into the June 1 Jensen Huang Computex Taipei keynote with the Vera Rubin NVL72 rack platform unveil.

2. **The tentative US-Iran 60-day Hormuz framework that emerged late Thursday is the most substantive step forward in the diplomatic track this cycle.** The proposed agreement would extend the ceasefire, reopen the Strait of Hormuz with mine clearance within 30 days, guarantee unrestricted shipping with no tolls, lift the US blockade on Iranian ports, and launch nuclear talks during the 60-day window. Two sticking points remain: the size of the Iranian enriched uranium stockpile reduction and Iran's stated preference for a transit service fee. President Trump has not yet signed off. The late-Thursday Iranian missile salvo at Kuwait, intercepted by Kuwaiti defenses and framed by US Central Command as a ceasefire violation, is a reminder that hardliners retain escalation capacity even as the diplomatic track advances. Brent crude fell to $91.37, down 1.4 percent on Friday, and WTI to $87.20, down 2 percent, both touching six-week lows on net deal optimism. Meanwhile, a Russian drone crashed into an apartment building in Galati, Romania on Friday, injuring two people, the first time a Russian strike weapon has hit a NATO member state during the Ukraine war. Romania expelled the Russian consul in Constanta and requested faster delivery of NATO anti-drone capabilities. NATO Secretary General Rutte affirmed the alliance's readiness to "defend every inch of Allied territory," and the incident raises the baseline risk premium on European security even as the Iran track de-escalates.

3. **The April Core PCE at 0.2 percent month on month (cooler than the 0.3 percent consensus) and 3.3 percent year on year (in-line) confirmed that the war-induced energy passthrough peaked in March and core disinflation remains intact.** The headline PCE cooled sharply from 0.7 percent month on month in March to 0.4 percent in April, though the year-on-year at 3.8 percent remains elevated on the residual energy pulse. The 10Y Treasury bid six basis points lower on the week to 4.44 percent, its lowest level in two weeks, as the bull-flatten reasserted on the cooler core read plus the Hormuz deal headlines. The Chicago PMI delivered a blowout at 62.7 versus 50.6 consensus, the highest since 2022 and the second-largest single-month gain since the index began in 1967, with new orders at their strongest since January 2022. The UMich Sentiment Final confirmed at a record low 44.8 with year-ahead inflation expectations at 4.8 percent and long-run expectations at 3.9 percent, reflecting the consumer-side strain from gasoline prices. The personal savings rate at 2.6 percent, a multi-year low, is the binding signal that the household cushion has thinned at the margin. Fed funds futures price a 98 percent probability of no change at the June 16-17 FOMC, with at most two rate reductions priced for 2026.
