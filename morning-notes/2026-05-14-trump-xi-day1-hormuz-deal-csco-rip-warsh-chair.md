Thursday pre-market, 8:30 AM ET, with US futures bid across the board and the Trump-Xi summit Day 1 readout already setting the global tape. The set-up overnight is the cleanest risk-on rotation we have seen since the May 2 NFP soft-print: S&P 500 e-mini futures at 7,491.50, up 0.29 percent, on pace to open at a fresh record above Wednesday's close of 7,444.25; Nasdaq 100 e-mini at 29,522.75, up 0.15 percent, also pointing to a record open; Dow futures leading at 50,203, up 0.83 percent or 411 points, on the Boeing tape and the Walmart day-of bid. The catalyst stack is dense and asymmetric. President Trump and President Xi closed the bilateral meeting at the Great Hall of the People after two hours and fifteen minutes, with the joint White House readout confirming that the two sides agreed the Strait of Hormuz "must remain open to support the free flow of energy," and that Xi expressed direct interest in buying more American crude and LNG to reduce China's structural reliance on the chokepoint. Trump told Fox News in the post-meeting interview that Xi committed to buy 200 Boeing 737 aircraft, ramp soybean purchases, and lean on Tehran on the Iran agenda. Kevin Warsh was confirmed Wednesday as the eleventh chair of the Federal Reserve by a 54 to 45 vote, the closest Fed chair confirmation in the modern era, with only Pennsylvania Democrat John Fetterman crossing the aisle and three Republicans absent. Warsh is sworn in Friday at Powell's departure, and his first FOMC meeting is June 16 and 17. Cisco printed Wednesday AMC at the strongest beat-and-raise of the cycle so far: revenue of 15.84 billion versus 15.56 billion consensus, EPS of 1.06 versus 1.03 consensus, FY26 AI infrastructure order book lifted to roughly 9 billion from 5 billion (a 4.5x of FY25), Q4 revenue guide of 16.7 to 16.9 billion versus 15.7 billion consensus, FY26 revenue guide of 62.8 to 63.0 billion, and a workforce action of just under 4,000 roles to fund the AI pivot. CSCO is up about 15 percent in after-hours and traded as high as plus 19 percent intraday late session, on pace for its sharpest single-day rally since 2002 if it holds through the cash open. Brent sits at 105.87, off Wednesday's 107 ceiling on the Hormuz language; WTI is at 101.40. The dollar is firm for a third session at DXY 98.49, gold is bid plus 0.44 percent at 4,707 on the geopolitical wraparound and the curve carry, and Bitcoin is holding the 80,000 handle at 80,631. The 10Y is at 4.47 percent, two basis points wider on the day and now ten basis points wider on the week post the CPI and PPI stack, with the 2Y at 4.01 percent.

Networking revenue at 8.82 billion was about 350 million ahead of the 8.47 billion consensus and the strongest single-segment print in five years. Splunk added 500 new logos in H1 FY26 and is tracking 1,000 by year end, which is the cleanest cross-validation of the hyperscaler order conversion against the legacy enterprise base. We had no position into the print but we are leaning into the read-across on the open: we add to the SMH June 19 310 to 330 call spread sized 0.10 percent NAV at the 4.45 mark (now at roughly 4.70 indicated pre-open), taking the position to 0.40 percent NAV, and we open a new ANET May 30 145 to 160 call vertical at 1.85 net debit sized 0.10 percent NAV as the cleanest single-name expression of the hyperscaler routing budget read-through ahead of the May 28 print. 2. **The Trump-Xi summit Day 1 deliverable stack is materially better than our base case at Wednesday close, with the Hormuz "must remain open" language alone worth a structural compression in the energy memo path, and the right structural read is to monetize a portion of the energy long book and roll proceeds into the BA call vertical extension plus a new XLI BMO collar.** Our Wednesday morning base case was an 86 percent probability of the Boeing announcement and a 12 percent probability of explicit Hormuz language from Xi; the morning readout delivered both, with the soybean and LNG commitments as bonus deliverables.
