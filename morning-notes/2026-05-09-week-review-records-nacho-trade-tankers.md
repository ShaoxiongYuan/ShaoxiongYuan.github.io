Saturday wrap, 9:00 AM ET. The week closed at all-time highs on the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite, each notching a sixth straight weekly gain (the longest stretch since 2024) on the back of a +115K April nonfarm payrolls print, accelerating beat-and-raise prints from DDOG, AFRM, ABNB, MELI, and a melt-up in semis where Micron added 13%, Qualcomm 9%, AMD 8%, and the SMH ETF tagged a fresh 52-week high. The bullish tape is paradoxical given what happened simultaneously in the Persian Gulf, where US Navy assets exchanged fire with Iranian forces in the Strait of Hormuz overnight Thursday into Friday, the United Arab Emirates intercepted three drones and two ballistic missiles fired from Iranian territory (with three civilians wounded after partial intercepts), and the US Fifth Fleet disabled two more Iranian-flagged tankers attempting to evade the US blockade, bringing the running tally to 57 vessels redirected and 4 disabled since the blockade began. A new acronym propagated across desks Friday afternoon, "NACHO" for "Not A Chance Hormuz Opens," reflecting a growing consensus that despite the ceasefire holding in name, the kinetic skirmishing pattern is now structural rather than transient. Brent closed the week at $101.29, up 1% on Friday and up roughly 2.5% week over week, while WTI settled at $97.62. Treasury yields drifted lower into the close as the bid for duration on energy passthrough fading offset the hot NFP print, with the 10Y at 4.38% (3 basis points lower on the day) and the 2Y at 3.90%. The University of Michigan May preliminary sentiment reading at 48.2 (a fresh record low, missing 49.5 consensus) was the cleanest counter-narrative on the day, with one third of consumers spontaneously citing gasoline prices and roughly 30% citing tariffs as the source of pessimism, but the equity tape ignored it because the earnings tape was simply too strong to argue with. Heading into next week, the calendar pivots from earnings to macro and politics, with April CPI Tuesday morning, April PPI Wednesday, and the Trump-Xi summit landing Thursday and Friday in Beijing on a Iran-dominated agenda that may push tariff and rare-earth progress to the back of the meeting.

Three things to internalize over the weekend:

1. **The "everything works" tape is structural for now, but the dispersion underneath is high.** The Mag 7 cohort beat the other 493 names on the S&P by more than 40 percentage points on year-on-year earnings growth this quarter, which is the widest spread we have ever recorded and is the cleanest mechanical reason the index can hit fresh highs while sentiment readings collapse. The risk is not the index but the rotation, which is what we sized for in the trade ideas.

2. **NACHO is a market regime, not a slogan.** Trump's "love tap" framing on Friday's exchange got priced as de-escalation by equities and as escalation by the energy curve, and only one of those reads will be right by Wednesday's close. The 14-point memorandum signing window is now tactically pushed, and our base case probability for a signed memo by next Friday's close drops to 38% from 48% Friday morning, which is below the 55% the market is roughly pricing through OPEC's December 2026 contract. Brent August $95/$108 strangle remains the cleanest expression.

3. **CPI Tuesday is the higher-conviction event than the Trump-Xi summit.** Cleveland Nowcast moved to 3.5% headline overnight on the Brent move back through $100, while consensus sits at 3.0% headline / 2.9% core. A 3.1% / 3.0% hot tail (we put it at 35% probability) flips the front-end pricing back to 0.9 cuts for 2026 from the current 1.1, and pulls the 10Y back to 4.45%. The 2Y June 17 4.10% / 4.30% put spread we put on Friday at 12 ticks is the position we want into Tuesday morning.

## The Hormuz / NACHO Mosaic

The equity tape is reading the kinetic events as kabuki (Trump's "love tap" framing, the calibrated intensity of Iranian responses, the maintained ceasefire framework, the diplomatic line through Pakistan), while the energy curve and tanker rates are reading the same events as a permanent regime change in Gulf shipping. NACHO crystallized this divergence on Friday afternoon when several macro desks (including JPM, GS, MS) circulated notes saying their base case for a clean Hormuz reopening had dropped below 30% on a six-month horizon.
