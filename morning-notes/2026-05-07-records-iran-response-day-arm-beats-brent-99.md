## Iran Memo Day, the Hormuz Tape, and Brent Below $100

The Pakistan-mediated channel is now the operative diplomatic line between Tehran and Washington, with the response expected mid-morning today. Trump's pause of Project Freedom escort operations on Wednesday is the second clearest tell yet that both sides expect this to converge, with the first being the September Witkoff-Aragchi handshake at Geneva. The 14-point memorandum, in its current form, would: (a) declare an end to the war in the region, (b) open a 30-day negotiation window for a final agreement, (c) impose an Iranian moratorium on nuclear enrichment, (d) commit the US to lifting sanctions and releasing roughly $6 billion in frozen Iranian funds, and (e) bilaterally lift restrictions on Strait of Hormuz transit. The new five points from yesterday's draft, per Axios, include a structured Geneva diplomatic track starting May 22 and a US commitment to a 30-day non-strike posture absent kinetic provocation.

We are at 62% probability of a signed memo by tomorrow's NY close, up from 55% yesterday. The market is closer to 75%. Three reasons we hold the lower number. First, the Sunday 14-point proposal that became Tuesday's UAE/Fujairah strike shares roughly nine of fourteen points with today's draft, so the kinetic precedent for the Iranian rejection of the framework is fresh. Second, the GCC has not yet been brought in formally, and the UAE airspace closure earlier this week leaves an operationally awkward seam. Third, the IRGC's signaling has been ambiguous on the nuclear moratorium clause, which is the binding lock for both sides.

The base case is the memo is signed in some form, but contingent rather than binding. The market has flipped from pricing-the-tail to pricing-the-base-case in 24 hours, which is what Brent at $99.79 and the WTI/Brent spread compression are telling you. We are using the structural break below $100 to rotate the energy short into a defined-risk put fly, and we are funding a long-duration add via a partial trim of the LMT/NOC pair that we have been short since April 8.

**Three market-moving catalysts we expect today:**
- White House readout on Iranian response, expected 11:00 AM to 1:00 PM ET
- Trump statement on the memo, tone matters more than substance. A "very good talks" framing keeps the bid; a "one week" framing or any deadline language is the rejection signal
- Pakistani Foreign Ministry briefing, expected late-afternoon ET
