Wednesday, September 2, 2026, written at 8:40 a.m. Eastern, after the ADP release and before the cash open, so every US number below is a future or a pre-market print and can look different by ten o'clock. The overnight tape and the overnight fundamentals pointed in opposite directions, which is the whole story.

## Top Call: Demand Is Settled, the Discount Rate Is Not

Dell reported fiscal second quarter revenue of $47.0 billion, up 58% year on year against a $44.9 billion consensus, and adjusted earnings of $7.04 against $4.90 expected. That is a 44% earnings beat. It booked $60.9 billion of AI server orders in a single quarter and exited with a $95.0 billion backlog, up from $51.3 billion three months ago. Management then raised full-year revenue guidance from $167 billion to $192 billion and full-year EPS from $17.90 to $25.50.

Take a step back from the individual lines. A systems integrator with almost no pricing power, sitting at the end of a supply chain where memory costs are rising and Nvidia takes the margin, just added twenty-five billion dollars to its own revenue guidance in one quarter. There is no reading of that print in which end demand for AI infrastructure is decelerating. The argument that has run through these pages since June, that the AI trade was a financing story dressed as a demand story, has now been answered on the demand half, and answered emphatically.

The market's response is what makes today interesting. Dell is up roughly 10% pre-market, which recovers what it lost in Tuesday's regular session and very little more. Nasdaq futures are still slightly negative. The ten-year touched 4.814% overnight, its highest since November 2023. Asia sold every AI-linked name it owns. The tape is not disputing Dell's numbers. It is repricing what those numbers are worth when the risk-free rate keeps climbing and a Fed hike is roughly two thirds priced for two weeks from now.

## Dell: The Print

| Dell FQ2 FY27 | Consensus | Actual | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | $44.9B | $47.0B, up 58% | Beat by $2.1B |
| **Adjusted EPS** | $4.90 | $7.04, up 203% | Beat by 44% |
| **AI server revenue** | not guided | $16.4B | Doubled year on year |
| **AI orders booked** | not guided | $60.9B | Record |
| **AI backlog** | watched | $95.0B | From $51.3B |
| **Operating margin** | the real test | 12.6% | From 7.7% |
| **Free cash flow** | not guided | $986M | Down 47% |
| **FY27 guidance** | about $167B | $192B, EPS $25.50 | Raised $25B |

**Our take**: the margin question that this note flagged as the only line carrying information was answered in the company's favour and then some. Non-GAAP operating margin nearly doubled to 12.6% from 7.7%, gross margin dollars rose 78% to $9.9 billion, and operating expenses fell 250 basis points to 8.5% of revenue. Dell did not merely pass the cost pass-through test, it expanded through it. Citi and Bank of America moved their targets to $600 and the highest target on the LSEG panel is now $735.

The one line that should give anyone pause is free cash flow at $986 million, down 47% year on year on record revenue, as capital expenditure rises to fund capacity. That is not a quality-of-earnings problem in the accounting sense. It is a description of how this cycle actually works: the boxes have to be built and the inventory has to be carried before the backlog converts, and the gap is funded externally. Multiply that across every vendor in the chain and you get the corporate issuance calendar that has been pressing on the long end of the curve all week. Dell's income statement and the thirty-year Treasury are the same story told twice.

## The Tape Has Started Selling Good News

Three prints in twenty-four hours beat and three were sold. Palo Alto Networks delivered fiscal fourth quarter revenue of $3.41 billion against $3.35 billion expected and adjusted EPS of $1.02 against $0.98, with net new platformizations around 220 and up 44%, and the stock fell 5.25% in the session to $362.08 and another 1.78% after hours. MongoDB grew revenue 30.5% to $771.8 million, guided next quarter above consensus at a $758.5 million midpoint and raised its outlook, and the stock is down roughly 12% pre-market. Dell itself dropped about 7% in Tuesday's regular session ahead of its own blowout.

The exception proves the point. GitLab is up 25% on 20% revenue growth and strong bookings, which is a smaller number than any of the three above. What separates it is that nobody had front-run it. The pattern is not that the market dislikes AI results. It is that positioning into AI results has become so crowded that only an unexpected winner gets paid, and the crowding itself is a function of how expensive the alternative has become. When the ten-year was 4.0% you could hold a 40x software name through a print. At 4.81% you take the beat and go.

Tuesday's edition argued that this was a term premium event rather than a Fed event, on the evidence that the two-year barely moved while the long end broke in four countries at once. That distinction is starting to erode. The two-year is six basis points higher this morning at 4.40%, September hike odds have gone from 35% a week ago to somewhere between 66% and 70% depending on the caliber, and the Schwab desk is now openly discussing a 5% ten-year before 2027. What began as a fiscal repricing is pulling the policy rate in behind it.

Governor Ueda supplied the clearest new input, saying that policymakers need to pay greater attention to upside price risks, which is about as close as a Bank of Japan governor gets to pre-announcing a hike at the September 17 and 18 meeting. The yen sits at 159.84. Japan remains the single most underpriced macro variable on the board and this note is not changing that view.

## ADP Says 38,000 and Nobody Cares

Private payrolls rose 38,000 in August against 48,000 expected, with July revised up to 46,000. That is the slowest pace of private job creation since January. In any other year that print buys a dovish reprieve.

It bought nothing this morning, and understanding why matters more than the number itself. At a 4.1% to 4.2% unemployment rate, a soft hiring number is consistent with a labour market that has stopped expanding rather than one that is contracting, and Kevin Warsh has already told the market his reaction function keys off six-month annualised PCE at 4.1% rather than off payroll momentum. A committee that is worried about inflation and comfortable with the level of employment does not respond to a 10,000 miss on ADP. Friday's non-farm payrolls print, expected near 90,000, is a much bigger deal, and even there the asymmetry is unfriendly: a soft number buys far less relief than it used to, while a firm one puts the September hike beyond argument.

Set against that, Tuesday's ISM manufacturing at 54.6 marked an eighth consecutive month of expansion with all five subindexes above 50 and fifteen of eighteen industries growing. It slipped a point from July's 55.6 and the committee chair flagged softer new orders, backlog and imports, so it is a deceleration from a strong level rather than a turn. The growth case for patience is thin.

## The Gulf: A Tanker for a Tanker

The United States struck Iranian oil tankers directly for the first time overnight, in retaliation rather than as blockade enforcement, under what is being described as a tanker-for-tanker policy approved by the President. Central Command separately hit IRGC air defence sites, radar, maritime assets, mine-laying capability and communications nodes. Trump said the strikes answered Iran's attempt to lay further sea mines in the strait and its targeting of a US base in Jordan.

Iran replied within hours. Jordan detected thirteen ballistic missiles and intercepted ten, with three landing in remote areas. Bahrain intercepted drones aimed at a military base and Kuwait was targeted as well. Iran's foreign ministry alleges more than fifty civilians were killed or wounded in a strike on a residential building in Kuhestak, in Hormozgan province. Trump's line was that if Iran retaliates it will be hit much harder, and if it does so again it will "no longer" exist as a functioning state. Qatar has offered to intensify mediation.

Brent rose about 5% on Tuesday to near $95, the highest since late July, and holds between $94.86 and $96.11 depending on which caliber you take this morning, with WTI at $90.34. Five vessels transited the strait on Monday against a ten-day average of fourteen. Separately, a Ukrainian drone attack set fire to Russia's Ust-Luga crude export terminal in the Baltic, which removes barrels from the other end of the map on the same day.

The analytical point is that the escalation has moved from cargo to combatant. Striking tankers is a decision to attack the revenue base rather than the military capability, and it invites a symmetric answer against Gulf export infrastructure that has so far been spared. The oil market is pricing a supply interruption, not a supply loss. That gap is where the risk sits, and it is why an energy shock that would once have been read as a growth scare is now trading squarely as an inflation event.

## Asia Sold What It Owns, and What It Owns Is AI

Korea took the worst of it, with the Kospi down 4.0% to 6,562.72 after two consecutive sessions in which buybacks absorbed macro shocks. The Nikkei fell 2.9% to 64,325.64 for a third straight decline, with Taiyo Yuden down 4.6%, Fujikura 3.9%, Tokyo Electron 3.4% and Advantest 2.5%. Taiwan lost 1.7%, Australia 1.0%, Shanghai 1.0% to 3,941.39 and India 0.6%. Hong Kong was again the most resilient market in the region at 25,311.21 and essentially flat.

SoftBank fell 5.9% to ¥4,953 and deserves its own paragraph. The trigger was the Nasdaq IPO filing of SB Energy, the data centre and power infrastructure venture backed by SoftBank and OpenAI, which disclosed a net loss of $3.2 billion on revenue of $139 million for the first half of 2026 against a $216 million loss a year earlier. The prospectus names dependence on OpenAI as a risk factor. SoftBank also carries a $40 billion bridge loan tied to its OpenAI position.

Hold that next to Dell. On the same night, the company selling AI infrastructure reported a $95 billion backlog and the company financing AI infrastructure disclosed a twenty-three times revenue loss on the power that infrastructure needs. Both are true and they are not in tension. The buildout generates enormous revenue for the vendors and enormous negative carry for whoever funds the assets. The market has spent three months trying to decide which of those two facts is the trade. Today it is pricing both at once, which is why Dell is up 10% and the Kospi is down 4%.

Europe extended the pattern rather than breaking it. The FTSE 100 shed 0.6% to 10,726.68, the DAX 0.6% to 25,798.01 and the CAC 40 0.4% to 8,260.77, with the Stoxx 600 reversing an opening gain to a one-month low. Media fell 1.4% and autos 1.2%. Tuesday's note flagged that European equities were refusing to price their own record bond yields. They have now started to.

Last quarter Broadcom beat on both lines and fell 13% because management declined to raise the fiscal 2027 AI target. That is the entire setup. A beat is assumed and priced. The question is whether Hock Tan moves the ">$100 billion" number, and secondarily whether gross margin holds as custom accelerator volume, which carries lower margin than networking silicon, becomes a larger share of the mix. A beat with an unchanged FY27 target is the bearish outcome and, given how management framed it in June, it is the more likely one. This note would rather be positioned for the margin line than for the headline.

Snowflake also reports tonight against roughly $1.48 billion of revenue and $0.45 to $0.47, with product revenue growth and net revenue retention the numbers that matter, and a fresh $6 billion AWS commitment that adds fixed cost against consumption revenue. Pure Storage follows against about $1.1 billion and $0.58, carrying a 151 times trailing multiple, an 81% year-to-date gain and a second hyperscaler design win that Guggenheim argues the sell side is materially under-modelling.

## Accountability

Tuesday's edition made a specific call on Dell and got it wrong. It argued that the backlog would beat and would not matter, that the Infrastructure Solutions Group margin was the only line carrying information, and that "a beat on revenue with a miss on ISG margin is the bearish outcome and it is also the likely one." Revenue beat by $2.1 billion and margin expanded rather than compressed, with total non-GAAP operating margin going to 12.6% from 7.7%. The reasoning was sound and the conclusion was wrong: memory cost inflation is real, but Dell is passing it through and taking scale benefits on top, which is not what Nvidia's 71% to 72% gross margin guide or Marvell's mix-driven miss implied for the integrator at the end of the chain. Systems integration at this volume turns out to be a better business than either of those datapoints suggested.

The gold call from Tuesday is holding. Bullion fell another 2.37% to $4,375.30 and is now lower on a hawkish Fed chair, a shooting war, a global bond rout and a direct US strike on Iranian tankers, across five consecutive sessions. Nothing about a debasement thesis survives that sequence.
