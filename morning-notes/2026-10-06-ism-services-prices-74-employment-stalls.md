## Top Call: Services Prices at 74.0 With Services Hiring at 50.1

The September ISM services report landed at 10:00 yesterday and the prices paid index printed 74.0, up from 72.6 and the highest reading since July 2022. The employment index printed 50.1, which is the stall line. The headline slipped to 54.9 from 55.4 against a consensus near 55.0.

We committed to both readings of that release in advance, in writing, so that neither could be chosen afterwards. The branch that hit is the one we described as the configuration the committee has no good answer for: prices accelerating in the three quarters of the economy that is services, employment no longer expanding, and the headline still describing growth. Thursday's 77.9 in manufacturing prices paid was not a goods-specific pass through from tariffs and energy. It was the first half of an economy-wide price impulse, and the second half arrived yesterday.

The market gave this a partial hearing. The ten year closed at 5.31%, up three basis points and the highest close in twenty four years, which is the only asset that traded the print honestly. The Nasdaq Composite closed at a record 27,477.31 on the same afternoon. October hike odds sit at 18% to 20%. This morning the long end is being bought back, not because anyone revised their inflation view but because Saudi Arabia cut the price of oil, which is the one input that can relieve a prices paid index without the Federal Reserve doing anything. That is a reprieve rather than a resolution, and it is worth exactly as much as the next tanker.

## Rates: A 24-Year Closing High, Then Oil Took Three Basis Points Back

The ten year closed 5.31% after touching 5.34% intraday, the thirty year closed between 5.66% and 5.70% for a 2002 vintage level, and the two year closed 4.84%. This morning the two year is 4.79%, the ten year 5.28% and the thirty year 5.63%.

The front end has fallen five basis points and the long end three, so two year to ten year is wider near 49 basis points. The shape has not changed in a week. The front end prices a committee that holds in October. The long end prices the inflation that committee is declining to fight, plus the term premium required to hold duration against this deficit and this supply calendar. Yesterday's services print strengthened the case for the second leg and the long end accepted it immediately, which is more than the equity market did.

This is not only an American repricing. Japan's ten year is 3.10% and its thirty year 4.22%, both at or near multi decade highs, and long yields in Korea, France, Germany and the United Kingdom have all been making multi year highs in the same weeks. When every major sovereign long end moves together, the explanation is not any one fiscal authority. It is that the global marginal buyer of duration has repriced the risk of holding a thirty year claim on a government through an energy shock.

Tomorrow at 2:00 p.m. the minutes of the September 15 and 16 meeting arrive, the meeting that lifted the funds rate to 3.75% to 4.00% on a twelve to nothing vote. We said yesterday those minutes are a weak instrument for resolving anything, being three weeks stale against four subsequent data points, and we still think so. Today's $58bn three year auction at 1:00 p.m. is the more informative event, because it is a real bid rather than a recollection. Williams, Bowman, Logan and Cook all speak.

Gold is up about 0.8% between $4,183 and $4,197 with the dollar index back near 101.8 from Monday's 102.22. Front end nominal yields are lower, the dollar is lower and gold is higher, which is the carry framework working with the dollar term we added yesterday after it failed without one. Bitcoin is down 0.4% near $86,072 and has gone nowhere for four sessions, so the two legs have separated and we mark the bitcoin leg as failing while the gold leg passes.

## Energy: Saudi Arabia Is Paying Asia to Take Crude It Cannot Ship Cheaply

Aramco set the November official selling price for Arab Light into Asia at $5.00 below the Oman and Dubai average, a cut of $3 from October and the widest discount since June 2020. Arab Medium and Arab Heavy were cut $5. A Reuters survey of refiners had expected an increase of as much as $5 in line with the strength in the Middle Eastern benchmarks. At the same time Aramco raised prices into northwest Europe and the Mediterranean.

Read those two decisions together, because separately each is misleading. This is not a demand signal. A producer cutting Asia and raising Europe in the same announcement is not describing weak consumption, it is describing a freight and insurance market that has made the delivered cost of a Gulf barrel in Asia uneconomic at the old differential. Aramco is absorbing the war risk premium on its own margin to defend market share. The barrel is not cheap. Moving it is expensive, and the seller has decided to pay that bill rather than lose the customer.

Brent is $98.50, having traded under $98 overnight, from over $101 on Monday morning. WTI is near $87.50. The other two supports for the move are the G7 agreement to release up to 100m barrels over four months and the continued recovery in Gulf throughput, with Middle East crude exports above pre-war levels on fourteen separate days in September and a seven day average of 18.3m barrels a day at month end, and flows excluding Iran back above 81% of pre-war levels.

None of that reaches the product market, which is the entire argument we have been making for seven weeks. The diesel crack has been above $100 since mid August and set a record $107.35 on September 1. American distillate inventories are 14% below the five year seasonal average and at the lowest seasonal level on record. The G7 release is front-loaded with diesel precisely because crude was never the binding constraint. When a producer discounts crude by the most in six years and the refining margin on turning it into diesel stays at a record, the shortage has a location and it is not the wellhead.

The security picture has not improved to match the price. Maritime trackers logged at least seven tanker incidents in the past week, including a Panama flagged vessel hit outbound on October 2, a crude tanker struck off Oman, a six week old Liberia flagged product carrier disabled on October 4, and explosions reported sixty nautical miles south of Al Mukha in the approaches to Bab el-Mandeb. Tehran maintains that Hormuz stays closed until seven conditions are met, Araghchi says there is no military solution and Pezeshkian calls talks meaningless. The flows are recovering because operators are running dark at night and accepting roughly a one in twenty chance of being hit, with war risk premiums at multiples of normal. That is a logistics achievement, not a diplomatic one, and it can be undone in an afternoon.

## Equities: A Record Composite, a Dow That Barely Moved, and Nvidia $300bn From Six Trillion

Monday closed with the Nasdaq Composite at a record 27,477.31 up 1.05%, past its September 22 high, the S&P at 7,773.95 up 0.66%, the Russell at 2,847.14 up 0.50% and the Dow at 51,267.90 up only 0.18%. SpaceX rose almost 8%, Nvidia and Tesla about 2% each, Meta close to 2% and Microsoft more than 1%.

The spread between a 1.05% Composite and a 0.18% Dow on a day the long end closed at a twenty four year high is the whole market structure in one line. Rising real discount rates are supposed to hurt long duration cash flows most. Instead the index made of the longest duration cash flows in the world rose five times as much as the index made of industrials, banks and consumer staples. That is not a discount rate market. It is a capacity market, where the scarce thing is accelerators, memory, optics and electricity, and where the companies that own them are being valued on physical allocation rather than on the cost of capital. The qualification we added yesterday still stands: breadth is weak in the middle of the large cap index rather than across the whole market, and the Russell at 0.50% beating the Dow at 0.18% is the evidence for that narrower claim.

Nvidia printed a record and carries a market value near $5.74 trillion, which means roughly $248 a share buys the first $6 trillion market capitalisation in history. That is about 4% away. We have no interest in the round number itself. What matters is that the move since the late July low is now a 27% year to date gain built on a revenue guide and the largest buyback authorisation ever announced, and a company whose board would rather repurchase stock than deploy the cash into supply is still telling you the bottleneck sits downstream of it.

Futures are higher across the board this morning, S&P contracts at 7,846 to 7,861, Dow contracts near 51,800, Nasdaq contracts near 31,470 and the Russell at 2,881.80. The VIX is 15.30, down 1.4%.

## Asia and Europe: Tokyo Closes Above 70,000, Seoul Takes Profits, Paris Gets a Reprieve

The Nikkei closed at 70,683.98, up 1.05% and 737 points, its first close above 70,000 after touching 70,037.61 intraday on Monday. The yen is 158.20. A thirty year Japanese government bond at 4.22% and an equity index at an all time high in the same market is a coherent trade only if you believe the currency keeps falling, because that is the single variable flattering the earnings translation and the one the Bank of Japan will eventually have to defend. Tokyo is long a weak yen and short nothing, and the import bill is accruing in the background.

Seoul reopened and gave back Friday's gain, the Kospi closing 6,941.39, down 0.89%, on profit taking and global bond yields. Hong Kong added about 1.0% to roughly 24,280 for a second session of gains. The mainland returns on October 8 having missed the payroll report, both ISM surveys and the entire repricing of the October meeting.

Europe is the session's most interesting reversal. The DAX is up about 0.6% near 25,414 and the FTSE up a similar amount near 10,555. The euro has rebounded from Monday's 17-month low of $1.1161 to about $1.1244 to $1.1270, the best performer in the majors, and the French ten year has eased to roughly 4.75% with the OAT to Bund spread back near 130 basis points from about 150 last week and almost 159 intraday on Friday. The stated reason is that fiscal consolidation proposals from both the government and Le Pen have gained traction.

We flagged that spread yesterday as sovereign risk returning to Europe as an equity input, and it has narrowed twenty nine basis points in two sessions. The structural case does not change in two sessions: French debt is still headed toward 121.7% of GDP in 2027, Lecornu is still moving 54 billion euros of savings through a parliament that has removed a prime minister over a budget, the deficit still only falls from 5.4% to about 5% next year, and Spain still votes on November 29. But we published the observation on the morning of the extreme, which is the least useful moment to publish it, and a reader who acted on the framing has lost money in forty eight hours. The level was the signal and we treated it as a trend.

## Corporate: The Power Leg Keeps Clearing, and It Is Nuclear

**Alphabet** is near a twenty year agreement with **Constellation Energy** worth $1bn or more to procure about 890 megawatts of nuclear output. Constellation rose about 4%. This follows a twenty year nuclear purchase agreement Constellation signed with **Amazon**, Google's earlier work with NextEra to restart Duane Arnold in Iowa and its agreement with Southern Company on uprates, and it comes one trading day after the federal government put $4.2bn of loan money behind **Vistra** uprates at Perry, Davis-Besse and Beaver Valley.

Four hyperscaler nuclear contracts and one federal loan inside a fortnight is a pattern rather than a sequence of announcements. The useful observation is about contract structure rather than megawatts. These are twenty year commitments on existing licensed capacity, which means the buyers are not funding new build, they are buying the output of plants that already exist and bidding it away from everyone else on the grid. Firm baseload is being removed from the merchant pool by the only customers who can sign a two decade take or pay. That is excellent for the owners of existing nuclear and it is a rising cost curve for every industrial and residential ratepayer behind the same interconnection. The electricity leg of this buildout is no longer a bottleneck story. It is becoming a distributional one, and that is a political input before it is a market input.

**Applied Digital** secured access to a potential one gigawatt of power in Finland, which is the same trade executed by a company with a fraction of the balance sheet and in a jurisdiction where the power is stranded rather than contested. **DeepSeek** is raising more than 80 billion yuan, about $11.9bn, ahead of a domestic listing, which puts a Chinese frontier lab on a comparable funding footing to the American ones in the same month Washington moved against Chinese optical transceivers.

In memory, **Micron** chief executive Mehrotra said supply and demand could tighten further in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026, which is a company with 75% of next year's volume already contracted telling you the shortage extends past the horizon it has sold. **SK Hynix** has completed HBM4 development and finished mass production preparation using a TSMC 12nm logic base die, and both it and **Samsung** have delivered paid final HBM4 samples to Nvidia. **AMD** chief executive Lisa Su said demand for its chips will remain exceptionally strong, has asked rivals to share Taiwanese capacity, and travels to Korea around October 8 to deepen HBM4 ties with Samsung. A designer publicly asking competitors for foundry allocation is the clearest statement available that the constraint is packaging and memory rather than demand.

**SpaceX** rose almost 8% on Monday to its highest since June on a Morgan Stanley overweight with a $300 target against a price near $159, and on the prospect of exclusivity with **TSMC** for the Terafab venture. That second leg is the same story that pressured **Intel** on Friday, read from the other side. Intel needs an anchor customer for its foundry and the most visible new anchor in American silicon is choosing Taiwan. One company's optionality is the other's missing order book.

Elsewhere, **McKesson** and Clayton Dubilier & Rice are near a $5bn-plus acquisition of **Option Care Health**, **Becton Dickinson** plans a $19bn American investment including $3bn of domestic manufacturing, **Vaxcyte** launched $500m of stock and $500m of convertible notes three sessions after its VAX-31 readout, **Berkshire Hathaway** took its **Lennar** stake to 12% at about $2.2bn, **Ariel Investments** is pressing **Mattel** on strategic alternatives with a 5.4% holding, and Wall Street profits rose 51% year on year to $49.5bn in the first half, already $4.2bn above New York City's full year projection.

## Earnings: Nothing in the Complex Reports Today

**Lamb Weston** is the only print of substance before the bell and it is not an artificial intelligence name, which is the point of including it. Fiscal first quarter net sales rose 1% to $1.67bn against a $1.65bn estimate, adjusted earnings were $0.75 against a $0.59 consensus, adjusted EBITDA was $286m, and the full year adjusted EBITDA range was updated to $1.125bn to $1.215bn.

**Our take**: a frozen potato processor beating by sixteen cents on cost savings with 1% sales growth is the mirror image of yesterday's ISM. The prices paid index says input costs are accelerating at the fastest pace since July 2022 and this company met the number by cutting its own costs rather than by raising prices, with reported earnings at $0.21 against $0.75 adjusted. That is what pricing power looks like when you do not have any. It does not move the index and it does not change our view, but it is a cleaner read on the margin squeeze than anything the AI complex will tell us, because the AI complex is not paying for inputs out of a fixed contract. **Apogee Enterprises** also beat, with second quarter sales of $391.1m against $362.6m and full year adjusted earnings raised to $3.00 to $3.40. **Constellation Brands** reports after the close against $3.56 on $2.55bn.

## Accountability

**The ISM services test resolved on the first branch, exactly as specified.** We wrote both outcomes down on Monday morning before the 10:00 release so neither could be selected afterwards. Prices paid rose to 74.0, the headline held near 55 at 54.9, and employment printed 50.1. By our own stated terms that makes the October pause a policy error in progress rather than a reasonable call, and it closes the question we opened on Thursday: 77.9 in manufacturing was not goods-specific. We will not overclaim this. We did not predict which branch would hit, we committed to what each would mean, and the value was in removing our own discretion after the fact rather than in any forecast.

**We ranked the wrong event on Monday and should say so plainly.** We wrote that the euro print was a more important market event than anything on the domestic calendar before 10:00. The ISM services release at 10:00 took the ten year to its highest close in twenty four years, and the euro move we were pointing at has since round-tripped from $1.1161 to about $1.1250 while the OAT to Bund spread narrowed from almost 159 basis points to about 130. We were right that the French spread is a real credit input and wrong about its timing and its relative importance on the day, and we published the observation at the extreme. That is the more expensive of the two errors.

**The carry framework passes on gold and fails on bitcoin.** We added a dollar term yesterday after the framework failed without one. This morning front end yields are lower, the dollar is lower and gold is up 0.8%, which is the amended version working on its first test. Bitcoin is down 0.4% and has been flat for four sessions through a softer dollar and a lower front end, so we are splitting the framework: gold responds to real yields and the dollar, bitcoin currently responds to neither, and we will stop quoting them as one trade.

**The distillate thesis takes its cleanest confirmation yet and it arrived as a price list.** Saudi Arabia has cut the Asian crude differential by the most in six years while the diesel crack holds above $100 and distillate inventories sit at a record seasonal low. A producer discounting the input while the margin on the output stays at a record is the separation of crude from product stated by the only party with perfect information about both. Seven weeks on the structure, and for three of those weeks we named the wrong trigger.

**The crude discipline rule pays again, this time inside twenty four hours.** On Monday morning Brent was back above $101 on a Yemeni ground offensive. This morning it is under $98 on an official selling price announcement nobody in the survey expected. Carrying no directional view on the barrel while trading the product crack has now been paid through three full round trips, and the lesson is holding: in this market the barrel is a geopolitical instrument and the crack is an industrial fact.
