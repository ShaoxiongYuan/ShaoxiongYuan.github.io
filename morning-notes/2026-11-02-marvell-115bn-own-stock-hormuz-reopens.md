Monday, November 2, 2026, written at 8:40 a.m. Eastern. Clocks went back yesterday, so London is five hours ahead from this week. Futures are higher across the board after the worst week for technology since the spring, and the reason they are higher has almost nothing to do with technology. Crude is through $100 on a diplomatic headline, which is the only thing that was going to rescue this tape from either direction. Apple and Palantir report after the close, the midterms are tomorrow, and the window in which renewed American action against Iran becomes politically possible opens on Wednesday.

## Top Call: The Biggest Tech Merger Ever Was Paid For in Shares, Not Cash

Marvell agreed this morning to acquire Lumentum for about $115 billion including debt, roughly $102.5 billion of equity value, entirely in stock. Lumentum holders receive 4.500 Marvell shares each, which values the company near $1,132 a share and is a 23.9% premium to the October 28 close before the talks leaked. Marvell holders keep about 67% of the combined company and Lumentum holders take 33%. Management points to a combined addressable market of roughly $550 billion and more than $1 billion of annual run rate cost synergies, with closing guided to the first half of 2028.

Two things in that paragraph matter more than the headline number. The first is the currency. We wrote last Wednesday that Marvell had $3.92 billion of cash against what was then reported as an $83 billion target, and the obvious question was how it intended to pay. The answer is that it is not paying, it is issuing. A third of the company changed hands without a dollar of new debt in a week when Blackstone reclassified Anthropic as a double B credit and OpenAI marked its own round down by a third. That is not a coincidence and it is not a stylistic preference. When the debt market stops funding the build, the acquisition currency becomes your own paper, and the deals that get done are the ones where both sides are willing to hold it.

The second is the price, which went up. The approach was reported at $83 billion on October 28. It signed at $115 billion, and it signed during the single worst week for optical components in a year, with Applied Optoelectronics down about 15%, Coherent down 11.3% and Lumentum itself down roughly 10% on Friday alone. A bidder who raises the price by nearly 40% into a collapsing sector tape is telling you something about scarcity. What Marvell is buying is the laser, which is the one component in the optical chain that co-packaged optics cannot design out, and Nvidia already owns a $2 billion stake in each company. Marvell has now bought Celestial AI for about $3.25 billion and Lumentum for $115 billion inside a year, and it has stopped being a chip designer.

The risk sits in the ownership split rather than in the strategy. Issuing 33% of yourself to buy an asset that has already tripled is only accretive if the combined entity holds its multiple, and the reason both stocks are slightly lower pre-market is that the arbitrage spread is doing its job. There is also a closing date eighteen months out, which in a cycle moving this fast is most of a lifetime.

## Hormuz: A Two Day Plan, and the Oil Premium Finally Has Two Sides

Iranian negotiators spent a third consecutive day in Doha with Qatari and Pakistani mediators, and Iran's foreign minister said Qatar helped compress Tehran's original seven day proposal into two: American measures on day one, the Strait of Hormuz reopened on day two. Brent fell 4.4% to $108.37 and WTI dropped more than 4% to $99.65, below $100 for the first time in about a month.

This is the headline the energy complex has been refusing to price since September, and the reason it is being priced today is the structure of the proposal rather than its existence. A seven day plan is a negotiating position. A 48 hour plan with a sequenced first step is an operational document, and traders can model it. Goldman has been carrying roughly $27 a barrel of geopolitical premium over a fair value near $85, which means a genuine reopening is worth something like twenty dollars of Brent rather than the two or three dollars that previous headlines bought.

Read the caveats properly, because they are substantial. The plan still requires Washington to accept a list of Iranian demands, reportedly including sanctions relief and restored oil waivers, which it has not answered. Secretary Rubio said over the weekend that the President has made no decision on renewed strikes and would prefer diplomacy. The President said separately that he is not in a hurry, that the Iranian navy has already been destroyed and that one or two targets remain. There is reporting that Prime Minister Netanyahu is pressing for strikes immediately after tomorrow's vote. Iran's Revolutionary Guard continues to claim it has hit tankers transiting without authorisation.

What has changed is not the probability of a deal, it is the shape of the distribution. For most of October the only question in crude was how much higher, because every piece of news was either neutral or escalatory. There is now a credible mechanism for a twenty dollar decline sitting alongside a credible mechanism for a twenty dollar rally, with both resolving inside roughly seventy two hours. That is the first symmetric oil market since the summer, and it argues for owning optionality rather than direction.

The equity read-through runs through inflation. We argued on Friday that a front end rallying on growth worry while the energy input ran was the configuration that left equities without rescue from either side. Crude below $100 breaks that. Lower headline inflation takes pressure off a December hike that futures still put somewhere between 70% and 78%, and a December that goes away is worth considerably more to the Nasdaq than any single earnings print this week. Nasdaq 100 futures are up around 0.6% to 0.9% while the S&P is up 0.33%. The bounce in technology this morning is an oil trade wearing a technology costume.

## Kioxia Found the Second Derivative, and the Market Refused to Generalise It

|--------|-----------|--------|--------|

| Oct-Dec op profit guide | ~¥2,350bn | ~¥2,000bn | 15% miss |
| Oct-Dec revenue guide | n/a | ~¥2,500bn, up ~1% q/q | Flat |

Kioxia posted a record quarter and beat its own guidance on both lines, then guided the December quarter to roughly 1% sequential growth in both revenue and adjusted operating profit, putting profit about 15% below the ¥2.35 trillion consensus. The stock fell as much as 17% intraday. The explanation given was delayed enterprise storage deliveries and a deceleration in NAND price increases from something like 70% sequentially to around 25%.

That is the clearest statement anyone has made this cycle that memory pricing has an upper bound, and it is exactly what the market suspected on Friday when SanDisk beat, raised, split eight for one and fell 13.5%. We wrote then that scarcity rent is good for whoever collects it only until the market starts discounting the far side of the cycle. Kioxia is that discount arriving with a number attached.

Now look at what happened next, because this is the part we got wrong on Friday. Micron rose 2.7% pre-market, SanDisk gained more than 3%, SK hynix closed up around 4% to 5% in Seoul and Samsung added 1.8%, lifting the Kospi 2.4% to 5,859.08. The sector did not follow Kioxia down. Our Friday framing treated the far side of the cycle as a single event that would be applied to memory as a class, and the market has instead separated it by product. NAND is normalising from an extraordinary base, and a 25% sequential price increase is still a very good quarter. High bandwidth memory and DRAM are not normalising, which is why SK hynix, sold out to 2029 on an 80% operating margin, trades differently from a NAND pure play with delivery problems. Kioxia itself still says its 2027 capacity is sold out and that long term agreements cover more than half of expected 2028 shipments.

The honest version is that we were right that the discounting had begun and wrong about how wide it would be. The correct frame going into Micron's next print is that the bull case has narrowed from memory to high bandwidth memory, which is a smaller and better business than the one the market was buying in September.

## China Fired the Emergency Flare, and Left Out the Only Thing That Works

The weekend data was poor. Official manufacturing PMI fell to 49.3 from 50.1 against a 49.9 consensus, with production down 1.9 points to 49.8 and new orders down 1.1 points to 49.4. Non-manufacturing slipped to 49.7 from 50.2 and the composite to 49.7 from 50.7. The statistics bureau pointed at Golden Week disruption. The private RatingDog gauge held at 52.0 and has now spent eleven months above fifty, and that gap between the official and private surveys is itself informative: state weighted heavy industry is contracting while private exporters are not.

The policy response arrived this morning. The central bank, the financial regulator and the securities commission met the major banks and agreed to expand the use of an 800 billion yuan market support fund and to ease relending conditions for share buybacks. The securities commission will step up suspensions of securities lending, pursue unofficial short selling and tighten rules on short term trading. Separately the central bank began daily overnight reverse repurchase operations at a fixed 1.25%, below the 1.4% seven day rate, as a step toward making the overnight rate its primary policy anchor. The CSI 300 rose 0.79% to 4,016.92 and the Shanghai Composite closed near 3,638, up about 1%.

Rank those in order of importance and the ranking is the opposite of the headlines. The stabilisation fund and the short selling clamp are the 2015 playbook, and the 2015 playbook buys days. It worked today because the CSI 300 fell 8.5% in October for its worst month since that rout and had printed a record twelfth consecutive weekly decline, so positioning was short enough that an announcement was sufficient. The repo change is the one that matters, because moving the policy anchor to the overnight rate is a real improvement in how monetary policy transmits and it is the first thing Beijing has done this year that a foreign fixed income investor should care about.

What is absent is fiscal. The National People's Congress Standing Committee convened today through Friday with no fiscal items on the published agenda. A manufacturing survey at 49.3 with both production and new orders contracting is a demand problem, and restricting short selling does not create demand. We would treat this bounce as tradeable and not investable until something appears on that agenda.

## Rates, the Dollar, and a Gold Test That Resolved Well

The ten year is at 4.91%, down about two basis points and near a seven week low, which is a long way from October's 5.36% peak, the highest since 2002. The two year sits at 4.588%, down a basis point. The thirty year is around 5.31%. December hike pricing ranges from roughly 70% on prediction markets to 78% on futures depending on the tracker and the hour, after the committee held at 3.75% to 4.00% on October 28 with two dissents in favour of an immediate increase. The dollar index is a touch softer near 101.60 with the euro up 0.5% at 1.1217 and the yen weaker at 157.87.

Friday's note set a specific test. We wrote that gold falling 3.5% to a one year low on a day when crude rose, yields fell and the dollar was flat could only be explained by forced selling to meet margin calls elsewhere, and that if gold kept falling while crude held above $100 the margin story was still running. Gold is up 0.6% this morning at about $3,900, having bounced off roughly $3,875, and crude has broken below $100. Both halves of that test resolved in the benign direction, which says the liquidation phase of last week's unwind has stopped, at least for a session. Bitcoin stabilising near $78,100 after the $6.3 billion Binance breach points the same way.

Today's data is the ISM manufacturing survey at 10:00 a.m., consensus 54.7 against 54.5, with the prices paid component expected at 77.5 after 77.9 and employment at 52.0 after 52.7. Prices paid near 78 is the number to watch rather than the headline, because it is the input that argues against the oil relief above. Construction spending is expected at 0.5% monthly after 0.9%. The week then builds to the Treasury refunding Wednesday and October payrolls Friday at a consensus near 55,000.

## Tonight: Apple Is the Hedge Being Tested on Its Own Terms

Apple reports fiscal fourth quarter results after the close, with consensus near $113.4 billion of revenue and $1.99 of earnings, up roughly 10.7% and 7.6% respectively, on about $56.4 billion of iPhone revenue and $31.7 billion of services. It is the first call under chief executive John Ternus. The company guided to 9% to 11% revenue growth and a gross margin of 47% to 48%, including about one percentage point of benefit from tariff refunds.

Apple has functioned all year as the hedge against the artificial intelligence trade, rising on most sessions when semiconductors were sold hard, and it reclaimed the largest company title on Friday at about $5.03 trillion while Nvidia fell 4.2%. The reason it works as a hedge is precisely that it is the only member of the group that has not committed to a hundred billion dollar annual build. The problem tonight is that the absence of a capital spending story leaves the gross margin line carrying the entire print, and a guide of 47% to 48% against the 50.1% delivered in the June quarter is a meaningful step down with a one-off tariff benefit inside it. Bernstein has iPhone shipment growth slowing to 2% in the September quarter. Consensus price targets imply roughly 3% downside from here after a 28.7% year. This is the one night where Apple gets marked on its own business rather than on the sector it hedges.

Palantir follows, at about $2.17 billion of revenue and $0.39 to $0.41 of earnings against a full year guide of $8.150 billion to $8.158 billion and a price to sales multiple above 100. At that valuation the print is not the event, the raise is. ON Semiconductor also reports, at roughly $1.70 billion and $0.88, and is worth watching as the cleanest read available this week on the automotive and industrial semiconductor cycle that has nothing to do with data centres.

## The Record

Two pre-committed tests resolved this morning and both went the right way. We said on Friday that if gold kept falling while crude held above $100, last week's margin liquidation was still running. Gold is up and crude is below $100, so that phase has stopped. We also said the Lumentum approach might land this week, and it landed at $115 billion rather than the reported $83 billion.

The thing we got wrong was the breadth of the memory call. On Friday we took SanDisk falling 13.5% on a beat and raise as evidence that the market had begun discounting the far side of the cycle for the memory complex as a whole. Kioxia tested that this morning with a guide 15% below consensus, and the complex went up anyway. The discount is being applied to NAND and not to high bandwidth memory, and we should have made that distinction on Friday rather than treating storage and memory as one trade.

The test we carry into tomorrow: if this morning's bid in technology is genuinely an oil trade rather than a technology trade, then a Hormuz deal that fails over the next seventy two hours should take the Nasdaq back down with crude, and Apple's print tonight should matter less to the index than the headline out of Doha. If the Nasdaq holds a crude rally back above $105, the recovery is broader than we think and the funding argument of the past three weeks has further to unwind than we have allowed.
