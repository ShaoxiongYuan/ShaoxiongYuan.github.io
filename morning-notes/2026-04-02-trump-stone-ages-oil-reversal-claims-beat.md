## The Speech — "Operation Epic Fury Is Nearing Completion." Then He Threatened to Destroy Everything.

Trump's roughly 20-minute primetime speech at 9 PM ET Wednesday contained two completely contradictory messages, and the market spent all of Thursday trying to figure out which one to believe.

**The victory narrative:**
- "Iran's navy is GONE. Their air force is in ruins."
- "We have complete regime change now," referring to the assassination of Khamenei and senior leadership.
- The war is "nearing completion." US forces could leave in "two or three weeks."
- The military is "meeting or exceeding all operational benchmarks": ballistic missiles destroyed, nuclear program set back, IRGC degraded.
- Formally named the operation: **Operation Epic Fury**.

**The escalation threat:**
- "We are going to hit them extremely hard over the next two to three weeks."
- If no deal: "We are going to hit each and every one of their electric-generating plants very hard and probably simultaneously."
- The US will send Iran "back to the stone ages, where they belong."
- Iran's oil sites are targets if leaders don't make a deal.
- A third aircraft carrier is being deployed to the region.

**On allies and Hormuz:**
- "The United States imports almost no oil through the Hormuz Strait and won't be taking any in the future. We don't need it."
- Told oil-dependent allies to "take the lead" in reopening Hormuz, an explicit shift of responsibility away from the US.
- Will discuss "my disgust with NATO," framing allied inaction as justification for potential US withdrawal.

**On gas prices:**
- Acknowledged $4/gallon gas but blamed Iran, saying the increase was "entirely due to Iran launching attacks on oil tankers."
- Offered no plan to bring prices down beyond ending the war.

**What it means:** The speech contained a fundamental contradiction that the market spent Thursday processing: the war is "nearing completion" but the US will escalate "extremely hard" for two to three more weeks. These aren't the same thing. "Nearing completion" implies winding down. "Extremely hard" and "back to the stone ages" implies maximum escalation before leaving. The market's initial read was pure bear: futures dropped 1%+, Asia collapsed, oil surged 11%. But by the close, US equities had recovered almost everything, finishing essentially flat. The intraday reversal suggests the market ultimately decided to believe the exit timeline over the escalatory rhetoric. The logic: Trump is telling Iran he'll destroy everything if they don't deal, but he's also telling the American public the war ends soon. The escalation is the leverage. The exit is the policy.

The problem: the Strait of Hormuz. Trump explicitly said the US doesn't need Hormuz oil and isn't going to reopen it. He told allies to do it themselves. No ally has the naval capacity to force open Hormuz against the IRGC without US support. If the US leaves in three weeks and Hormuz stays closed, oil doesn't go to $85; it stays above $100 indefinitely. The speech offered no plan for Hormuz, and that's why oil surged.

## The War — Day 34: Iran Vows "More Crushing" Attacks. Gold Crashes. The World Reacts.

Iran's combined military command dismissed Trump's assessment of its remaining capabilities as "incomplete":

- Vowed to carry out "more crushing, broader, and more destructive" attacks against US and Israeli forces.
- The IRGC stated the Strait of Hormuz remains "fully" under its control and will not be reopened.
- Iran's Foreign Ministry reiterated that Trump's ceasefire claim was "false and baseless."
- Pezeshkian, in a letter published in US media, urged Americans to "question your government's war motives," continuing the strategy of appealing directly to US public opinion.

**What it means:** Iran is calling Trump's bluff. The military vowed escalation, the IRGC said Hormuz stays closed, and the diplomatic track is neither advancing nor collapsing. Iran appears to believe it can outlast the two-to-three-week timeline: let the US exhaust itself, then negotiate from a position of having survived. This is a dangerous gamble if Trump follows through on the power grid threat.

The threat remains live and creates a background risk premium on US mega-cap tech with Middle East operations.

## Jobless Claims — 202K: The Labor Market Refuses to Break

Initial claims fell to 202,000 (vs. 212K consensus), down 9,000 from the prior week. Near a two-year low:

- **Four-week average**: 207,750, down from 210,750. Trend is firmly lower.
- **Insured unemployment rate**: 1.2%, unchanged. No deterioration.
- **Continuing claims**: Stable. Layoffs aren't accelerating.

**What it means:** The labor market is the single strongest pillar of the US economy right now. ISM at 52.7 + claims at 202K = the economy is not in recession. The war is hurting consumer sentiment and raising energy costs, but it hasn't triggered mass layoffs. This is why the intraday equity reversal happened: the hard data says the economy can absorb two to three more weeks of war. The risk is duration. If the war extends beyond Trump's timeline, the consumer breaks.

## Warsh Watch — The Hawkish Shadow Over Everything

Kevin Warsh's confirmation hearings for Fed Chair begin mid-April, with Powell's term ending May 15. The "Warsh shock" is already priced into gold and yields:

- Warsh is known as an inflation hawk focused on financial stability over employment.
- Markets are pricing a single rate cut for 2026 (down from three cuts expected in January).
- If Warsh signals a willingness to hike rates to combat war-driven inflation, the 10Y breaks 4.50% and gold collapses further.
- The gold-to-oil ratio is at multi-year lows. Gold is losing the inflation hedge trade to oil and the dollar.

**What it means for positioning:** The Warsh transition creates a "hawkish floor" under yields. Even if the war ends and oil falls, the new Fed chair may not cut rates. This limits the upside for duration-sensitive assets (tech, growth, housing) even in a peace scenario. The post-war macro environment may be tighter than the market expects.
