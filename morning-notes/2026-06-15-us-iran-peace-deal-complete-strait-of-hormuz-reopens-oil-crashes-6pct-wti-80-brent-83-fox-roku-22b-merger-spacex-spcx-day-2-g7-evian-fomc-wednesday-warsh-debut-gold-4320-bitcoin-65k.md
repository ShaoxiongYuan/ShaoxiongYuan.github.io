Monday pre-open, 6:30 AM ET. Futures are surging across the board to start a holiday-shortened week after President Trump declared the US-Iran peace deal "now complete" late Sunday, with a formal signing ceremony set for Friday in Switzerland. S&P 500 futures are up 1.2%, Nasdaq 100 futures lead at +2.1%, and Dow futures add 1.0%. Oil is cratering on the news: WTI plunged 5.5% to $80.20 and Brent fell 5.2% to $82.80, both breaking below levels last seen in March as markets reprice the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The VIX collapsed 9.1% to 16.08, its lowest level since the war began in late February. In corporate news, Fox Corporation announced it will acquire Roku in a $22 billion deal that reshapes the streaming landscape. SpaceX is indicated up roughly 5% pre-market on its second day of trading. Asia posted its strongest session of the year, led by Japan's Nikkei surging 5%. The G7 summit opens today in Evian, France, and the FOMC begins its two-day meeting tomorrow with Kevin Warsh's debut as Chair and fresh dot plot projections. Markets are closed Friday for Juneteenth.

## Iran Peace Deal: From Framework to Finished

Trump posted on Truth Social late Sunday that the agreement with Iran was "now complete." The deal includes an immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping, a 60-day ceasefire formalized into a permanent cessation of hostilities, the lifting of oil sanctions, and up to $300 billion in reconstruction commitments. An official signing ceremony is scheduled for Friday, June 19, in Switzerland, with both sides expected to sign electronically before then. Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi confirmed the Memorandum of Understanding was finalized but noted that nuclear enrichment issues have been carved out for separate follow-on negotiations.

This matters enormously for markets. The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20% of the world's oil shipments. Its reopening would return over 12 million barrels per day of transit capacity and fundamentally change the energy cost picture that has been driving inflation higher since March. WTI's move below $80 for the first time since the war started is the market saying it believes this deal is real. If crude stays in the $75-$80 range through the summer, the energy-driven inflation shock that pushed CPI to 4.2% and PPI to 6.5% starts to unwind meaningfully, potentially giving the Fed breathing room. But the "nuclear carve-out" means this is a partial peace, not a comprehensive resolution. Iran retains leverage, and hardliners in Tehran have stalled previous agreements. The signing ceremony needs to happen before markets fully relax.

## Fox-Roku: $22 Billion Streaming Mega-Merger

Fox Corporation announced Monday it will acquire Roku in an all-cash-and-stock deal valued at approximately $22 billion. Under the terms, Fox will pay $96 in cash and 0.9693 shares of Fox Class A common stock for each Roku share, valuing the transaction at roughly $160 per share. Existing Fox shareholders will own approximately 73% of the combined company, with Roku shareholders holding the remaining 27%.

The strategic logic is clear: combining Fox's content portfolio, including sports, news, entertainment, and the Tubi streaming service, with Roku's connected TV platform and its direct relationship with over 100 million global streaming households. The combined entity gains first-party data, advertising scale, and distribution that neither company could build alone. Fox projects approximately $400 million in run-rate cost synergies with additional revenue upside, and expects the deal to be accretive to free cash flow per share by the second full year post-close.

Both boards approved unanimously, but regulatory clearance and shareholder votes remain. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027. Roku shares jumped 28% in pre-market while Fox rose 3.2%. This deal signals that the media industry's consolidation cycle is far from over.

## SpaceX Day 2: Momentum Carries Forward

SpaceX shares are indicated up approximately 5% pre-market at roughly $169, extending Friday's historic debut where SPCX closed at $161.11, up 19.3% from the $135 IPO price. The $75 billion raise at a $1.77 trillion valuation was the largest IPO in stock market history, making Elon Musk the world's first trillionaire. Trading volume on Friday exceeded 360 million shares.

The continued strength is notable. Early lock-up dynamics typically create selling pressure on day two, but SpaceX's unique position as the sole publicly traded pure-play space and satellite infrastructure company creates structural demand. Index inclusion mechanics are the next catalyst: SPCX will qualify for the Nasdaq-100 before eventually entering the S&P 500, triggering mandatory passive buying. The question is whether the stock can hold above $160 through the first week, or whether profit-taking by IPO allocatees pulls it back toward $145-$150.

## FOMC Preview: Warsh Takes the Stage

The Federal Open Market Committee meets Tuesday-Wednesday for what is effectively Kevin Warsh's inauguration as Fed Chair. Markets are pricing a 99% probability that rates hold at 3.50-3.75%, so the decision itself is not the event. What matters is the updated Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot.

Warsh was sworn in on May 22 and has deliberately kept a low profile, making his first press conference a significant communication event. The macro data heading into the meeting sends mixed signals: May CPI at 4.2% and PPI at 6.5% argue for hawkishness, but if the Iran deal sticks and oil continues dropping, those inflationary impulses fade quickly. Nonfarm payrolls came in at 172,000 in May, strong enough to keep rate cuts off the table but not so hot as to force a hike. Futures markets price the effective fed funds rate rising toward 3.8% by late 2026 and 4.0% by mid-2027, implying the market thinks the next move is more likely a hike than a cut. Cleveland Fed's Hammack has said there is no urgent need to move rates in 2026, reinforcing the "patient hold" baseline.

The key tell will be whether the dot plot median shifts higher. If it does, even slightly, it confirms that the committee is leaning hawkish and that the bar for any easing has risen substantially. Given the Iran deal and potential oil price relief, Warsh may use his first press conference to emphasize data dependence and flexibility, avoiding a premature hawkish commitment.

## Oil: The War Premium Evaporates

The crude selloff is the most consequential market move today. WTI at $80.20 and Brent at $82.80 represent a roughly 23% decline from the April peaks when the Strait of Hormuz was fully blocked. If the deal holds, crude could drift toward the mid-$70s as global supply normalizes. The IEA's projection of a 3.8 million barrel-per-day global surplus in 2026 would become even more bearish for oil if Iranian supply returns fully.

The knock-on effects ripple through the entire macro landscape. Lower oil means lower gasoline prices, which directly improves consumer sentiment (Michigan just bounced to 48.9 from record lows). Lower energy costs ease input prices for manufacturers and airlines. And most critically, it removes the single biggest source of inflationary pressure that has been complicating the Fed's decision framework since March. Energy stocks will face pressure today, but the rest of the equity market benefits from cheaper oil through both the inflation channel and the consumer spending channel.

## Asia and Europe Overnight

Asian markets posted their best session in months. Japan's Nikkei 225 surged 5.0% to 69,318, powered by the peace deal euphoria and a weaker yen boosting exporter earnings. China's CSI 300 rallied 2.4% to 4,892, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng gained 0.8% to 24,910, snapping an eight-session losing streak. The broad-based Asian rally confirms that the Iran risk premium had been weighing on global risk appetite far more than daily price action suggested.

European markets opened sharply higher, with the Stoxx 600 gaining 0.7% to 637 and the DAX jumping 1.8% to 24,635, surpassing its pre-war record high. European energy stocks were the notable laggards as oil's decline hurt the sector, but financials and industrials more than offset the drag.

Nuclear issues carved out for follow-on talks. Ceasefire holds since April 7 now formalized. Market pricing the deal as done; risk is Friday ceremony falls apart.
