Friday, July 10, 2026, written pre-bell. The day the whole week has been pointing to is finally here, and it is arriving with a split personality. SK Hynix lists on the Nasdaq this morning after pricing its American depositary receipts at 149 dollars and raising 26.5 billion dollars, the largest first-time US listing by any foreign company on record, edging past the 25 billion dollars Alibaba raised in 2014. Demand was ferocious, with the book covered roughly seven times, and the company holds about 56 percent of the high-bandwidth memory market that every AI accelerator on the planet depends on. Yet the pre-bell tape does not look like a celebration. Nasdaq 100 futures are lower by about a third of a percent as the chip complex slips into the very debut it was supposed to crown, while the Dow adds about 130 points and small caps hold green. Oil is easing rather than spiking, WTI back near 72 and Brent near 76, as Washington confirms it will keep holding technical talks with Iran even after declaring the ceasefire over. The war premium that ran the tape all week is coming out, and in its place the market is staring at a 30-year yield pushing 4.90 percent. Delta opens earnings season with a clean beat before the bell. The primary market just cast an emphatic vote for the AI memory trade. The secondary market is not so sure.

## Top Call: The Deal Is a Blowout, the Tape Is Cautious, and That Gap Is the Whole Story

The one thing PMs need to carry into the open is that the SK Hynix listing split into two verdicts, and they point in opposite directions. In the primary market the answer was euphoric: the biggest foreign listing in US history, a book covered seven times, direct access at last to the purest large-cap HBM play, and a stock up more than 220 percent this year on a memory shortage that is real and not a story. That validates the entire scarcity thesis the tape has been trading. But in the secondary market the answer is a shrug bordering on caution, with Nasdaq futures red and the chip group sliding into the debut, exactly the sell-the-news and new-supply digestion risk we flagged all week now showing up on cue. Our read is that both are true and the tension between them is the trade. The listing legitimizes the memory and storage stack as the scarce, hardest-to-replicate layer of the AI build, and we stay constructive on that layer through the noise. But a group this richly valued absorbing a fresh 26.5 billion dollars of stock, on a morning when the money is rotating into the Dow and small caps instead, is a market telling you the easy part of this trade is behind it. Add the second rotation underneath, from a fading war premium to a hardening rate overhang, and the posture is clear: own the scarce parts of the stack, respect that the cost of capital is now the enemy rather than the strait, and do not chase the debut pop if it comes.

## SK Hynix: A Record Book Meets a Nervous Tape

Start with the deal itself, because it is genuinely historic. SK Hynix priced 177.9 million ADRs at 149 dollars each, every ten ADRs standing for one underlying Seoul share, to raise 26.5 billion dollars, the largest debut US listing a foreign issuer has ever done. It trades today under the temporary ticker SKHYV before converting to the permanent SKHY on Monday. The order book ran about seven times oversubscribed, the Korea-listed shares tacked on another 5 percent Thursday to sit up roughly 223 percent on the year, and management is steering the proceeds straight into new Korean fabs and EUV lithography scanners to press its HBM lead. This is the deepest capital market on earth handing the dominant memory maker a direct line to US investors, and it should narrow the long-standing valuation discount the group has traded to Micron.

Our take is that the fundamentals earned this listing but the timing forces a hard question. The bull case is intact and if anything strengthened: a seven-times book is not the signature of a top in demand, it is scarce supply meeting real money, and the HBM shortage that has driven this year's memory melt-up is a physical constraint, not sentiment. The bear case is equally live. Dropping 26.5 billion dollars of fresh paper into a jittery, expensive group, on a morning the broader chip tape is already red, is the classic setup for the debut to be a liquidity event the sector has to digest before it can move higher. We lean long the scarce layer, memory and storage, into and through the listing, but we would size for a choppy first few sessions and treat any first-day fade as digestion rather than a verdict on the thesis.

## Oil and Iran: The War Premium Comes Out as Talks Survive the Strikes

The clearest tell that the mood has shifted is crude. WTI has eased back toward 72 and Brent toward 76, unwinding a chunk of this week's war spike, even though the US and Iran traded their heaviest blows since the June framework only days ago. The reason is on the diplomatic wire: Washington confirmed it will continue technical talks with Tehran despite the president calling the ceasefire over, and regional mediators describe extensive efforts to schedule another round and pull both sides back from the strait. The physical waterway has stayed open throughout, tankers keep transiting, and with OPEC-plus still adding barrels and Russian exports near records, the underlying market was oversupplied going into the conflict. So the moment the escalation stops accelerating, gravity reasserts and the premium bleeds out.

Our take is that this is the right direction but a fragile one, and the risk is now two-sided rather than one-way up. We spent the week arguing the strait hedge was cheap precisely because nobody wanted it, and that call has paid as the premium built. With talks alive and crude giving it back, we would take some of that hedge off into strength rather than press it, because a genuine de-escalation plus an oversupplied physical market can send WTI back into the high 60s quickly. But we would not fully abandon the position. A negotiator who still conditions the reopening of Hormuz on Iran's own terms, and a campaign that widened before it paused, means one bad headline reprices this overnight. Trade the fade, keep a small tail hedge on.

## Macro: The Overhang Rotates From the Strait to the Long End

Here is the handoff that matters most for the leadership. All week the risk that mattered was geopolitical, and it is now receding. What is stepping into its place is the cost of money. The 30-year Treasury yield is pushing 4.90 percent, up on the day, and the long end is grinding higher on what the desks are calling persistent higher-yield concerns, the residue of Wednesday's hawkish June minutes that signaled no cuts until 2027 and a committee split down the middle on whether the next move is a hike. With the war premium leaving oil, the inflation scare that came with it eases too, but the structural message does not: this is a Fed that will not cut into an economy that keeps running warm, and the curve is repricing to it.

Our take is that the rotation under the surface, from war to rates, is more important than the SK Hynix headline for how the next month trades. The AI and memory complex is priced for a multi-year buildout financed cheaply and valued on long duration, and a 30-year yield marching toward 5 percent is the single cleanest headwind to that math. It is why the money this morning is moving into the Dow, small caps, and cyclicals rather than piling back into chips even on the sector's marquee day. We would treat any equity strength that leans on a dovish story as a fade, because the Fed just took that story off the table, and we would watch the long end as the real leash on the leadership into next week's June CPI.

## Asia and Europe: Chip Gear Leads Asia, Europe Firms

Asia leaned firmer, led by the parts of the chain most levered to the memory build. Japan's Nikkei added close to a percent as semiconductor-equipment names did the heavy lifting, with Advantest, Tokyo Electron, and Screen Holdings among the strongest, a direct read-through from the same HBM demand that is bringing SK Hynix to New York. Korea was the one to watch as investors squared positions into the listing that pulls a marquee name off the Seoul board and onto the Nasdaq. China stayed the soft spot, the CSI 300 easing about 0.6 percent and the Hang Seng off around 0.4 percent on continued technical weakness and a still-firm dollar, the region's importers getting no relief even as oil cooled.

Europe opened modestly higher after a strong Thursday, the Stoxx 600 up about 0.3 percent with Germany's DAX extending its lead among the majors. The picture into the New York open is a global tape that has swapped one worry for another: the Gulf war is quietly de-escalating for now, and the fresh question is whether a richly valued equity market can keep climbing with the long end of the curve pushing higher. The one consistent thread is the memory and AI-hardware chain, which keeps drawing money on both sides of the world even on a morning its flagship listing meets a nervous US tape.

## Geopolitical Tracker

**US-Iran:** De-escalating at the margin, but unresolved. After the heaviest exchange since the June framework, Washington confirmed it will keep holding technical talks with Tehran even after the president declared the ceasefire over, and mediators report extensive efforts to arrange another round. Crude is handing back its war premium in response, WTI near 72 and Brent near 76. The strait stayed physically open throughout, but Iran still conditions any normalization of Hormuz on its own terms, and the oil-export waiver remains revoked, so the calm is a pause rather than a settlement.

**Ukraine-Russia:** Structurally unresolved and overshadowed. Combat grinds on along the eastern axes and a pending US security package stays a live headline risk, while record Russian crude exports keep the global supply math loose, one reason the Iran premium has bled out so quickly.

**Taiwan Strait and South China Sea:** Quiet. A low-simmer coast-guard standoff with no fresh escalation, especially notable on a day the market's chip attention is fixed on a Korean memory maker rather than the Taiwan foundry chain.

## Notable Earnings

The scheme-relevant catalyst today is the listing, not a print. No AI, memory, storage, or space name reports this morning, and the next real tests of chip demand come next week when TSMC, the foundry at the center of the AI build, and Netflix report on July 16. Today's marquee earnings are Delta's, and while an airline sits outside the memory-and-rockets complex the tape actually trades, this one is a clean macro tell worth reading.

Delta beat cleanly before the open, posting adjusted EPS of 1.56 dollars against the 1.48 consensus on adjusted revenue of 17.67 billion dollars, ahead of the roughly 17.5 billion expected, and guided the third quarter to 2.00 to 2.50 dollars against a 2.02 Street number while reaffirming its full-year 6.50 to 7.50 range. The mix is the story: premium cabins out-earned the main cabin, 6.92 billion dollars against 6.85 billion, and management said fares should stay firm even as fuel prices slide. Our quick take is that this is a genuinely reassuring consumer read and doubly so given the backdrop. The high-end traveler is still spending, and the same oil pullback that is pulling the war premium out of crude now works as a tailwind to jet-fuel costs rather than a headwind, the reverse of the risk we flagged into the print. It does not move the AI trade, but it is a live data point that the real economy the market keeps ignoring looks steadier than the chip tape's nerves suggest.
