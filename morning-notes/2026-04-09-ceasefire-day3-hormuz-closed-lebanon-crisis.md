The ceasefire is holding, barely. What was supposed to be Day 2 of peace instead became 48 hours of diplomatic crisis as Iran accused the US of three violations, Israel launched its largest-ever Lebanon strike (250+ killed), and the Strait of Hormuz remained functionally closed with fewer than 10 ships transiting in the first two days versus a pre-war norm of 130 per day. Markets rallied Tuesday on the original ceasefire euphoria, with the Dow surging 1,325 points (+2.85%), airlines popping double digits, and WTI crashing 16% to $94. But by Wednesday the narrative shifted: oil rebounded 3% to $97.87 as traders realized Hormuz is still a chokepoint, not a corridor. The saving grace came late Wednesday when Netanyahu agreed to open direct negotiations with Lebanon, a key de-escalation that kept equity markets in the green (+0.62% on the S&P). Vance, Witkoff, and Kushner head to Islamabad Saturday for the first face-to-face talks. The ceasefire is alive, but it's on life support.

**Three things that matter this morning:**

1. **Hormuz is open in name only.** Five to nine ships have transited in 48 hours. Zero oil tankers. Iran is charging $1M+ tolls. Goldman says another month of this means $100+ Brent for all of 2026. Dated Brent spot is already above $120; the physical market is far tighter than futures suggest.

2. **The Lebanon question could unravel everything.** Trump and Netanyahu say Lebanon is NOT included in the ceasefire. Iran and Pakistan say it IS. Israel hit 100+ targets in 10 minutes on Monday night, killing 250+ and wounding 1,160. Lebanon declared national mourning. Pakistan physically held back Iranian retaliation overnight April 8–9 through diplomatic back-channels.

3. **The English and Persian versions of the 10-point plan still differ on uranium enrichment.** This is the time bomb. If neither side realizes the other signed a different deal, the ceasefire collapses the moment inspectors arrive.

## The War — Day 40 (Ceasefire Day 3)

### The 72-Hour Timeline

**Monday April 7 — Power Plant Day.** The day began with Trump's 8 PM ET deadline to destroy Iran's power grid. Human chains formed at the Kazerun power plant. Pakistan's ISI chief brokered a last-minute deal. At 6:30 PM ET, 90 minutes before deadline, both sides announced a ceasefire through Pakistan as intermediary. Iran agreed to "supervised peaceful nuclear energy" and Hormuz reopening. The US agreed to a 45-day pause, prisoner exchange, and humanitarian corridors.

**Monday Night — Israel Changes the Calculus.** Within hours of the ceasefire announcement, Israel launched its largest Lebanon operation of the war: 100+ targets struck in under 10 minutes across Beirut's southern suburbs, the Bekaa Valley, and southern Lebanon. Over 250 killed, 1,160 wounded. Lebanon declared three days of national mourning. Iran immediately accused the US of its first ceasefire violation, arguing Lebanon was covered under the agreement.

**Tuesday April 8 — The Euphoria Trade.** Markets responded to the ceasefire headline, not the fine print. The Dow surged 1,325 points (+2.85%). The Nasdaq gained 4.35%. WTI collapsed 16.4% to $94.41, the largest single-day oil decline since the war began. The VIX plunged from 28 to 21. Asia was even more dramatic: KOSPI +6.87%, Nikkei +3.2%, Hang Seng +4.1%. Energy names were crushed (XLE -5.5%) while consumer discretionary, tech, and travel stocks ripped higher.

**Wednesday April 9 — Reality Sets In.** Iran accused the US of three ceasefire violations: (1) the Lebanon strikes, (2) a US drone in Iranian airspace, and (3) denial of Iran's right to uranium enrichment under the Persian version of the agreement. Hormuz remained effectively closed, with Iran suspending tanker traffic entirely and only 5–9 non-oil vessels transiting, each paying $1M+ in tolls. Markets opened flat-to-lower on violation fears, then recovered after Netanyahu made a surprise offer to open direct negotiations with Lebanon. S&P closed +0.62% at 6,824.66. Oil rebounded: WTI +3% to $97.87, touching $100 intraday before the Netanyahu headline pulled it back.

### Iran

Iran's Foreign Ministry released a statement cataloging three "material breaches" of the ceasefire within 36 hours. The most dangerous is the enrichment clause: the English text says Iran accepts "supervised peaceful nuclear energy" while the Persian version reportedly preserves Iran's right to enrich to 20%. Neither side has publicly acknowledged the discrepancy. Pakistan's PM Sharif spent Wednesday working the phones between Tehran and Washington. Iran's IRGC has not stood down from its Hormuz positions; naval assets remain deployed at the eastern chokepoint near Jask.

### Israel / Lebanon

Netanyahu's offer to negotiate directly with Lebanon was the most important development Wednesday. This came after intense US pressure, reportedly Vance called Netanyahu three times Tuesday night. The offer includes a potential 72-hour Lebanon-specific ceasefire if Hezbollah agrees to pull back north of the Litani River. Hezbollah has not responded. The Monday night strikes were described by Lebanese officials as the deadliest single operation of the entire conflict. Hospitals in Beirut are overwhelmed. The UN Security Council held an emergency session Tuesday but no resolution passed.

### Hormuz

This is the real story. The ceasefire promised Hormuz would "reopen within 24 hours." Instead:
- **Ships transited (48 hours):** 5–9 total (vs. 130/day pre-war)
- **Oil tankers transited:** 0
- **Iran's toll:** $1M+ per vessel
- **IRGC naval presence:** Unchanged
- **Insurance premiums:** Still at war-risk levels ($500K+ per transit)

Goldman Sachs warned Wednesday that another month of effective Hormuz closure means Brent stays above $100 for all of 2026. Dated Brent in the physical spot market is already trading above $120, a $20+ premium to futures that signals severe physical tightness. Saudi Arabia is routing 100% of exports via the Red Sea, adding 7–10 days to delivery times and $3–5/barrel in freight costs.

### Diplomatic Path Forward

Vance, Witkoff, and Kushner are heading to Islamabad for Saturday April 11 talks, the first direct diplomatic engagement since the ceasefire. Pakistan has emerged as the essential broker. Key items on the agenda: (1) resolving the English/Persian enrichment discrepancy, (2) Hormuz reopening timeline with verification, (3) Lebanon's status, (4) prisoner exchange mechanics. Iran has signaled it wants the terms approved personally at the highest level, which could take weeks.
