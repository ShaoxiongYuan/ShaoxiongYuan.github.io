Wednesday, June 24, 2026, written pre-market around 7:30am ET. The tape has steadied overnight after Tuesday's violent memory unwind, and the rebound came from the same place the selling did, namely Seoul. S&P 500 futures are up about half a percent near 7,420, Nasdaq 100 futures have clawed back roughly 0.7 percent to 29,960 after dropping 3 percent yesterday, and the Dow is firmer too. The bounce is being led by the very names that broke on Tuesday, with the Kospi closing up 3.26 percent as Samsung surged on a buyback and SK Hynix rallied on a US listing plan. But the steadiness is fragile and conditional, because the one event that actually settles the AI-memory argument arrives tonight when Micron reports fiscal Q3 after the close. As always with a pre-open note, these are overnight and futures levels and they can move by the bell.

## Top Call: Everything Waits for Micron Tonight, and Tuesday's Rout Lowered the Bar

The single most important thing for PMs this morning is that the entire AI-memory complex is holding its breath for one print, and that print lands tonight after the close. Micron reports fiscal Q3 with consensus near $34.5 billion in revenue, roughly $19.72 to $19.95 in EPS, and a record gross margin around 81.6 percent, and after yesterday's de-grossing the stock is no longer priced for perfection. That matters more than the headline numbers. Our read is that Tuesday's flush was a positioning event rather than a fundamental one, and the clearest confirmation is what happened overnight in Korea, where the same memory names that triggered the global selloff snapped back hard. We stay constructive into the print but would respect a two-sided setup that did not exist a week ago. The one number we care about above all is gross margin, because at roughly 81 percent it is the cleanest possible read on whether HBM pricing power is intact, and any softness there or in 2027 commentary would validate the bears just as sentiment turned jittery. A clean beat with margins held and confident HBM4 commentary would reframe the whole rout as a buying opportunity and likely pull the group higher into quarter-end.

## Semiconductors: Korea Snaps Back, and Micron Is the Verdict Tonight

The rebound overnight tells you most of what you need to know about the nature of Tuesday's selloff. The Kospi rallied 3.26 percent in a near mirror image of the prior session's limit-down, and the catalysts were company-specific and constructive rather than macro. Samsung jumped about 9 percent after announcing a 90 trillion won share buyback, an aggressive capital return that forced short covering and steadied the broader index. SK Hynix added a few percent on news it is accelerating a roughly $26 billion US ADR listing, a move that both widens its investor base and signals confidence at the top of the cycle. This is exactly the behavior you would expect if Tuesday was leverage and profit-taking unwinding rather than the demand story rolling over. Nothing in the underlying businesses changed in either direction over 48 hours. What changed was positioning, and positioning is now resetting.

That sets up the most important print of the quarter. After Tuesday, the bar Micron has to clear has shifted lower on price but higher on scrutiny. Consensus sits near $34.5 billion in revenue and around $19.72 in EPS, against company guidance closer to $33.5 billion, with the Street modeling a record gross margin near 81.6 percent. The bull case is structural and intact. Management has said its entire calendar 2026 HBM output is sold out under multi-year contracts, Nvidia has certified Micron alongside Samsung and SK Hynix to supply HBM4 for the next-generation Vera Rubin platform, and Micron remains the only US-based maker of the memory that feeds every AI accelerator in production. The forward tells matter most now. We are watching the trajectory of HBM4 allocations into 2027, any color on closing the gap with SK Hynix on the Nvidia roadmap, and whether that 81 percent margin holds or starts to compress. A clean print frames the rout as a gift. A wobble on margin or 2027 visibility lands on a group with far less cushion than it had a week ago.

## Corporate: FedEx Beats but the Guide Reads Cautious

FedEx delivered its first quarter as a pure-play parcel and logistics company last night, the first report since the June 1 spinoff of FedEx Freight, and the result was a clean operational beat met with a wary market. Adjusted EPS came in at $6.31 against the $6.02 the Street expected, and revenue landed near $25.0 billion versus consensus around $24.3 billion, with the company saying it exceeded its DRIVE transformation cost-savings target for the period. That is a strong print on its face. The catch was the outlook. Management set a calendar 2026 adjusted EPS range of $16.90 to $18.10 and pointed to roughly 11 percent consolidated revenue growth, and the stock slipped 3 to 4 percent after hours as investors focused on a decelerating revenue trajectory rather than the headline beat.

Our take is that FedEx is the soft-landing tell, not the AI tell, and the message it sent is nuanced rather than alarming. The cost program is clearly working and the parcel business is more profitable post-spinoff, but the cautious top-line framing is a quiet flag on goods demand heading into the back half of the year. That reads through to a tape that is already nervous about growth and lands two days before core PCE. We would not over-extrapolate one guide, but pairing a cost-driven earnings beat with a soft revenue outlook is the textbook signature of a late-cycle freight environment, and it deserves attention alongside the more exciting AI print tonight.

## Macro: The De-Grossing Reverses, but the Inflation Wall Is Still Friday

The cross-asset picture this morning is the cleanest evidence that Tuesday was a positioning flush. Gold is steadying near $4,106 rather than extending its crack, the VIX is back down toward 19.6 after spiking above 22, and Treasuries are giving back a touch of their haven bid with the 10-year ticking up a couple of basis points to roughly 4.50 percent as risk appetite returns. When stocks bounce and the fear gauges relax in tandem, that is the mirror image of yesterday's all-correlations-to-one selloff, and it supports the read that investors were raising cash, not repricing the economy.

The backdrop that keeps this from being a clean all-clear is the rate picture under Kevin Warsh and the inflation print waiting at the end of the week. The new Fed has leaned hawkish, lifted its 2026 PCE forecast toward the high 3s, and flipped its dot plot toward a hike, and renewed focus on US rates was a contributing trigger for Tuesday's unwind in the first place. Core inflation has been creeping higher, with the latest reading near 2.9 percent year over year and at its hottest since last autumn, driven by sticky shelter and services. That puts Friday's May core PCE squarely in the spotlight. A hot number would hand the higher-for-longer camp a fundamental anchor and could undo this morning's calm, while a cooler print would let the bulls argue the Fed is chasing a ghost just as the AI trade steadies. With quarter-end one session away, expect positioning to keep amplifying the moves in both directions.

## Oil: The Slide Extends as Iranian Barrels Get the Green Light

Crude pushed to fresh three-month lows overnight, with WTI sliding toward $72 and Brent slipping under $75.50, both down again on the day. The fresh catalyst remains the US Treasury's 60-day license authorizing the production, shipment, and sale of Iranian crude and fuel, which clears a path for more sanctioned barrels just as tankers resume transit through a reopened Strait of Hormuz. Layer that onto an OPEC group still adding supply against a trimmed 2026 demand outlook and the path of least resistance is lower. Oil has now unwound the overwhelming majority of the conflict premium it built in the spring. For portfolios the read-through is the familiar one. Cheaper energy is a clean disinflationary tailwind that feeds directly into Friday's PCE math, even as it keeps draining the energy equities that have been the weakest corner of the market all month.

## Geopolitics: Tehran Disputes the Inspector Deal

The optimism that drove this week's war-premium unwind hit a snag overnight, and it is worth flagging because we leaned on it yesterday. President Trump said Iran had fully and completely agreed to allow nuclear inspections to resume, but Tehran pushed back hard, with Iranian officials saying there are no plans for IAEA inspectors to return to the bombed enrichment sites. That is a meaningful walk-back from Tuesday's framing, when the inspector agreement was presented as a concrete first step. The 60-day ceasefire memorandum signed earlier this month still stands, the strait is still open, and the oil-relevant pieces are still working, which is why crude keeps bleeding lower. But the substantive nuclear file, especially uranium enrichment and the status of Iran's stockpile, is now visibly contested again, and the gap between Washington's account and Tehran's is the live tail risk. We read the market impact as limited for now because the energy and shipping channels are functioning, but a breakdown in the inspection question is the headline that could put a bid back under oil and gold.

## Asia and Europe Overnight

Asia split along the same fault line as Tuesday, just in the other direction. Korea was the standout, with the Kospi rallying 3.26 percent as Samsung climbed about 9 percent on its 90 trillion won buyback and SK Hynix advanced on its accelerated US listing plan, a sharp recovery from the prior session's circuit-breaker collapse. Japan diverged and lagged, with the Nikkei 225 slipping about 0.9 percent to near 71,320 as investors took profits after its record run and rotated away from the names that had led. Greater China firmed, with the Hang Seng up roughly 1 percent to 23,570 on a tech bounce and the CSI 300 higher by about half a percent near 4,713. Europe opened steadier with a modest risk-on tone tracking Asia's memory rebound and the calmer US futures, the Stoxx 600 up about 0.4 percent, the DAX firmer near 24,900, and the FTSE 100 close to flat, as the chip complex that dragged the continent lower on Tuesday found a footing.

## Geopolitical Tracker

**US-Iran:** The 60-day ceasefire memorandum holds and the Strait of Hormuz stays open, but the inspection question has reopened. Washington says Iran agreed to readmit IAEA inspectors; Tehran says there are no such plans for its bombed enrichment sites. Uranium enrichment and the stockpile remain the unresolved core of the talks and the live tail.

**Ukraine-Russia:** Still structurally unresolved and overshadowed by the Iran track. Combat continues along the eastern axes and a pending US security-assistance package is the item to watch for fresh headline risk.

**United Kingdom:** Keir Starmer remains in office pending the Labour leadership contest that opens July 9, with Andy Burnham the front-runner. A domestic story with limited US spillover.

**Taiwan Strait and South China Sea:** PRC coast guard pressure near the Pratas Islands continues at a low simmer with no major escalation.
