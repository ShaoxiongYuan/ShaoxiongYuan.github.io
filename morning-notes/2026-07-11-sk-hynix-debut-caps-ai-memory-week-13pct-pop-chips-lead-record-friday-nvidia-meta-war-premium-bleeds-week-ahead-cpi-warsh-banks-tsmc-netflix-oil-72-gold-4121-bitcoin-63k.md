Saturday, July 11, 2026, weekend wrap written with markets closed. The week that the whole tape had been building toward is over, and it delivered. SK Hynix went public on the Nasdaq Friday and jumped about 13 percent from its offering price to close near 168 dollars, after pricing its American depositary receipts at 149 dollars and raising 26.5 billion dollars, the largest first-time US listing any foreign company has ever done. The primary market did not just accept the AI memory trade, it embraced it, and the secondary tape followed: the S&P 500 closed at a fresh high near 7,575 with Nvidia up almost 4 percent and Meta up about 6 percent doing most of the lifting, the Dow added roughly 150 points to sit near its own record around 52,637, and the Nasdaq Composite pushed to about 26,281. Yet the move was narrow. Small caps went the other way, the Russell 2000 slipping about half a percent, a quiet reminder that this is still a handful of AI names carrying the index rather than a broad advance. Oil kept easing, WTI back near 72 and Brent near 76, as Washington confirmed it will keep talking to Tehran even with the ceasefire officially over. With the marquee listing behind us, the market now pivots to the two tests that actually decide the next leg: Tuesday's June CPI landing alongside new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first testimony to Congress, and an earnings week that finally moves from headlines to numbers with the big banks, ASML, TSMC and Netflix.

## Top Call: The Deal Delivered, Now the Bill Comes Due on Rates and Earnings

The one thing to carry into Monday is that the AI memory trade passed its biggest test of the year and the leadership is intact, but the character of the rally should keep everyone honest. SK Hynix landed the largest foreign listing in history, the book ran seven times covered, and the stock still finished up double digits even after 26.5 billion dollars of fresh supply hit the tape. That is a real validation of the scarcity thesis, and Friday's record closes with Nvidia and Meta out front confirmed that money still wants the scarce, hardest to replicate layer of the AI build. Our read is that the thesis is fine and the tape is fragile at the same time. The advance is dangerously narrow, with small caps falling on the same day the headline indices set records, so the market is paying up for a shrinking list of winners while the average stock sits out. That is exactly the setup that gets tested when the story turns from listings to hard data, and next week it does. June CPI and Warsh's debut before Congress on Tuesday will reset the rates picture that has been the quiet enemy all along, and within 48 hours the banks, ASML, TSMC and Netflix have to put real numbers behind the AI capex narrative. We stay constructive on the memory and compute layer through the noise, but the easy, headline driven part of this trade is over, and from here the leadership has to earn its multiple against a no cuts Fed and a live earnings season rather than against a countdown to an IPO.

## SK Hynix: The Listing That Validated the Trade

Start with the event that defined the week. SK Hynix priced 177.9 million ADRs at 149 dollars apiece, every ten ADRs representing one Seoul share, to raise 26.5 billion dollars, and the stock opened around 170 before settling up roughly 13 percent near 168. That tops Alibaba's 2014 debut to become the largest first-time US listing by a foreign issuer on record. The company holds somewhere between 50 and 55 percent of the high bandwidth memory market that every AI accelerator depends on, its HBM output is effectively sold out through 2026, and management is steering the proceeds straight into Korean fabs and about 8.6 billion dollars of new ASML lithography gear to press that lead. The stock trades under the temporary ticker SKHYV before converting to the permanent SKHY on Tuesday, with a pair of leveraged single stock ETFs, SKUU and SKDD, set to launch Monday, a sign of just how much retail appetite the name has already drawn.

Our take is that the fundamentals earned the reception and the pop is the right signal, but the arrival of the leveraged products the same week tells you where the risk now sits. A seven times oversubscribed book is scarce supply meeting real demand, not the signature of a top, and a stock that absorbs 26.5 billion dollars of new paper and still closes up double digits is a market voting with conviction. The listing should also help narrow the long standing Korea discount the shares have carried, giving US institutions a direct large cap HBM vehicle for the first time. The flip side is that speculative plumbing, the leveraged ETFs and the retail frenzy, tends to arrive at exactly the moment a move gets crowded. We stay long the scarce memory and storage layer but treat the debut euphoria itself as a reason to size carefully rather than chase.

## Oil and Iran: The Premium Keeps Bleeding as Talks Outlast the Ceasefire

The clearest tell that the mood shifted this week is crude. WTI eased to about 72 dollars and Brent to about 76, giving back the bulk of the war spike even though Washington declared the June ceasefire dead. The reason is on the diplomatic wire. President Trump said the ceasefire is over but confirmed the US agreed to keep holding talks at Tehran's request, and mediators in Qatar and Pakistan are working to bring both sides back to the table. Crucially the Strait of Hormuz has stayed physically open, with at least 15 commercial vessels transiting in the past 24 hours even as Iran keeps asserting control over parts of the waterway and the US oil export waiver stays revoked. With OPEC-plus still adding barrels and the physical market oversupplied, the moment escalation stopped accelerating, gravity took the premium back out.

Our take is that the direction is right but the risk is genuinely two sided into a weekend with markets shut. A market that is oversupplied on the physics can send WTI into the high 60s quickly if the talks gain traction, and that would be a clean tailwind for the consumer and for the disinflation story CPI is about to test. But the Strait remains the unresolved knot, the waiver is still gone, and a single bad headline over the weekend reprices crude before Monday's open with no futures tape to cushion it. This is the one live wildcard in an otherwise calm setup, and it is the reason weekend positioning stayed cautious even after Friday's records.

## Macro: The Real Contest Moves to CPI and a Brand New Fed Chair

Here is the handoff that matters most for how the next leg trades. All week the swing factor was the listing and the war. Next week it becomes the cost of money and the man who now sets it. June CPI lands Tuesday, and the consensus looks for headline inflation to ease toward roughly 3.9 percent year over year, helped by June's sharp drop in oil, while core is expected to stay sticky near 2.9 percent. The same morning, new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, sworn in in late May, delivers his first testimony to Congress. That combination is the whole game. A soft headline print alongside a Warsh who sounds even modestly open to patience could pull the long end down and broaden the rally beyond the AI handful. A firm core print alongside a hawkish debut does the opposite and puts the 30 year, which finished the week near 4.9 percent, back on a path toward 5 percent.

Our take is that the rates picture, not the SK Hynix headline, is what decides whether next week broadens or breaks. The AI and memory complex is priced for a multi year buildout financed cheaply and valued on long duration, so a long end grinding toward 5 percent under a no cuts Fed is the single cleanest headwind to that math. It is also why the money on Friday flowed into the mega cap AI names and away from small caps rather than into a broad advance. Warsh is an unknown quantity in the chair, and his first words to Congress will carry outsized weight precisely because the market has no read on him yet. We would treat any equity strength that leans on a dovish interpretation as suspect until the print and the testimony actually confirm it.

## Sector Spotlights

**AI and Semiconductors:** The group had the week and it earned it. Beyond the SK Hynix debut, Samsung guided to a roughly nineteen fold jump in quarterly operating profit on the same memory shortage, Nvidia rose almost 4 percent Friday to drive about 70 percent of the S&P's gain, and Meta jumped 6 percent after a positive SemiAnalysis note on its AI compute business. The read through is that scarcity is still the story across memory, packaging and compute, with TSMC's advanced node and CoWoS capacity described as fully tight. Our view stays constructive, but next week's ASML and TSMC prints are where the capex narrative gets marked to actual numbers.

**Financials:** The banks open the real earnings season Tuesday with JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Citigroup and Goldman Sachs, then Morgan Stanley and BlackRock Wednesday. With the yield curve steepening and the long end near the highs, net interest margin commentary and any read on credit quality will matter more than the headline EPS. This is the first broad, non AI test of whether the real economy underneath the index is as steady as Delta's clean beat last week suggested.

**Energy:** Crude spent the week handing back its war premium, WTI near 72 and Brent near 76, and the sector now trades on whether US-Iran talks hold rather than on the Strait. With OPEC-plus still adding supply and the physical market long, the path of least resistance is lower barring a fresh escalation, which cuts two ways: a relief for the consumer and disinflation, a drag on energy earnings.

## Geopolitical Tracker

**US-Iran:** De-escalating at the margin but formally unresolved. The June ceasefire is officially dead per the White House, yet both sides have agreed to keep talking, with Qatar and Pakistan mediating an effort to schedule another round. Crude has handed back most of its war premium in response. The Strait of Hormuz stays the sticking point, physically open with vessels transiting but with Iran still asserting control and the US oil waiver revoked. The calm is a pause, not a settlement, and it is the weekend's one real headline risk.

**Ukraine-Russia:** Structurally unresolved and overshadowed. Combat grinds on and record Russian crude exports keep the global supply math loose, one reason the Iran premium bled out so fast.

**Taiwan Strait:** Quiet. No fresh escalation, notable on a week the market's chip attention sat with a Korean memory maker rather than the Taiwan foundry chain, though TSMC's Thursday print will put Taiwan back in focus.

## Notable Earnings

No scheme-relevant AI, memory, storage or rocket name reports over the weekend, so the earnings story this week is a preview rather than a reaction, and it is a heavy one. The prints that actually matter to the tape the market trades arrive Wednesday and Thursday. ASML reports Wednesday, and as the sole supplier of the EUV lithography machines that SK Hynix just committed 8.6 billion dollars to buy, its bookings are the cleanest forward read on whether the memory and logic buildout is still accelerating. TSMC follows Thursday, and as the foundry at the dead center of the AI build, its capex guide and any color on CoWoS advanced packaging capacity, described this week as fully tight, is the single most important data point for the entire complex. Netflix, also Thursday, sits outside the memory and rockets scheme but is a useful tell on the high end consumer, with the Street looking for roughly 79 cents of EPS on about 12.6 billion dollars of revenue.

Our quick take is that the setup rhymes with the SK Hynix week: expectations are high and the bar to simply confirm the narrative is now steep. A strong ASML booking number and a raised TSMC capex plan would keep the scarcity thesis fed and give the narrow leadership a fundamental leg to stand on. Anything that hints the capex cycle is even flattening, on stocks priced for years of acceleration, would land hard precisely because the group is this crowded and this richly valued. This is the week the AI trade stops being about who is listing and starts being about who is actually shipping.
