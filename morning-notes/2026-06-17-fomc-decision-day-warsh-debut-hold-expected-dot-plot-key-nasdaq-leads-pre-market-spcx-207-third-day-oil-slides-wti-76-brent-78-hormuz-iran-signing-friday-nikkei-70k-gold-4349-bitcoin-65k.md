Wednesday pre-open, 6:30 AM ET. Today is the day. The FOMC announces its rate decision at 2 PM ET, followed by Kevin Warsh's first press conference as Chair at 2:30 PM. Markets have priced a 97% probability of a hold at 3.50-3.75%, so the rate call itself is a formality. What matters is the updated dot plot, the Summary of Economic Projections, and whether Warsh signals a bias shift from easing toward neutral or tightening. Nasdaq futures are leading the tape higher, up 0.59% to 30,493, outperforming a flat Dow, as tech sentiment recovers from last week's Broadcom-induced wobble. Oil continues to slide with Brent dropping below $79 and WTI falling to $75.47, the fifth straight session of declines as the Hormuz reopening timeline and OPEC supply increases weigh on pricing. Japan's Nikkei rose 0.8% to close near 70,000 on a 17% surge in May exports, while Hong Kong and China slipped. The G7 summit wraps up today in Evian. The Iran peace deal signing is confirmed for Friday in Switzerland.

## FOMC Decision Day: Warsh's Debut Sets the Tone

This is the main event. The Federal Open Market Committee concludes its two-day meeting today, and while the rate decision is virtually a foregone conclusion at 3.50-3.75%, the surrounding communication carries real risk. May CPI at 4.2% and PPI at 6.5% remain uncomfortably above target, even as the energy shock from the Strait of Hormuz closure is beginning to fade with oil down over 20% from April peaks. The macro picture is genuinely conflicted: strong payrolls (172K in May), resilient consumer spending, but collapsing housing starts (-15.4% in May) and elevated inflation expectations (Michigan 1Y at 4.6%).

The dot plot is where the action is. If the median 2026 dot shifts higher, suggesting the committee sees the next move as a hike rather than a cut, that would be a hawkish surprise even if rates stay on hold today. Futures currently imply the effective fed funds rate reaches 3.8% by late 2026, meaning the market is already pricing in a meaningful chance of one hike. The other unknown is whether the committee formally drops its easing bias. Warsh has been silent since his May 22 swearing-in, so his press conference at 2:30 PM ET will be dissected word by word for hints about his framework, reaction function, and tolerance for above-target inflation. A more hawkish-than-expected dot plot paired with Warsh signaling patience on cuts could push the 10Y yield above 4.50% and weigh on rate-sensitive sectors. The bond market is already positioning: the 10Y yield fell 5 basis points overnight to 4.43%, suggesting some participants expect a dovish surprise, though that could simply reflect pre-event hedging.

## Oil: The Slide Accelerates

Crude is in its fifth consecutive session of declines and the trajectory is steepening. WTI fell to $75.47, down roughly 6% from Monday's levels, while Brent dropped below $79. The dual forces driving the selloff are now well established: the Iran-US peace deal that will reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restore 20% of global oil and LNG transit capacity, and OPEC's decision to increase production by 188,000 barrels per day in July, its fourth consecutive monthly increase since April.

The supply picture is becoming increasingly bearish. OPEC cut its own 2026 demand growth forecast to 970,000 bpd, the second consecutive downward revision. Non-OPEC production continues expanding: US Permian output sits near 13.7 million bpd, Guyana now exceeds 900,000 bpd with ExxonMobil's Yellowtail project, and Brazil is adding FPSOs in the Santos Basin. The IEA has warned that a full Hormuz reopening could create a surplus of roughly 5 million barrels per day once Gulf producers return to pre-war output. OPEC is clearly prioritizing market share recovery over price support, a strategic pivot that echoes the 2014 playbook. For equity markets, the energy complex drag is increasingly offset by the consumer benefit: falling gasoline prices support household spending and give the Fed one less reason to hike. Energy stocks remain under pressure as the war premium drains from the sector.

## SpaceX Post-IPO Consolidation

SPCX is trading around $207 in pre-market, down 2.6% from yesterday's close of $212, as the stock enters its natural post-IPO consolidation phase. After surging from a $135 IPO price to a $212 close, the stock is still up roughly 54% in three trading days with a market cap approaching $2.7 trillion. The Cursor acquisition announcement on Monday provided a temporary catalyst, but the reality of digesting a $60 billion all-stock deal and the mechanics of lock-up expiry schedules are now weighing on shares.

The key near-term catalyst is execution. SpaceX needs to demonstrate that Cursor's $4 billion ARR justifies a 15x revenue multiple, and that the integration into Starlink and satellite operations produces tangible synergies. Jim Chanos's warning about 100x revenue stocks is worth keeping in mind, though SpaceX's combination of launch monopoly, Starlink recurring revenue, and now AI software makes it unlike any company the public markets have priced before. The more immediate impact is the rotation within the space sector: Rocket Lab, AST SpaceMobile, Intuitive Machines, and Planet Labs all sold off on Friday as SpaceX's record IPO pulled capital from smaller space names. Rocket Lab joins the Nasdaq-100 on Monday June 22, which should provide a short-term technical bid. ASTS rose 5% pre-market ahead of its Block 2 BlueBird satellite launch from Cape Canaveral.

## Nikkei Nears 70,000 on Export Surge

Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.8% to 69,926, flirting with the psychologically significant 70,000 level after briefly crossing it intraday on Tuesday. The latest catalyst was May trade data showing Japanese exports surged 17% year-over-year, driven by robust demand for technology and semiconductor equipment. The export strength validates the thesis that Japanese manufacturers are benefiting from both the weak yen (which boosts competitiveness) and the global AI infrastructure buildout.

However, the BOJ's rate hike to 1% yesterday complicates the picture going forward. The carry trade dynamics that have fueled Japanese equity flows are incrementally less attractive with each rate increase. Yesterday's hike was absorbed calmly, but the Nikkei's failure to sustain gains above 70,000 suggests resistance at that level. The yen carry trade unwind risk remains the tail risk to monitor. Any indication from the BOJ that 1.25% is the next target, or that bond purchase tapering will accelerate, could trigger the kind of sharp reversal seen in August 2024. For now, Japan remains in the sweet spot of orderly monetary normalization plus structural export competitiveness, but the window is narrowing.

## Semiconductor Outlook: Micron Earnings Loom

Micron reports Q3 FY2026 earnings on June 24, and the setup is both elevated and pivotal. Wall Street expects EPS of $19.95, implying 942% year-over-year growth, with revenue expected at $34.66 billion, up 272%. The stock has surged 246% year-to-date, with shares near $1,088, built almost entirely on the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) supercycle that feeds AI accelerators. The critical question is whether this represents a durable demand shift or another peak in the notoriously cyclical memory business.

The bull case rests on Micron's statement that its entire 2026 HBM output is sold out under multi-year contracts, with customers increasingly signing three-to-five-year agreements, a structural change from the spot-driven cycles of the past. The bear case points to Broadcom's Q3 AI chip guidance miss from June 4, which rattled the entire semiconductor complex and raised questions about the linearity of hyperscaler spending. The SIA estimates that semiconductor technologies account for 95% of an AI data center rack's value, meaning any deceleration in the AI capex cycle gets amplified across the entire supply chain. Intel's pre-market gain of 4% on its 18A-P node production announcement is a positive signal for domestic chip manufacturing, but the sector remains in a show-me mode after the Broadcom disappointment.

## G7 Wraps Up, Iran Signing Friday

The 52nd G7 summit concludes today in Evian-les-Bains, France. The final communique is expected to reaffirm "unwavering support for Ukraine," announce increased air defense assistance to Kyiv, and pledge coordinated economic pressure on Russia. However, the real geopolitical headlines center on Iran. The formal signing ceremony for the US-Iran peace deal is confirmed for Friday, June 19, in Switzerland. Canadian PM Mark Carney has called the memorandum of understanding "a game changer," and Trump has vowed to release the full text within days.

The deal extends the ceasefire for 60 days while nuclear enrichment issues are carved out for separate negotiations. The market is largely treating the deal as done, with residual risk centered on hardliner opposition in Tehran and the timeline for sanctions relief. For equities, the reduction in geopolitical tail risk is unambiguously positive, especially for energy-importing economies and consumer discretionary sectors that benefit from lower fuel costs. The Strait of Hormuz reopening remains the most market-relevant aspect: once fully operational, it restores the transit of roughly 20% of global oil and LNG flows. Mine-clearing and tanker repositioning will take weeks to months, but the direction is clear.
