## Ongoing Conflict | Iran War Week 3 | Hormuz Still Closed

> **🚨 STATUS No Ceasefire. Hormuz Blockaded. LNG Crisis Emergent. War May Be Long.**
>
> As of Friday March 13 (Day 14 of the conflict), the Strait of Hormuz remains near-closed at 10% of normal traffic. Iran's new Supreme Leader Khamenei stated again that Hormuz closure is "a tool to pressure the enemy." Iran forces attacked commercial tankers mid-week. Trump says "there is practically nothing left to bomb" but Israel says the operation has "no time limit." The US Navy may begin escorting tankers through Hormuz by end of March at the earliest (Energy Secretary Wright, CNBC). A Time magazine analysis is titled: 'Why the War with Iran Could Be a Long One.' This is not a short-duration event.

**Oil arithmetic:** Every week Hormuz stays closed, the cumulative supply deficit grows. Goldman now models 21 days at 10% normal flow. Commodity futures are pricing no normalisation before August 2027. Brent has a structural floor around $90 until the Strait reopens.

**The LNG escalation changes the risk map.** The Qatar force majeure is not a Hormuz story. Ras Laffan is in the Persian Gulf, not the strait. Its force majeure signals that the conflict's energy disruption radius is expanding. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Pakistan are scrambling. European LNG spot markets repricing.

**Watch for: US executive action on energy.** Multiple Republican senators sent letters demanding action. A Presidential executive order on energy — emergency SPR sales, expedited LNG export permits, or direct price controls — remains a tail risk that would spike markets intraday.

## FOMC Preview | March 18 — The Most Important Meeting of 2026

> **🏛️ FOMC Powell Faces Stagflation. No Good Outcome. Every Word Will Move Markets.**
>
> The March 17-18 FOMC meeting is the most consequential since the 2022 rate hike cycle. The Fed holds at 3.50-3.75% (92%+ CME FedWatch probability) but the statement, dot plot, and SEP (Summary of Economic Projections) must now incorporate: $100 Brent, a −92K NFP print, 2.4% CPI (pre-shock), and a war with no end date. The dot plot will show whether members are shifting cuts further out or pricing in hikes. Powell's press conference language on "inflation progress," "policy patience," and "downside risks" will be parsed by algorithms in real time. Hawkish shift = S&P breaks below 6,600. Dovish pivot = oil-driven inflation credibility crisis. There is no neutral outcome at $100 Brent.

**Scenario A: Hold + hawkish tone (most likely, 55%).** Fed acknowledges oil-driven inflation is "uncomfortably elevated," reiterates data dependence, signals cuts remain on the table but pushes first cut to Q4 2026 at earliest. Dot plot shows 1 cut in 2026. Market reaction: S&P −1 to −1.5%.

**Scenario B: Hold + neutral tone (30%).** Powell threads the needle — acknowledges inflation risk but emphasises labour market deterioration risk equally. Dot plot unchanged at 2 cuts. Market relief rally: S&P +0.5 to +1%.

**Scenario C: Hold + dovish pivot (15%).** Powell pivots to downside risks, signals readiness to cut if oil shock tips economy into recession. Market initially rallies, then sells off as inflation credibility concern sets in. Volatile session.

**The dot plot is the sleeper.** Updated quarterly. If members reduce 2026 cut projections from 2 to 0-1, that alone moves markets regardless of Powell's language.
