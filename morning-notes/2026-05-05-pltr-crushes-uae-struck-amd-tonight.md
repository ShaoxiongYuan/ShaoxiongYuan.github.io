Tuesday pre-market, 7:00 AM ET. Two stories own the tape this morning, and they pull in opposite directions. Palantir delivered the cleanest software print in the S&P 500 in over a decade after Monday's close, with revenue up 85% to $1.63 billion, US commercial up 133%, full year guidance lifted to $7.66 billion, and the stock initially up only 1.5% in aftermarket because everything good was already in the price. Then at roughly 9:00 PM ET Iran broke the April 8 ceasefire, firing 12 ballistic missiles, 3 cruise missiles, and 4 drones at the United Arab Emirates, igniting a fire at the Fujairah Petroleum Industries Zone and forcing ADNOC to shut a 922,000 barrel per day refinery. Brent sits at $112.90, off the overnight $116 high but still 4% above Monday's close, and S&P futures have managed to claw back to +0.20% on the back of the PLTR halo and a sense that Tuesday's Iran salvo is theatre rather than escalation. We do not share that read.

The geopolitical sequence matters more than any single tape today. Iran's salvo answered Project Freedom on its second day, and the US Navy responded by sinking six small Iranian boats overnight, with two of those vessels reportedly armed with rocket pods. The kinetic threshold we flagged Monday morning, which was whether Iran fires on a US-escorted civilian vessel, was not crossed last night. The threshold that was crossed is far more damaging strategically, namely a direct strike on Gulf Cooperation Council infrastructure for the first time since the war began. The UAE has been the most cautious GCC member on US-Iran escalation, and Tehran just removed any incentive for Abu Dhabi to broker. Pakistan-mediated talks are now the only working channel, and they did not survive Sunday's 14-point proposal becoming Tuesday's drone strike. Brent's overnight $116 print is the market's first vote on whether ceasefire is salvageable. The pullback to $113 is the second vote, partial only, contingent on no further escalation through Wednesday.

Three things into Tuesday:

1. **PLTR is the print of the year, and the muted reaction is the single most important read in software.** US commercial revenue printed $595 million on a $440 million whisper, growing 133% year over year against a +94% consensus and a +110% buyside bar. The full year US commercial guide moved to $3.224 billion (+120%), which is the level that historically inflects the AIP narrative from "hot product cycle" into "platform monopoly." The stock was up just 1.5% in aftermarket because the multiple was already at 46 times forward revenue and Burry's Q4 short still looms. This is the pattern of a stock that needs a 20% reset to clean up positioning before the next leg up. We hold the long, sell the May 16 $130/$120 put spread for a gain, and rotate to a longer-dated June $200/$220 call vertical funded by the $145 strike short put.

2. **The UAE strike rewrites the energy book on a four-week view.** Fujairah is a separate matter from the Strait itself, the entire reason the Habshan-Fujairah pipeline exists is to bypass Hormuz risk on the run from inland fields to the Gulf of Oman. ADNOC running 922,000 barrels per day through Fujairah was the single largest piece of "operational despite blockade" capacity in the GCC. Take that offline for any duration and the spot cargo market loses one of its pressure-release valves. Brent backwardation steepens, the front-month versus December 2026 spread blows out, and the calendar trade we put on Monday shifts from "ceasefire normalization" to "war premium permanence." We are reversing that calendar spread today.

3. **AMD reports tonight, and the binary is still gross margin.** Consensus sits at $1.28 to $1.30 EPS and $9.84 billion in revenue, +33% year over year. The stock has run 64% in April and 68% YTD, which is the entire reason implied vol is just 8% on a quarter where the data center number probably prints 30% above Q4. Lisa Su's job tonight is to thread the OpenAI 6GW deal commentary, the Meta 6GW deal commentary, and a clean Q2 gross margin guide above 54%. Our base case is a slightly disappointing GM print at 53.8% offset by a strong Q3 outlook, leaving the stock flat to down 3% in aftermarket. We are flat into the print after taking off the long position last week.

## UAE Attack and the Hormuz Tape Day 2

The strike package was multi-vector and multi-target, which is the operational signature of a planned response rather than a tactical reaction. UAE air defense intercepted 12 ballistic missiles and 3 cruise missiles, with one cruise missile getting through to Fujairah. Four drones were fired, with two reaching the Fujairah Petroleum Industries Zone, igniting the VTTI storage facility and a separate ADNOC refinery line. ADNOC has shut down 922,000 bpd of refining capacity for an "indefinite assessment period." Three Indian nationals are confirmed injured. Multiple flights diverted to Muscat. UAE airspace was partially closed from 4:30 AM to 6:15 AM local time. A cargo ship is on fire off the Fujairah coast as of writing, with UKMTO reporting smoke and structural damage but no confirmed crew casualties.

The strategic read on Fujairah specifically: this facility was the entire point of the Habshan-Fujairah pipeline build-out, which was completed in 2012 specifically to give the UAE a sales channel that did not require Strait of Hormuz transit. Hitting Fujairah is hitting the bypass. It removes the cleanest "operational despite Hormuz blockade" capacity in the Gulf and forces every barrel back through the contested waterway. That is bullish front-month Brent for two reasons. First, the immediate cargo loss of roughly 900K bpd. Second, the optionality loss on the bypass route as a contingency for any further Hormuz disruption.

The US response so far has been measured. CENTCOM confirmed the sinking of six small Iranian craft, two of which were "armed with rocket pods" and were intercepted within 12 nautical miles of US-escorted merchant vessels. Defense Secretary Hegseth held a 5:30 AM ET press briefing reiterating that Project Freedom is "defensive in nature, focused in scope, and temporary in duration." That language is deliberate de-escalation framing on the US side. Iran has denied responsibility for the Fujairah fire, with state media blaming "US military adventurism." That denial is doing a lot of structural work. It tells us Iran wants the strike to register as a warning, not as a casus belli. We read the tape as 35% probability the ceasefire framework recovers within two weeks, 50% extended low-grade conflict with sporadic strikes and oil in the $105 to $120 range, and 15% full re-escalation with Brent printing $130+.

**Two market-moving headlines we expect today:**
- ADNOC operational update on Fujairah, expected mid-morning local UAE time (4:00 AM ET to 7:00 AM ET)
- Trump statement, expected mid-morning ET. Tone matters more than substance. A "very tough decisions" framing is far more bearish than "the Iranians made a mistake"
