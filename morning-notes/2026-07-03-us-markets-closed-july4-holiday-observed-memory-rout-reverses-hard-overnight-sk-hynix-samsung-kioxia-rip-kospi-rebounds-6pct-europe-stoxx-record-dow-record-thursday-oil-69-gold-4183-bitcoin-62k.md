Friday, July 3, 2026, and this is a holiday note. US cash equity and bond markets are both shut for Independence Day, observed today because the Fourth falls on Saturday, so Wall Street reopens Monday, July 6. Thursday's closing prints are frozen behind us, which means the levels that matter for positioning are the ones being set right now in Asia and Europe, and they are telling a very different story from the one that ended the US session. The memory-chip rout that swept Wall Street on Wednesday and then crushed the Kospi by nearly 8 percent on Thursday has reversed with a vengeance overnight. Seoul is up almost 6 percent, the Korean memory giants are ripping, Kioxia is leading Tokyo higher, and Europe's Stoxx 600 has printed a fresh record on its best week in more than a month. The dovish jobs miss is still doing its work, the tone is risk-on almost everywhere the tape is open, and the thin holiday session is a good moment to ask whether Thursday's chip panic was the start of something or just a two-day positioning flush.

## Top Call: The Memory Panic Was a Flush, Not a Thesis Break

The one thing PMs need to carry into the long weekend is that the overnight tape just answered the question Thursday posed. The memory complex that looked like it was breaking down forty-eight hours ago is the single strongest group in the world this morning. SK Hynix is up almost 11 percent, Samsung is up more than 8, Kioxia is up better than 9 and dragging the Nikkei green, and the Kospi has recovered most of Thursday's collapse in one session. That is the signature of a positioning washout that overshot, not of a fundamental crack. We were early to flag the crowding risk in memory and we owned the crack when it came, so we will be just as direct now that the other side has printed: a group that round-trips a 10 percent move in two sessions is being driven by flows, not by earnings, and the earnings, as Micron's blowout showed on Wednesday, remain excellent. Our read is that the AI memory and storage thesis survived its first real stress test intact. We would use the reversal to re-establish rather than chase, because the SK Hynix listing on July 10 and the Nasdaq rebalance next week can still whip this group around, and a holiday bounce on light volume is not the same as an all-clear.

## The Overnight Reversal: Memory Rips and Asia Snaps Back

The clearest read of the overnight session is that the contagion trade unwound as fast as it built. On Thursday a US chip wobble became a global one because two names make up roughly half the Kospi, so a memory selloff was effectively a selloff of the whole Korean market. This morning that same concentration worked in reverse. Samsung and SK Hynix carried Seoul up almost 6 percent, and the recovery rolled through the region's supply chain, with the Hong Kong and mainland chip names that got hit hardest on Thursday clawing back a chunk of the damage. Japan told the same story through a different name. Kioxia, the storage maker, jumped more than 9 percent and did the heavy lifting to push the Nikkei up about 1 percent, exactly the mirror image of Thursday when the memory names dragged Tokyo lower.

What makes the bounce credible rather than a dead-cat is that nothing in the fundamental picture changed for the better overnight. There was no new supply agreement, no dropped lawsuit, no delayed listing. The only thing that changed is that the forced sellers were done and the price had fallen far enough to bring buyers back to a group whose pricing power is still the best in tech. That is worth remembering. The story that powered the best quarter since 2020 did not need good news to bounce, it just needed the panic to exhaust itself, and on this evidence it has for now.

## Europe: Stoxx 600 Prints a Record While the US Sleeps

With Wall Street closed, Europe is setting the risk tone and it is decisively positive. The pan-European Stoxx 600 pushed to a fresh record high and is on track for its best week in over a month. Germany's DAX is up about 0.7 percent, the UK's FTSE 100 is up around 0.4 percent to roughly 10,700 and a four-month high, and the French CAC is modestly green. The driver is the same dovish read that lifted US small caps on Thursday. A cooling US labor market pulls global yields lower and lifts the rate-sensitive corners of the market, and Europe, which is less concentrated in the crowded US AI winners, gets the tailwind without carrying the memory-rotation baggage. Our take is that Europe's record on a US holiday is a genuine tell, not a quirk of thin trading. When the world's second-largest equity bloc makes a new high on the same macro impulse that is rotating money inside the US market, it says the rate-cut trade has broad legs, and it argues against reading Thursday's Nasdaq weakness as anything more than an internal rotation.

## Macro: The Dovish Jobs Miss Still Owns the Narrative

The number that framed the entire week remains the June payrolls miss, and with no fresh US data today it keeps its grip on the tape. The economy added just 57,000 jobs against a consensus near 115,000, the third soft labor read in a row after ADP's 98,000, while the unemployment rate slipped to 4.2 percent. The market's interpretation held all the way into the holiday: a labor market cooling this fast forces the Fed back toward cuts, and futures now price roughly a coin flip on a September move where a hike was the base case only two weeks ago. That is a real repricing, and it is why Thursday's tape rewarded small caps and defensives, why gold is pressing higher, and why the dollar is soft. The tension has not gone away, though. New Chair Kevin Warsh spent last week insisting inflation is still too high, and a bloc of his colleagues has signaled support for hikes this year. A labor market printing under 60,000 jobs while the central bank frets about prices is the stagflation-lite setup that keeps us cautious on paying up for long-duration growth, even as the rate-cut odds climb. For today the bond market is closed, so the two-year sits frozen near 4.02 percent and the ten-year near 4.36 percent, and the next real test is the reopen Monday.

## Oil: Pinned Near $69 as the Supply Glut Story Holds

Crude is quiet and slightly soft into the holiday, with WTI hovering around $68.50 and Brent near $71.70, both little changed on the day but still down sharply over the past month. The structural story is unchanged and it is bearish. Iran says it has moved tens of millions of barrels since the naval blockade came off, Russian exports are running at record levels, OPEC keeps adding supply, and the market has stopped pricing any real risk of a Hormuz closure, so the glut narrative for the back half of the year is intact. For portfolios this remains clean disinflation that reinforces the rate-cut case a soft jobs report just strengthened. The only nuance worth keeping in view is the one we have flagged all week: crude this weak is partly a peace-and-supply story and partly a demand story, and a market cheering the disinflation should keep asking whether oil is also whispering that growth is cooling faster than record-adjacent equities assume.

## Iran: Doha Talks Conclude With Progress but the Toll Fight Drags On

The geopolitical file stayed constructive enough to keep pressure off oil. The latest round of indirect US-Iran technical talks in Doha concluded with both Qatar and the negotiators describing positive progress, building on the 60-day memorandum the two presidents signed in mid-June. Vice President Vance said the talks are going well and focused on the nuclear and missile files, though outside experts caution the immediate work is really about keeping the existing framework intact rather than resolving the hard nuclear questions. Shipping is operationally normal, with more than 35 vessels transiting the Strait in the latest 24-hour count. The sticking point is money, not missiles. Washington is pressing Iran to drop transit fees entirely while Tehran continues to assert shared control of the waterway and floats a voluntary toll. Our read is unchanged: open lanes are the correct base case because that is the only variable that moves the real economy, but a framework is not a signature, and a breakdown before the 60-day window closes is still the cheapest tail nobody is paying up for with the VIX near 15.

## Geopolitical Tracker

**US-Iran:** Doha talks concluded with reported progress and a plan to continue, the Strait of Hormuz stays operationally open with normal transit counts, and the unresolved fight is over Iran's demand for passage tolls versus the free transit the June memorandum envisioned. This is still the single largest swing factor for oil and gold, and the framework remains unsigned.

**Ukraine-Russia:** Structurally unresolved and overshadowed by the Gulf, with Russian crude exports at record levels feeding directly into the global supply glut now pressuring oil. Combat continues along the eastern axes and a pending US security package is the live headline risk.

**Taiwan Strait and South China Sea:** PRC coast guard pressure near the Pratas Islands continues at a low simmer with no major escalation. The pattern of incremental activity persists but stays contained.
