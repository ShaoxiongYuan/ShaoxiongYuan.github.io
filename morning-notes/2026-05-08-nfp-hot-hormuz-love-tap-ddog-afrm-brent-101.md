## Hormuz Exchange of Fire and the Iran Memo Recalibration

The fire exchange happened around 11:00 PM ET overnight, when three US Navy destroyers transiting the Strait of Hormuz came under what CENTCOM described as "unprovoked Iranian attacks" and responded with self-defense strikes. Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters claimed the US violated the ceasefire by targeting an Iranian tanker and that Iranian retaliation against US Navy vessels east of Hormuz inflicted "significant damage." CENTCOM denied any US asset hits. Press TV reported normalization by 4:00 AM ET. Trump told ABC News the US salvo was "just a love tap" and reaffirmed the ceasefire is in effect.

The market is reading this as a tactical event for three reasons. First, the rhetoric on both sides is calibrated to de-escalate within hours, not days. Second, the Iranian strike claim has not been corroborated by independent satellite imagery, and the Iranian government's own Press TV walked back the situation by Friday morning Tehran time. Third, the 14-point memorandum is still an active diplomatic line through Pakistan, with both sides communicating that the Hormuz incident does not derail it.

We are at 48% probability of a signed memo by next Friday's NY close, down from 62% yesterday morning. Three reasons we lower the number. First, the Hormuz incident eliminates the option of a Friday or weekend signing window, which was the cleanest path. Second, the IRGC's signaling on the nuclear moratorium clause was already ambiguous, and a kinetic event between US Navy assets and Iranian forces gives the IRGC an internal pretext to walk back language. Third, the GCC has not yet been brought in formally, and the UAE airspace dynamic from earlier this week creates a seam that the IRGC can exploit. The market is roughly at 60%, and we are below it.

The base case is the memo is signed in some form by the close of the Trump-Xi summit on May 15, but it is contingent rather than binding. Brent at $101.20 is repricing the tail back into the curve, but the WTI/Brent spread compression has not unwound. The structurally bullish read for energy remains the OPEC+ ministerial on May 21, where the production response to the Fujairah strike is the read.

**Three catalysts we expect today:**
- White House readout on Iranian response, expected 11:00 AM to 1:00 PM ET, tone matters more than substance
- Pakistani Foreign Ministry briefing, expected late-afternoon ET
- Iranian state media commentary on the Hormuz incident, watch for IRGC commander statements
