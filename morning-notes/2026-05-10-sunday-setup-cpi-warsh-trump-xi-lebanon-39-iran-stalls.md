Sunday morning, 9:30 AM ET. The week ahead is the highest-stakes overlay of the second quarter, and the weekend tape did nothing to soften the binary risk: Israel ran the most intense night of strikes against Lebanon since the April ceasefire framework, killing at least 39 people across the south on Saturday with the IDF claiming hits on more than 85 Hezbollah infrastructure sites, while in Washington the Qatari prime minister departed Miami after a Sunday morning huddle with senior White House officials, reporting back to Doha that the gap on the Iran 14-point memo is now narrowest on the nuclear moratorium clause and widest on the Strait of Hormuz governance language. Tehran's Foreign Ministry restated late Saturday that the proposal is still under review and that any agreement must be "fair and comprehensive," which is the same formulation Iran used Wednesday and Thursday, and which the Witkoff and Kushner channel reads as a tactical stall to bleed Trump's Beijing summit window. The new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei's message read on state television Sunday morning re-endorsed "death to America" chants from a workers' rally in Tehran, which is consistent with the public posture but not the back channel signal. Brent settled Friday at $101.29 and gapped roughly $0.40 lower in early Sunday electronic on the Qatari PM headlines before drifting back to $101.20 area; WTI sits around $97.60. June S&P 500 e-mini futures opened the Sunday evening session essentially flat (+0.03%) with Nasdaq 100 e-mini futures down 0.01%, with the cash market having closed Friday at fresh records on the S&P (7,398.93) and Nasdaq Composite (26,247) for a sixth straight weekly gain. The 10Y closed Friday at 4.38% and the 2Y at 3.90%, with the front end pricing 1.1 cuts for 2026 versus the 1.4 it priced two weeks ago. Bitcoin sits at $80,702 (+0.4% on the day, dominance up to 60.4%), gold near $4,712, DXY at 98.21, and the VIX at 16.74. The information sequence into Monday open is heavy and front-loaded: China April CPI and PPI overnight Sunday, Senate cloture on the Warsh Fed Chair nomination at 5:30 PM Monday, Cleveland Energy and Mosaic prints Monday alongside Existing Home Sales at 10:00 AM, then April CPI Tuesday 8:30 AM with Cleveland Nowcast at 3.5% versus 3.0% headline consensus, PPI Wednesday, Cisco AMC Wednesday, Walmart and Applied Materials Thursday before the Trump-Xi summit Day 1 in Beijing, and Friday capping with Powell's term ending and Warsh sworn in by EOD. The book is positioned for a 35% CPI hot tail, a 60% probability the Hormuz kinetic pattern persists irrespective of memo paper progress, and a 55% probability the Trump-Xi summit produces no incremental tariff surprise on day one given the Iran agenda crowding it out.

Three things to internalize before Monday open:

1. **CPI Tuesday is the higher-conviction event than the Trump-Xi summit on Thursday.** The Cleveland Nowcast moved to 3.5% on the Brent move back through $100, a full 50 basis points above the 3.0% headline and 60 basis points above the 2.9% core consensus. The May energy passthrough offset that desks were modeling Friday is already fading on the weekend reports of a roughly 71 square kilometer oil slick from Iran's Kharg Island terminal (about 80,000 barrels spilled since Tuesday per satellite imagery), which signals damaged export infrastructure rather than a clean technical halt. We hold the 2Y June 17 4.10% / 4.30% put spread at 12 ticks entry, mark 14 ticks (+17%), and add 0.05% NAV at the same strikes Sunday afternoon to bring sizing to 0.25% NAV into the print. A 3.1% headline / 3.0% core hot tail (we put it at 35%) flips the front end pricing back toward 0.9 cuts and pulls the 10Y to 4.45%, which drags multiples down even on a hot tape.

2. **The Hormuz "NACHO" regime is now structural, and the memo signing is a paper ceiling, not a kinetic floor.** Saturday's read across is almost entirely bearish for memo probability and bullish for the energy curve. Israel killed 39 in Lebanon, the new Supreme Leader publicly re-endorsed "death to America" from Tehran, the Kharg Island oil slick is widening on satellite, and US blockade vessel disablement count remains at 4 with 57 redirected, with Iranian state media still reporting that the cargo vessel struck overnight Friday into Saturday cost one Iranian sailor's life with 10 wounded. We mark memo probability at 32% Sunday morning, down from 38% Saturday morning, against a market still pricing roughly 52% via the Brent September call skew. We hold the Brent August $95/$108 strangle from Friday at $4.30 entry, mark $4.85 (+13%), and we add the XLE June 19 $99/$108 call spread sizing by 0.05% NAV at $2.40 mark to bring total energy long sizing to 0.55% NAV. The ceiling on the trade is a one-page MoU signed clean by Friday's Trump-Xi handshake, which we put at 28% probability; the floor is a kinetic incident in the Strait that takes Brent through $108, which we put at 38% over the next 10 sessions.

3. **The Trump-Xi summit Thursday and Friday is a tactical event with structural noise.** Treasury Secretary Bessent confirmed Friday that Iran will be a topic in Beijing, which mechanically squeezes the time available on tariffs and rare earths to under half the agenda. Beijing's deliverable list is the predictable set of Boeing aircraft purchases, US agricultural commitments, and the newly-floated "Board of Trade" framework that USTR Greer has been telegraphing. The base case is a "stabilizing visit" with surface-level deliverables and no incremental tariff escalation, which is constructive for the Trump-Xi summit equity beta names (BA, ag complex, AAPL, NKE) but neutral to negative for the rare-earth names that needed a hard concession (MP, USAR, REMX). We hold the BA June 19 $230/$260 call spread at $4.10 net debit, mark $4.65 (+13%), with PT $260 and the stop at $215 close.
