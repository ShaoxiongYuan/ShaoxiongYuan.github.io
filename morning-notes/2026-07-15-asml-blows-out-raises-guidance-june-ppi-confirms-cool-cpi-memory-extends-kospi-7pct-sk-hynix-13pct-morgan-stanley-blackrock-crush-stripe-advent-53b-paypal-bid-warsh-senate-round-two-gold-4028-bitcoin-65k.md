Wednesday, July 15, 2026, written pre-market with US futures modestly higher and the dovish tape from Tuesday carrying through. The setup that looked so fraught 48 hours ago keeps resolving in the bulls' favor. June wholesale inflation cooled for a second straight print, ASML delivered the AI reality check the whole week was waiting on and raised its full-year outlook, the memory complex extended its rebound with a violent revenge rally across Seoul, and Morgan Stanley and BlackRock opened the second leg of bank earnings with blowouts, while dealmaking roared back to life as Stripe and Advent lobbed a 53 billion dollar bid for PayPal. Producer prices fell 0.3 percent in June against expectations for no change, the first monthly decline since last summer, with gasoline down 12 percent doing most of the work and core rising a benign 0.2 percent. It all lands hours before new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh returns to Capitol Hill, this time before the Senate, in his last real public window before the pre-meeting blackout.

## Top Call: Two Cool Prints and an ASML Blowout Give the Bulls the Data, but Oil and Warsh Still Hold the Veto

The one thing to carry into the open is that the disinflation signal is no longer a single data point that could be dismissed as an oil-driven fluke. Tuesday's soft CPI has now been confirmed by a soft PPI, and two prints pointing the same direction is a trend the bears cannot wave away. Layer on ASML, the single most important scheme-relevant earnings of the week, beating and lifting its full-year guidance on very strong bookings, and the morning has both the macro and the micro pointing the same way. Memory is extending Monday's snap-back into a genuine revenge rally, the banks are validating the soft-landing read, and even the animal spirits are back with a mega-cap fintech takeover bid. Our read is that this is the most constructive tape the market has had in two weeks, and it deserves to be treated as more than a relief bounce because the fundamentals underneath it are showing up in hard numbers rather than positioning. The catch is unchanged from yesterday and it is still the whole game. June's disinflation was rented from June's oil crash, and Brent is sitting at a one-month high above 84 dollars because the US reimposed its naval blockade around Hormuz. That is the input that will bleed into July's prints, and it is why Warsh, testifying to the Senate this morning, can still lean either way with full cover. We would respect this rally and stop fading it, but we would not treat the September hike as dead until oil rolls over, because on the data alone the odds are still a coin flip.

## Inflation: A Second Cool Print Turns a Data Point Into a Trend

Start with the confirmation. June producer prices fell 0.3 percent from May, beating the flat consensus and marking the first monthly decline in wholesale inflation since last summer, with the twelve-month rate at 5.5 percent. Nearly two-thirds of the drop came from gasoline, which fell 12 percent, the same energy tailwind that pulled the CPI lower a day earlier. The cleaner signal is in the core. Stripping out food and energy, PPI rose just 0.2 percent against the 0.3 percent expected, and final demand services edged up only 0.2 percent, which tells you the disinflation is not purely an energy illusion at the wholesale level either. Coming 24 hours after a headline CPI that fell 0.4 percent to 3.5 percent, this is the second leg of a two-print disinflation signal, and it has quietly finished off any lingering case for a July rate hike, which now prices near zero.

Our take is that the PPI matters more than its usual second-fiddle status because of what it confirms rather than what it says on its own. One soft inflation print in an oil-distorted month is noise the hawks can rationalize. Two soft prints in a row, with core cooling on both the consumer and producer side, is a signal, and it is the strongest evidence this year that the underlying trend is bending back toward target rather than reaccelerating. The honest caveat is the same one we flagged yesterday. Both prints capture June, when crude was on the floor, and the July data will have to digest a Brent price that has round-tripped back above 84 dollars. So the disinflation is real and confirmed in the rear-view mirror, while the windshield still shows an energy shock the Fed cannot ignore. We think the two cool prints take an imminent hike off the table and buy the market room, but they do not settle the September debate, which now rests almost entirely on where oil goes from here.

## ASML: The Week's Real Verdict Comes in Bullish and Validates the Whole Buildout

This is the print that mattered most, and it delivered. ASML, the sole supplier of the extreme ultraviolet lithography machines that every advanced chip in the world depends on, beat its second-quarter guidance with net sales of 9.3 billion euros and a 54 percent gross margin, helped by stronger-than-expected demand in its installed-base business. More important than the quarter, the company raised its full-year 2026 outlook to sales of 43 to 45 billion euros and gross margin of 54 to 56 percent, and guided the third quarter to 11 to 12 billion euros, well ahead of where the street sat. The language on the call is what should carry the tape. Management described very strong order bookings through the first half, said it is close to having all the orders it needs for 2027, and confirmed a plan to add roughly 30 percent EUV capacity versus this year. The stock rose in pre-market and dragged the semiconductor complex and Nasdaq futures higher with it.

Our take is that this is the single cleanest piece of evidence for the durability of the AI buildout that the market has received, and it could not have arrived at a better moment. For two weeks the bear case has been that the memory and AI trade is a financing-driven, positioning-heavy bubble that Monday's Black Monday exposed. ASML is the pick-and-shovel supplier at the very bottom of the stack, the one company that sees every chipmaker's capital plans before anyone else, and it just told the world that bookings are so strong it is racing to add a third more capacity and has nearly filled its 2027 book already. You cannot fake that with sentiment. It is a hard, forward-looking order number from the chokepoint of the entire industry, and it is a far more credible signal than any sell-side upgrade. Combined with IBM's warning yesterday that clients are diverting budgets to lock up scarce memory, the fundamental picture underneath the memory rout is not weakening, it is tightening. We stay constructive on the memory and semiconductor-equipment names and read ASML as confirmation that Monday was a flush, not a top. TSMC tomorrow is the last box to check, and after this the bar for it to disappoint is higher.

## Memory: Monday's Flush Becomes a Revenge Rally

The overnight action in Asia turned a bounce into a stampede. The Kospi surged roughly 7 percent, so violently that the Korea Exchange tripped a buy-side sidecar and briefly suspended programmatic buy orders, the mirror image of Monday's circuit-breaker crash. SK Hynix jumped about 13 percent, Samsung climbed more than 7 percent on chatter it may raise cash in the US, and SK Square rocketed 20 percent. The move had a fundamental catalyst beyond the CPI relief. Barclays initiated coverage of SK Hynix's US-listed shares at overweight with a 330 dollar target, giving American investors an analyst anchor for the memory rerating just days after the record IPO. The bid carried across the region, with the Nikkei up 0.9 percent and the whole AI-memory chain firmer.

Our take is that this is exactly the shape a positioning flush is supposed to take on the way back. Monday was a record 15 percent SK Hynix collapse driven by ADR-arbitrage and forced deleveraging, not a demand verdict, and the round trip to a 13 percent gain and a buy-side circuit breaker in three sessions confirms that read. What makes this rebound more durable than a dead-cat bounce is that it is now backed by fundamentals rather than just dip-buying, ASML's bookings and IBM's scramble on the supply side, and a fresh sell-side overweight on the demand side. The one flag we would raise is that a market that trips circuit breakers in both directions inside a single week is not a calm one, and the volatility itself argues for respecting the trend without chasing the gap. But the thesis that scarce memory is the tightest link in the AI chain is intact and, if anything, better supported this morning than it was before Monday's scare.

## Banks Round Two: Morgan Stanley and BlackRock Blow the Doors Off

The second wave of bank earnings extended the strong open. Morgan Stanley posted record quarterly revenue and profit, with net income up 58 percent to 5.58 billion dollars and EPS of 3.46 dollars, crushing the 2.94 dollar consensus, powered by a 69 percent surge in equities trading revenue that speaks directly to how active and risk-on the quarter was. BlackRock delivered adjusted EPS of 13.91 dollars against a 12.59 dollar estimate and rose more than 4.5 percent, as the market rally lifted assets under management to fresh highs. Outside the financials, Johnson and Johnson beat with adjusted EPS of 2.90 dollars on 25.31 billion in revenue, a steady read on the defensive side of the tape.

Our take is that Morgan Stanley's 69 percent jump in equities trading is the tell of the morning for anyone worried the AI-led index masks a weak economy underneath. That is not a number you print in a fragile market, it is what a bank earns when clients are trading aggressively and capital is moving, and it dovetails with the animal spirits showing up in the PayPal bid. Following JPMorgan's blowout yesterday, the banks are now two-for-two on validating the soft-landing case, and BlackRock's asset-growth beat says the wealth effect from record equity prices is real and compounding. The read across the group is that the real economy under the narrow index is not just holding, it is accelerating, which is the reassurance a market rattled by Monday needed most. The one thing to watch remains net interest margin and any commentary on credit quality, but on the market-sensitive lines the results are unambiguously strong.

## M&A Returns: Stripe and Advent's $53B PayPal Bid Signals the Animal Spirits Are Back

The clearest sign that risk appetite has flipped is a blockbuster takeover bid. Stripe, the private payments giant, and private-equity firm Advent International offered to buy PayPal for more than 53 billion dollars, or 60.50 dollars a share, roughly 28 percent above Tuesday's close, backed by about 50 billion dollars in committed bank financing. PayPal jumped 18 to 20 percent in pre-market. The proposal calls for Stripe and Advent to share ownership equally, with no plan to break up the company, and follows an initial approach in April that has not yet drawn a formal response from PayPal's board.

Our take is that the deal matters as much for what it signals as for its own merits. A 50 billion dollar committed financing package for a take-private of this size tells you the credit markets are wide open and sponsors are willing to write enormous checks again, which is a direct byproduct of the softer rate outlook the last two prints delivered. It is also a strategically aggressive move by Stripe to fold in PayPal's brand and merchant base rather than keep grinding market share the hard way. Whether PayPal's board engages is a separate question, and a 28 percent premium may not be enough to close a deal the company has so far ignored, but the mere existence of a bid this large on a morning like this is a sentiment marker. When mega-cap M&A returns, it usually confirms that the financing window and the risk appetite have both reopened.

## Oil and Iran: The Blockade Keeps the War Premium Sticky

The offset to all the good news is unchanged and it is crude. Brent is holding a one-month high near 84.73 dollars and WTI is around 80 dollars, after the US reimposed its naval blockade of Iranian ports around the Strait of Hormuz and struck dozens of coastal military targets in a seven-hour operation. Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed attacks on two tankers crossing the strait without active tracking signals, and the UAE's ADNOC confirmed two vessels were hit. The one piece of de-escalation is that President Trump abandoned his threatened 20 percent fee on Hormuz cargo, saying lost revenue would be offset by future Gulf investment into the US, which took the most inflationary single measure off the table even as the blockade keeps the premium in place.

Our take is that dropping the 20 percent fee is a genuine positive at the margin because a transit tax would have fed straight into shipping costs and downstream prices, whereas a blockade is a supply-risk premium that the well-supplied paper market can partly absorb. But the direction of travel is still wrong for disinflation. Oil above 84 dollars on Brent is the single cleanest reason to distrust the durability of the June inflation prints, because it is the input that reverses them in July. The cross-asset tells are consistent with a market treating Hormuz as a rates-and-inflation story rather than a pure fear trade, with gold choppy rather than surging and the front end rallying on the data rather than on a flight to safety. As long as the strait stays physically open, this is a premium and not a supply loss, but it is enough to keep Warsh's hawkish option alive.

## Macro: Warsh Gets the Last Word Before the Blackout

Everything funnels to the same place, the new Fed chair, who walks to the Senate microphone this morning with two cool inflation prints behind him and an oil spike pulling the other way. Kevin Warsh testifies before the Senate Banking Committee today, following Tuesday's House appearance, in one of the last substantive public windows before the FOMC's communications blackout ahead of the July 28 to 29 meeting. Yesterday he struck a deliberately non-committal tone, telling the House the latest improvement on inflation is not mission accomplished, language that gives him room to move in either direction. Market pricing now implies roughly a 44 percent probability of a September hike, down from the mid-70s before Monday's panic and easing further as the second cool print landed. The policy rate is held at 3.5 to 3.75 percent.

Our take is that Warsh's Senate session is the last real catalyst of the week and it will set the tone into the blackout. The two prints give him cover to sound patient, and if he leans on the confirmed disinflation to signal a sustained hold, the memory and small-cap bounce has room to run into month-end. If he instead points at oil above 84 dollars and reminds the Senate that a 3.5 percent headline is still well above target, he can cap this morning's relief and keep the 30-year pinned above 5 percent. Our lean remains that a brand-new chair building anti-inflation credibility is more likely to preserve optionality than to declare victory, which is why the honest base case is a hold with a hawkish bias rather than a green light. The data has done the bulls a favor this week. Whether Warsh lets them keep it is the one thing still unresolved.

ASML's beat, raised guidance and 30 percent capacity expansion validated the buildout from the industry's chokepoint, and the memory complex extended its rebound with SK Hynix up 13 percent and a 7 percent Kospi revenge surge that tripped a buy-side sidecar. With IBM's supply-scramble warning yesterday and a fresh Barclays overweight on SK Hynix, both the supply and demand sides of the memory thesis firmed. TSMC tomorrow is the last check.

**Financials:** Two-for-two on the soft-landing read. Morgan Stanley posted record revenue and profit on a 69 percent equities-trading surge, BlackRock beat on record assets and rose over 4.5 percent, and both followed JPMorgan's blowout. The group is validating an accelerating real economy under the narrow index, with net interest margin and credit commentary the only things left to scrutinize.

**Energy:** Crude is holding a one-month high, Brent near 84.73 and WTI around 80, on the reimposed Hormuz blockade even after Trump dropped the 20 percent transit fee. Energy equities stay bid and refiners have been among the year's biggest winners, but the more important signal is the inflation impulse the move feeds into future prints, which is the main threat to the disinflation story the June data just told.

**Fintech and Payments:** M&A is back. The Stripe-Advent 53 billion dollar bid for PayPal, backed by 50 billion in committed financing, sent the stock up nearly 20 percent and signaled that the credit window and sponsor risk appetite have reopened on the softer rate outlook. Watch for read-through into other beaten-down payments and fintech names as a re-rating candidate list.

## Geopolitical Tracker

**US-Iran:** Still the dominant offset to the cool data. The US reimposed its naval blockade of Iranian ports around Hormuz and struck dozens of coastal targets, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard hit two tankers in the strait, per ADNOC. The de-escalation is that Trump dropped the 20 percent cargo fee, removing the most inflationary measure, but oil holding above 84 keeps a live inflation tail open even after two soft prints.

**Ukraine-Russia:** The loose supply counterweight remains in place. Record Russian crude exports are one reason the global oil balance stays long on paper even with Hormuz contested, which caps how far the current premium can run absent a physical closure of the strait.

**Taiwan Strait:** Back off the boil with the memory rout fully reversed. The regional supply-chain pressure that flared Monday has eased as Seoul stages its revenge rally, though TSMC's print tomorrow keeps the region squarely on the screen.

## Notable Earnings

The scheme-relevant print of the morning is ASML, and it is the one that matters most for the entire AI and memory trade. As the sole maker of the EUV lithography machines that light every advanced chip into existence, ASML sees the industry's capital plans before anyone, and it beat with 9.3 billion euros in Q2 sales, raised full-year guidance to 43 to 45 billion euros, and told the market it is racing to add 30 percent more EUV capacity while nearly sold out on 2027 orders. That is the durability signal the bears have been demanding, delivered from the chokepoint of the supply chain and impossible to dismiss as sentiment. On top of it, Morgan Stanley posted record revenue and profit with equities trading up 69 percent, BlackRock crushed on record assets, and Johnson and Johnson beat on the defensive side, a clean sweep across cyclical and defensive earnings.

Our quick take is that the earnings and the macro are finally telling one coherent story. The last verdict comes tomorrow with TSMC and Netflix, and after ASML the setup favors a beat, but a heavy tape of AI capex all reporting into a market that just tripped circuit breakers in both directions means the bar for cleanliness is high. For now the fundamentals are validating the bounce rather than fading it.
