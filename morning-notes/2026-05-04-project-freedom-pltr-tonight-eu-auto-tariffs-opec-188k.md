Monday pre-market, 7:00 AM ET. The weekend delivered exactly the kind of headline reversal that breaks single-direction positioning. Trump announced "Project Freedom" Sunday afternoon, an open-ended US Navy operation to guide civilian ships out of the Strait of Hormuz, and CENTCOM confirmed at 5:30 AM ET that two US-flagged merchant vessels had successfully transited. Brent gapped to $114 in early Asia trading on initial Iranian press claims of a missile strike on a US patrol boat, then unwound the move once the US flatly denied the report and the first transits cleared. As of writing, Brent sits at $108.40, WTI at $103.10, both down roughly 2.5% from Friday's close and 5% off the overnight highs. S&P futures are 4 handles lower at 7,253.75. Nasdaq futures are 27 points higher. The tape is split, oil is the swing factor, and PLTR after the bell is the binary.

The geopolitical backdrop has shifted in a meaningful way over the weekend, even if the cracking ceasefire in Lebanon has not. Iran made its 14-point proposal public Saturday rather than negotiating in private through Pakistan, which forces the US into a counter-offer in a public forum. The terms are predictable on the headline (lift sanctions, end the blockade, withdraw forces, formal ceasefire within 30 days, nuclear track parked separately) but the act of publishing them is itself a de-escalation signal because Tehran wants to be seen offering rather than refusing. Trump told reporters on Air Force One Sunday that he "can't imagine" the latest version is acceptable, but his deputies are framing the talks as "very positive." That is the same pattern that preceded the Lebanon ceasefire framework last November. We read the tape as a 55/45 lean toward formal ceasefire by the May 14 to 15 Trump-Xi summit, which would force a $15 to $20 unwind in front-month Brent.

The other weekend story for the energy book is OPEC+. The Saudi-led seven held their first ministerial without the UAE on Sunday and agreed to add 188,000 barrels per day from June. The number itself is small, the choreography is the signal. With Brent at $108 and Hormuz finally seeing flow, an output add reads as a vote for normalization rather than a defense of price. The UAE's empty chair is the bigger structural read. The cartel just lost 2.9 mbpd of swing capacity and the remaining members chose to add rather than hold. That tells you the Saudis prefer market share over price discipline once the war premium fades, which is consistent with our medium-term Brent target range of $85 to $95 once ceasefire is signed.

Three things into Monday:

1. **PLTR is the marquee print of the week, after the close.** Consensus sits at $0.28 EPS on $1.54B in revenue (revenue +74% y/y). Polymarket is implying a 96% probability of an EPS beat. Options are pricing a ~10.06% straddle on Monday, slightly tighter than the four-quarter realized average of 10.5%. The stock is down 20% YTD and sits 31% below its November all-time high of $207.18, which means positioning is materially cleaner than any quarter in the last 18 months. Two things matter: US commercial growth versus the +94% consensus, and whether the full-year guide gets a meaningful raise. We hold the underlying long into the print at 60% of target weight and overlay a long-dated June $200 call into any 5%+ post-print weakness.

2. **EU auto tariffs at 25% just landed, and European autos are already down 1.6% in early dealings.** Trump's Friday announcement to lift the EU vehicle tariff from 15% to 25% takes effect this week. Mercedes, BMW, and Volkswagen import the largest share of US-sold inventory from European plants. The read-through to the US OEMs (F, GM) is mixed because Detroit also sources from Europe but has more domestic production on the ramp. The cleaner trade is short the EU-domiciled OEMs through ADRs (MBG.DE, BMW.DE, VOW3.DE) into any rally on Trump-Xi summit headlines.

3. **The bond market has rallied a little, but the macro setup has not changed.** 10Y back to 4.43% and 30Y to 4.96% over the weekend on the Hormuz transits and Iran proposal headlines, but auctions return this week with 10Y on Wednesday and 30Y on Thursday. CPI prints next Wednesday May 13 and the Cleveland Nowcast still has April at 3.6% headline. With Brent in the $105 to $110 range through April, the energy passthrough is real. We stay structurally short duration via TLT put spreads, sized smaller into the headline-driven rally.

## Project Freedom and the Hormuz Tape

This is the new market-moving variable for the next two weeks. Trump's announcement Sunday (Truth Social, 4:12 PM ET) framed the operation as "humanitarian" and limited to escorting flagged civilian ships out of the Gulf. CENTCOM later clarified the force package: guided-missile destroyers, "over 100 land and sea-based aircraft," drones, and roughly 15,000 service members in the regional package. The deployed Navy will not enter the strait itself. The escort happens at the edge of the contested waterway, with merchant vessels routed through with US air cover.

The math on stranded vessels: the IMO put the figure at roughly 2,000 ships and 20,000 seafarers as of last week, including LPG/LNG carriers, crude tankers, and bulk dry cargo. If Project Freedom successfully clears even 30% of that backlog over the next two weeks, the spot oil market normalizes very quickly and the front-month dated Brent flips from backwardation to contango.

Iran's response so far has been verbal rather than kinetic. Ebrahim Azizi (head of the parliament's National Security Commission) called the operation a violation of the April 8 ceasefire framework. The Khatam al-Anbiya commander threatened attacks on any foreign forces "attempting to approach or enter" the Strait. The IRGC released a statement that any "aggressive US army" presence "will be attacked." This is rhetoric we have heard for sixty days. The kinetic threshold is whether Iran fires on a US-escorted civilian vessel. If they do, the ceasefire is functionally dead and Brent prints $130. If they do not (our base case at 65%), Hormuz volumes normalize over four to six weeks and Brent settles in the $90s.

**Two false starts overnight that did not survive an hour:**
- 2:14 AM ET: Iranian press wire claims a missile strike on a US patrol boat in the Gulf. US Navy denies within the hour. Brent rallies to $114 then unwinds.
- 4:08 AM ET: Iranian state TV reports a US warship "turned back" from the entrance to Hormuz. CENTCOM confirms transit ongoing. Brent sells off another 80 cents.

Both episodes are examples of the kind of two-way headline risk that will dominate price discovery this week. Position with that in mind. Tight stops on directional oil exposure, larger stops on optionality.
