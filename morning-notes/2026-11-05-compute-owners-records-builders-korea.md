Thursday, November 5, 2026, written at 8:15 a.m. Eastern. The Dow had its best day since April 2025 yesterday, rising 1,569 points or 3.03% to 53,356.12, and the Technology sector finished the session down one basis point. Overnight the Kospi fell 5.28% and tripped a sell side curb, Qualcomm is indicated 10.6% lower, and Nasdaq 100 futures are off 0.87% against Dow futures up 0.67%. Jobless claims land at 8:30 a.m. and payrolls are tomorrow, so this is a reading of the tape before the week's real data.

## Top Call: The Build Got Separated From the Builders

Yesterday's session is worth describing precisely, because the headline number hides what actually happened. The S&P 500 rose 1.58% to 7,688.62 and the equal weight version rose 1.86%. Consumer Discretionary gained 4.26%, Financials 3.51%, Communication Services 3.47% and Staples 3.36%. Technology was flat. Industrials fell 1.09% and Energy fell 4.74%.

Inside that flat Technology line, Apple rose more than 5% to a record, Microsoft and Amazon each added more than 6% and Alphabet 5.8%. Against them, Nvidia fell 3.17%, Micron fell 7.00% and CoreWeave fell 9.90%. The worst of it was not in technology at all. Everus Construction closed down 29.29%, Sterling Infrastructure down 21.46%, Vertiv down roughly 13% and GE Vernova down somewhere between 9.3% and 11.5% depending on the source. Vertiv had just reported third quarter sales up 61% and raised its full year forecast.

So the market did not sell artificial intelligence yesterday. It bought the four companies that already own compute, have cash flow and need no new permits, and it sold every company whose revenue depends on new capacity being sited, powered and built. The transmission is Gina Hinojosa's pledged moratorium on new Texas data centre approvals, which we flagged yesterday, and UBS has now put a number on it: roughly 30% of expected United States data centre capacity additions in 2028 are exposed to construction pauses. Texas alone is around 27% of installed gigawatts.

This is a cleaner distinction than it first looks, and it is the one to carry forward. A hyperscaler with existing sites and a signed power contract is long an asset that just became harder to reproduce. A construction firm, a power equipment maker or a neocloud renting out capacity it has not finished building is short that same scarcity. Both can be true in the same tape, and yesterday both were, which is why the index went up 1.58% while the supply chain lost a fifth of its value.

The second order consequence showed up overnight, and it is the part the American session has not yet priced. Memory sits at the far end of that supply chain. The entire argument for paying up for SK hynix and Samsung is an order book sold out through 2029, and that order book is a claim on racks that do not exist yet. Korea understood this immediately. The Kospi fell 5.28% to 5,383.80, triggered a sell side sidecar at 10:16 local time, and saw SK hynix close down 9.46% at 1,260,000 won and Samsung down 7.57% at 201,500 won. Those two are roughly half the index. Yahoo calculates that chip and memory stocks have now lost around $3.7 trillion of market value since October 6.

## Qualcomm: The Memory Bill Shows Up in Revenue

| Metric | Consensus | Actual | Result |
|--------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Revenue | ~$10.4bn | $10.32bn, down about 8% | Light |
| Adjusted EPS | $2.16 | $2.17 | In line |
| Handset revenue | $6.96bn a year ago | $5.14bn, down 26% | Miss |
| Q1 revenue guide | $10.48bn | $10.4bn to $11.2bn | In line |
| Q1 adjusted EPS guide | $2.32 | $2.15 to $2.35 | Miss |

**Our take**: this is the most important print of the week and it has nothing to do with the headline beat. Handset revenue fell from $6.96 billion to $5.14 billion in a year. Qualcomm did not lose share and phones did not stop selling. Memory makers reallocated capacity toward high bandwidth memory for artificial intelligence, phone memory got scarce and expensive, and handset manufacturers responded by cutting build plans. That is the loop closing. For three sessions we have described the memory shortage as a cost of goods problem for Apple and a guidance problem for Intel and AMD. Qualcomm is the first company to show it as a volume problem, which is a different and worse thing, because a cost can be passed through and a unit that was never built cannot be recovered. At least eight brokers cut targets overnight. Arm fell about 2% on cautious guidance for the same reason, and GlobalFoundries was roughly flat, which makes it the best of the three almost by default.

The uncomfortable implication for the memory complex is that its own pricing power is eating the demand base underneath it. Phone and personal computer memory is not a rounding error in DRAM and NAND volumes. If handset output keeps falling, the sold out book through 2029 relies entirely on data centre racks arriving on schedule, which is exactly the assumption the Texas result just put in question. That is the argument Korea traded this morning, and it is a better argument than the one the market was making two weeks ago about positioning.

## Oil Fell 8.67% and the Bank of England Hiked Anyway

Brent closed down 8.67% at $94.62 on Wednesday and WTI down 8.29% at $88.12, both at two month lows and both below $100 for the first time since late August. The proximate cause was political rather than physical. Vice President Vance attributed the midterm losses to the Iran war, which the market read as a weakened administration now having a domestic incentive to settle. Qatar continues to broker, Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei called the American reply to Tehran's Hormuz plan unconvincing, and Trump separately confirmed strikes on Qeshm Island. Iranian officials say the strait can reopen within two days if sanctions relief and the end of the naval blockade are delivered. Crude has bounced this morning, with WTI around $89.58 and Brent near $96.

Four hours before that bounce, the Bank of England raised Bank Rate 25 basis points to 4.00%, its first increase since 2023, on an 8-1 vote with Catherine Mann dissenting for 50. The Bank sees inflation reaching 5% by mid-2027. Andrew Bailey said the hike is not the start of a long series but that it does not look like the last one unless the Middle East conflict is resolved.

Read those two paragraphs together. Bailey has written an oil option directly into his reaction function and published the strike. If the Qatari process delivers, the December 17 meeting is dead and the market has not priced that. If it fails, the Bank hikes again into an economy where the ten year gilt yields 5.13%. The FTSE 100 is up around 1% and at a three month high, which is a currency and energy weighted index responding to a weaker pound and cheaper crude rather than to the decision itself. The Stoxx 600 is up about 0.48% after closing 1.82% higher at 657.40 on Wednesday, the DAX is roughly flat after a 2.2% gain to 26,437.67, and the Swiss market index crossed 15,000 for the first time.

## Rates, Gold and a Central Bank That Just Got Its Cover Back

The curve rallied on Wednesday but less than the equity move implied: ten year down about 6 basis points to 4.89%, thirty year down 5 to 5.28%, two year down only 3 to 4.59% and now near 4.56%. A three basis point move at the front end on a 3% Dow day tells you the rates market treated the election as a fiscal and political story rather than a monetary one. December hike pricing sits around 77%, essentially unchanged.

That is probably too complacent in one specific respect. Democratic control of the Senate hands the Banking Committee to Elizabeth Warren and effectively ends the administration's ability to confirm loyalist appointments to the Board of Governors. Kevin Warsh, confirmed with exactly one Democratic vote, now chairs a committee whose independence is politically protected in a way it was not on Monday. A Fed that is harder to capture is a Fed that is freer to hike, and the front end has not moved for it.

Gold agrees. It rose 3.29% on Wednesday to $4,070 and is up another 1.44% this morning to $4,131, a two week high, with silver above $52. Bitcoin is at $79,672, up 1.81%, having recovered from roughly $75,600 on Tuesday. We flagged gold's late October collapse as forced selling rather than a change of view and said the tell would be whether it stabilised. It has now fully retraced that liquidation in four sessions. The dollar is at 101.38 after closing 101.53. Note also that the Treasury borrowed a record $839 billion in a single week, which is the supply side of the same trade.

Challenger job cuts for October came in at 43,994, up slightly on September but down 71% on the year, with the first ten months the lowest since 2022. Initial claims are due at 8:30 a.m. against a prior 194,000, and third quarter preliminary productivity is expected at 3.0% after 1.4%. Real third quarter output grew at a 3.4% annual rate and nominal growth was around 6%, which is a difficult backdrop in which to argue for cuts.

## Japan: Toyota Up 17%, Everything Touching AI Down 17%

The Nikkei fell 1.66% to a three month low while the Topix rose, and the composition of that gap is the most informative thing in Asia this morning. Toyota rose 16.68% to 3,533 yen, its largest single day move since 1987, after July to September operating profit climbed 34% to 1.13 trillion yen against a 992 billion yen consensus, a full year guide raised roughly 15% to 3.9 trillion yen, and the buyback ceiling lifted to 1.3 trillion yen from 1.0 trillion. Hybrid demand in the United States and a weak yen at 157.90 did most of the work, and China sales still fell 12%.

Against the largest company in the index gaining almost 17%, the Nikkei still fell, because Furukawa Electric lost 17.06%, Murata 16.58% and Ibiden 16.31%. Those three are optical cable, passive components and the advanced substrates that go underneath every artificial intelligence accelerator. Advantest, Tokyo Electron and SoftBank were the other main drags. This is the same split as the American tape, drawn even more sharply: the old economy cash machine up 17%, the artificial intelligence supply chain down 17%, in one index on one day.

## China Goes the Other Way

The Hang Seng rose 3.29% to 24,705.29 led by Chinese chipmakers, the CSI 300 added 0.29% to 4,113.41 and the Shanghai Composite 0.10% to 3,689.91. Domestic semiconductor names rallied on continued Beijing support measures while Korean and Japanese chip shares were being liquidated.

That divergence is not a coincidence and it deserves more attention than a single session normally earns. Demand for Chinese domestic chips is set by industrial policy and import substitution, not by whether a county commission in Texas issues a permit. If the constraint on the American build is now physical and political rather than financial, then the one semiconductor complex in the world whose demand curve is written by a government rather than by a permitting queue has just become structurally less correlated to the thing everyone is selling. This is the ninth session this month in which Hong Kong and the mainland have traded against the regional direction.

## The Record

One test was set yesterday and it resolved in the direction we wrote, though with a caveat worth stating.

We said to watch whether the neocloud group stayed down for a second session once the election headline flow cleared, and that a failure to recover would mean the market had started pricing a physical constraint on the build rather than reacting to a result. CoreWeave did not recover. It closed Wednesday down 9.90%, worse than the 7% it was indicated at when we wrote, and it is lower again pre-market alongside Nebius. The builders fell considerably harder than the neoclouds did, which we did not anticipate and which arguably makes the point better than the test we chose. The caveat is that this morning's declines in that group are small, between 1% and 2%, so the second session has confirmed direction rather than conviction. We are treating the test as passed and the conclusion as provisional.

We also want to claim one thing and concede another. The claim is Monday's framing that the memory bill had become the standing tax on every consumer silicon line in the market. Qualcomm's handset revenue falling 26% on the year is that sentence in its most extreme form so far. The concession is that we have spent three weeks treating the memory producers as the beneficiaries of that tax, and we never asked what happens to their own volumes when the devices stop being built. Korea asked this morning and answered it with a 5.28% decline. That was available to us on Monday and we missed it.

Two tests for tomorrow. First, whether the construction and power equipment names, Vertiv, GE Vernova, Everus and Sterling, bounce today while the neoclouds stay down. If they separate, the market is distinguishing physical construction risk from compute rental credit risk, and the two should be traded differently from here. If they fall together again, it is one trade and it is about permits. Second, payrolls tomorrow against a consensus near 55,000. If the print clears that and December hike odds do not move back above 85%, then the front end is pricing a political constraint on the Fed that the Senate result has just removed, and the two year is wrong.
