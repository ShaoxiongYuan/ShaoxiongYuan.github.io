## Two Chokepoints: Why Oil Is Headed to $120+

The single most important development of the weekend is not any individual event. It is that the war now threatens two of the world's three critical maritime energy chokepoints simultaneously:

- **Strait of Hormuz (blocked since Day 1):** 20% of global oil supply, 25% of LNG. IRGC Navy continues to turn away ships. Three container ships rejected Friday. 20,000 seafarers stranded. Saudi Arabia has been diverting 4.6 million bbl/day through the East-West Pipeline to its Red Sea port of Yanbu, a workaround, not a solution.
- **Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea (newly threatened):** 10% of global maritime trade, 40% of container traffic. The Houthis' missile strikes on Israel signal they are willing to expand operations. In 2024, Houthi Red Sea attacks forced major shipping lines (Maersk, Hapag-Lloyd, CMA CGM, MSC) to reroute via the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10–14 days and $1M+ per voyage. If this resumes, it compounds the Hormuz disruption.
- **The Yanbu vulnerability:** Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline hit 7M bbl/day on March 28, diverting Persian Gulf oil to Yanbu on the Red Sea. But Yanbu is within Houthi missile range. A drone already fell on the Samref refinery at Yanbu, and a ballistic missile was intercepted nearby. If the Houthis attack Yanbu systematically, the bypass fails and global oil supply loses both exit routes simultaneously.

> **If Both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb Are Disrupted, There Is No Alternative Route for Persian Gulf Oil**
>
> The Hormuz-to-Yanbu-to-Red-Sea pipeline was the market's comfort blanket. That blanket is now on fire. Analysts project Brent $150+ within weeks if Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping resume in earnest. Even the threat is worth $5–10/bbl of risk premium. Brent at $112 this morning is pricing the Houthi entry but not a full Red Sea disruption. The tail risk is extreme.

## Houthis Enter the War

Yemen's Houthi rebels launched their first strikes on Israel since the war began on February 28: two ballistic missiles targeting Beersheba, a cruise missile intercepted en route, and a drone aimed at Eilat. All were neutralized. No casualties reported. But the strategic consequences go well beyond the physical damage. The Houthis now operate on the second active maritime chokepoint of this war, and their track record in 2024 shows they are willing and capable of disrupting global shipping at scale. If they resume attacks on commercial vessels transiting the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, 10% of global maritime trade faces rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10–14 days and $1M+ per voyage.

## Pentagon Ground Plans: What Kharg Island Raids Would Mean

The Washington Post reports the Pentagon is planning limited ground operations in Iran. Here is what that means in practice:

- **What it is:** Limited raids by special operations and 82nd Airborne on specific targets, notably Kharg Island (90% of Iran's oil exports), coastal Hormuz facilities, and possibly IRGC Navy bases. These would be in-and-out operations to damage Iran's ability to control the Strait and export oil. Duration: days to weeks, not months.
- **What it isn't:** A full-scale ground invasion. No one is talking about occupying Tehran or regime change. The 2,500 Marines and airborne units are structured for rapid-strike operations, not sustained ground war.
- **Market implication:** A Kharg Island raid would temporarily remove 1.5–2M bbl/day of Iranian exports from the market, but it would also demonstrate the US is willing to escalate beyond air strikes. This is simultaneously bearish (supply destruction) and bullish (credible military pressure increases ceasefire probability). The net effect on oil depends on timing relative to Islamabad talks.
- **The Iran response:** Ghalibaf's "set on fire" rhetoric is alarming but typical. Iran's actual capability to repel US special operations forces is limited. The real risk is asymmetric retaliation, such as Hezbollah attacks on US bases in Iraq/Syria, Houthi escalation in the Red Sea, or cyber attacks on Gulf infrastructure.

## Islamabad Four-Nation Summit: The Most Coordinated Peace Effort Yet

This is real. Four regional powers do not convene a summit over a head fake.

## Bottom Line

### The Race Between War and Peace Is Accelerating.

The war just got bigger. The Houthis opened a new front. The Pentagon is positioning ground forces. Israel is striking nuclear sites. Oil is at $112 and headed higher. Two of three global maritime energy chokepoints are threatened simultaneously.

But the diplomacy also got bigger. Four regional powers convened in Islamabad, the most coordinated peace effort of the war. Pakistan says both sides trust them. Trump says talks are "going very well." The April 6 deadline creates urgency. If face-to-face talks happen this week, the entire war trade unwinds violently in the bullish direction.

This is the paradox of the moment: the military escalation and the diplomatic effort are both intensifying simultaneously. The ground ops prep might be the leverage that makes Islamabad talks work. Or it might trigger Iranian retaliation that kills the talks. We don't know which. The market doesn't either, which is why the VIX is at 27 and oil is at $112.
