Monday, August 3, 2026. The single largest input to this market for four months was removed from the screen over the weekend, and it was removed by a sentence rather than by a barrel. President Trump said on Air Force One on Sunday that he had called off what he described as the biggest attack since World War II against Iran, at the request of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar and Tehran itself, and that negotiators would sit down this morning on a deal covering the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian denuclearisation. Brent for October fell as much as 7.3 percent to trade below $84, WTI slipped under $81, and the equity futures complex sorted itself in a very particular order: Dow futures up 676 points or 1.28 percent, Russell 2000 futures up 0.99 percent, S&P 500 futures up 0.60 percent, and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.19 percent. That ordering is the whole story of the morning. This is not a growth rally. It is the unwinding of an energy tax.

## Top Call: The War Premium Left, and the Bond Market Collects First

Brent rose roughly 25 percent in July, its strongest month since March. That move was doing real work inside the September Fed debate. Three FOMC members dissented at the July 28 to 29 meeting in favour of an immediate hike, the committee held at 3.50 to 3.75 percent, and market-implied odds of a September increase have swung between 55 and 80 percent for three weeks almost entirely on the oil complex rather than on growth, which undershot at 1.5 percent in the second quarter. Take seven percent out of Brent in one session and you have removed the most visible source of the second-half inflation impulse that the hawks were pointing at.

So the first-order read is that this is a rates event dressed as a geopolitical one. Treasuries are bid across the curve, the dollar index sits at 99.72, and the equity expression is showing up in exactly the places that were paying the energy tax: transport, travel, industrials, small caps and the Dow. The complication is the long end. The 30-year sits around 5.25 percent, within a few basis points of a 19-year high, and it has now failed to confirm three separate equity recoveries in eleven sessions. If the front end rallies on a lower hike probability while the 30-year stays where it is, the curve steepens for term-premium reasons, which is a worse outcome for long-duration equity than a parallel drop in yields would be. Watch the 30-year rather than the 2-year for whether this is a regime change or a one-day relief trade. Written at 9:15am Eastern before the opening bell, so a 7 percent move in crude and a 1.28 percent print in Dow futures can both look materially different by ten o'clock, particularly with ISM landing inside the first half hour of trading.

## What Was Actually Agreed, and What Was Not

Nothing was agreed. That distinction is worth holding onto while the tape celebrates. According to a regional official involved in the mediation, the proposal calls for both sides to return to the table and continue working the issues that derailed the previous round, including reopening Hormuz and halting attacks across the region, with the United States ending its naval blockade and allowing Tehran to export oil again. Those are the terms of an agenda, not the terms of a settlement.

Within hours of Trump calling a deal imminent, Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei once again publicly rejected the premise of direct negotiations with Washington. This is not the first declared pause of the campaign, and on the previous occasions strikes resumed within about a week. The honest framing is that the market has repriced a start date. If the blockade genuinely lifts and Iranian barrels return to a market that OPEC+ has just finished restoring, the supply-side disinflation is large and durable. If the talks collapse the way the June and July rounds did, the entire 7 percent comes back, and it comes back faster than it left, because inventories have been drawn through a quarter of disrupted transits.

## OPEC+ Quietly Finished the Job

The second oil story of the weekend got almost no attention and deserves more. At Sunday's virtual meeting the eight producers carrying voluntary curtailments approved an increase of 188,000 barrels per day for September, and that increment completes the phased rollback of the additional voluntary cuts introduced back in 2023. The 67th Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee met the same day to review May and June compliance and to press the overproducers on their compensation schedules.

Read the two headlines together. OPEC+ has now returned its entire voluntary cut to the market, and Washington is negotiating to bring sanctioned Iranian supply back on top of it. The war premium was masking a supply picture that has been loosening steadily all year, which is why Brent could not hold above $100 in late July even with Hormuz effectively shut and tankers being struck. Peace is not the only bearish thing that happened this weekend. It is simply the loud one.

## Korea Handed Back a Third of a Record in One Session

The Kospi fell 5.12 percent to 6,257.45 on Monday, with Samsung Electronics down about 8.95 percent and SK Hynix down about 8.56 percent. That comes forty-eight hours after the index posted the largest one-day gain in its history at 17.9 percent, with SK Hynix limit-up and Samsung up 28 percent.

Nothing changed in memory over the weekend. No contract price was renegotiated, no capacity was announced, no customer cancelled. What that tells you is what Friday's move actually was: a short squeeze detonated by a chairman's personal share purchase, layered on positioning that had already been washed out by a three-day, 17 percent crash the week before. It was not a re-rating, and Monday priced it accordingly. Our underlying view on the memory cycle is unchanged and, if anything, was strengthened last week when Apple guided September revenue growth to 9 to 11 percent against a 12 percent consensus and named DRAM and NAND inflation as the reason. That is the largest NAND buyer on earth confirming supplier pricing power through its own income statement. The distinction we would hold is between the cycle, which is now confirmed by invoices rather than by management commentary, and the Korean equity expression of that cycle, which has become a volatility instrument moving five to eighteen percent a session on flows. Those are two different assets. Elsewhere in Asia the Nikkei fell 1.1 percent to 63,652.26, the Hang Seng edged up 0.1 percent to 25,893.57, and the Shanghai Composite lost 0.8 percent to 3,801.86.

## Washington Joined Tokyo in the Currency Market for the First Time Since 2011

Japan's Ministry of Finance confirmed on Monday that Friday's yen-buying operation was conducted jointly with the United States Treasury, which makes it the first coordinated intervention between the two since the 2011 action that followed the Tohoku earthquake. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said Tokyo will not hesitate to intervene again in coordination, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Washington will not hesitate to participate. The yen gained as much as 1.4 percent to a near three-month high of 155.20, compounding a 3.8 percent surge over the prior two sessions, having earlier fallen to a 40-year low.

This is the most underpriced item on the page. Three weeks of yen strength were being read as unilateral Japanese action of low informational quality, the sort of move that fades. A confirmed joint operation, with an explicit commitment from both treasuries to repeat it, is a different object entirely. It places a policy floor under the yen, it removes the cheapest funding currency in the world from the carry trade at the margin, and it means the United States Treasury is now an active participant in weakening its own currency against the yen while the Federal Reserve debates a hike. Those two things point in opposite directions, and the tension between them has not been priced.

## Europe Rotated Out of Energy Without Selling the Index

The Stoxx 600 was up 0.4 percent at 651.88 in early trade, with energy down 2 percent and travel and leisure up 2.1 percent. That is the cleanest available expression of the morning: the index absorbed a 2 percent hit to one of its heaviest sectors and still rose, because everything that buys fuel rather than sells it repriced at once. Europe closed July with a fourth consecutive monthly gain and touched a record 656.67 intraday on Friday, and it keeps outperforming for the structural reason we have flagged all month, which is that it never took the hyperscaler capital-spending bet and therefore has nothing to hand back when that bet is questioned.

## Prysmian Paid a 30% Premium for American Conduit, and That Is an AI Trade

The one real corporate transaction of the morning is instructive. Italy's Prysmian agreed to acquire Atkore for $95.00 per share in cash, an enterprise value of roughly $3.8 billion, a 30 percent premium to Friday's close of $72.96 and a 57 percent premium to where the shares traded before Atkore announced its strategic review last September. Atkore is up about 28 percent pre-market.

Atkore makes electrical conduit, cable management and metal framing. It is about as far from a semiconductor as it is possible to get while remaining levered to the same demand. A European cable group paying a 30 percent premium for North American electrical infrastructure at this point in the cycle is a statement about where the binding constraint in data-centre construction actually sits, and it is consistent with what the hyperscalers said in their own prints last week: the limit is not chips alone, it is power delivery, and increasingly it is the physical hardware that moves electricity from a substation to a rack. This is the layer beneath the picks and shovels, and it is now being consolidated in public. Separately, GameStop is down about 9.5 percent on a $1.4 billion debt-for-equity swap that dilutes existing holders.

Palantir tonight carries the most information of the four, because it holds the named customers and the highest multiple at the same time, which isolates the valuation question cleanly. On SpaceX we would separate the operating story from the share-supply story.
