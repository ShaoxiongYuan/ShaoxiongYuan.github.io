The ceasefire is fraying from three directions at once, and the CPI just made everything worse. March headline CPI printed 3.3% y/y (+0.9% m/m), the hottest since May 2024, driven almost entirely by a 21.2% monthly gasoline spike. Energy accounted for three-quarters of the monthly increase. The print landed the same morning that Vance, Witkoff, and Kushner touched down in Islamabad for proximity talks with Iran, the most consequential diplomatic event since the war began. Markets couldn't decide what to do: the Nasdaq eked out +0.27% on tech strength while the Dow fell 0.53% on rate-sensitive names repricing a June cut from 68% to ~40%. Meanwhile, the real story is off-screen: Iran officially admitted it mined the Strait of Hormuz, releasing an IRGC navigational map for ships to route around explosives. Seven vessels transited Thursday, still a trickle. Saudi Arabia revealed that drone strikes hit the East-West pipeline and multiple energy facilities, knocking out 700K bpd of capacity. Hezbollah broke its 24-hour silence and fired 30 rockets into northern Israel, hitting Safed and Nahariya. The ceasefire isn't dead, but it's bleeding from every wound.

**Three things that matter this morning:**

1. **CPI at 3.3% kills the June cut.** Gasoline +21.2% m/m. The war is now in the inflation data. Fed funds futures repriced immediately: June probability dropped from 68% to ~40%. Warsh confirmation hearings haven't even started. The Fed is stuck.

2. **Iran admitted it mined Hormuz.** The IRGC released a formal navigational map routing ships around its own mines, the first official acknowledgment. This is not a strait that's "reopening." It's a controlled chokepoint under permanent IRGC surveillance. Twelve ships per day maximum, $1M+ tolls, no oil tankers.

3. **Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline was hit.** Drone strikes on the pipeline and multiple energy facilities knocked out 700K bpd of Saudi export capacity, roughly 10% of the kingdom's output. The Red Sea reroute just got harder. Dated Brent spot remains above $120.

## The War — Day 41 (Ceasefire Day 4)

### Islamabad Talks — The Main Event

Vance, Witkoff, and Kushner arrived in Islamabad Friday morning for the first direct diplomatic engagement of the war. Iran's delegation is led by Foreign Minister Araghchi and Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf. The format is proximity talks: both sides in the same hotel, different rooms, with Pakistani officials shuttling between them. They will not sit face to face.

Pakistan's stated goal is modest: get both sides to agree to keep talking. The 10-point Iranian proposal covers nuclear program, regional security, sanctions relief (including pre-war secondary sanctions dating back 45 years), and Hormuz. The enrichment clause remains the time bomb: English and Persian texts still differ on Iran's right to enrich to 20%. Vance warned Iran before departure not to "play us," while Araghchi told Iranian state media the talks are "a test of American sincerity, not ours."

**Market implication:** Any positive headline from Islamabad this weekend sends oil down $5–8 and equities up 1–2% Monday. A breakdown sends oil through $105 and reverses the ceasefire rally entirely.

### Iran Admits to Mining Hormuz

Thursday's most significant development: the IRGC Navy released a formal navigational map designating alternative shipping corridors through Hormuz to help vessels avoid anti-ship mines. This is the first official Iranian acknowledgment that mines are deployed, not merely threatened. Designated corridors route ships north of Larak Island on entry and south on exit, through a narrow band under direct IRGC surveillance. Iran announced a cap of 12 ships per day, each paying $1M+ in tolls. Zero oil tankers have transited since the ceasefire began. Seven total vessels crossed Thursday, down from 11 Wednesday.

**What this means:** The "reopening" language in the ceasefire text is meaningless. Iran has converted Hormuz from an international waterway into a toll road under military control. Even a successful Islamabad outcome won't demilitarize the strait for months. Physical oil markets will remain severely tight.

### Saudi Pipeline Attack

Iran-attributed drone strikes hit Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline (the 5-million bpd Petroline connecting the Eastern Province to the Red Sea port of Yanbu) along with production and refining facilities in Riyadh, the Eastern Province, and Yanbu Industrial City. The attacks knocked out approximately 700K bpd of Saudi export capacity, roughly 10% of current output. Damage was described as "contained" and the pipeline was not fully shut down, but the attacks demonstrate that even the Saudi Red Sea reroute (which bypasses Hormuz) is vulnerable. This is a new escalation vector.

### Lebanon — Hezbollah Breaks Silence

After holding fire for 24 hours following Monday's devastating Israeli strikes (250+ killed), Hezbollah launched approximately 30 rockets into northern Israel on Friday. Hits reported in Safed (parking lot), Nahariya (property damage), Kibbutz Misgav Am (direct hit on house), and Deir al-Asad (school). One IDF reservist severely wounded by a suicide drone in southern Lebanon. Israel responded with continued strikes across Lebanon but reportedly reduced military activity Thursday to align with the Islamabad diplomatic track.

Netanyahu's offer to open direct negotiations with Lebanon has not been formally received by Beirut. Lebanese officials told CNN they were not officially notified. Hezbollah's response was rockets, not words.
