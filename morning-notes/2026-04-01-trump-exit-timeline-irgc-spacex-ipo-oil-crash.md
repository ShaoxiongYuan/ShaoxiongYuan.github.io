## The War — Day 33: Trump Signals Exit. Iran Denies Everything. IRGC Threatens Big Tech.

The war entered its most contradictory phase. Trump is publicly signaling an exit timeline while simultaneously threatening NATO allies. Iran says it never asked for a ceasefire while its president tells Europeans he wants peace. The IRGC is threatening to destroy American tech companies. And oil is crashing on the assumption that all of this means the war is ending.

### Trump: "Two or Three Weeks"

The most market-moving statement of the past 24 hours:

- **Exit timeline**: Trump told reporters at the White House that US military forces could leave Iran in "two or three weeks." When asked whether successful diplomacy was a prerequisite, he said: "Iran doesn't have to make a deal, no." This reframes the war from "negotiate or escalate" to "we're leaving regardless."
- **Ceasefire claim**: Trump claimed Iran has asked the US for a ceasefire and that he would only consider it after the Strait of Hormuz is reopened. Iran's Foreign Ministry immediately called this "false and baseless." The IRGC stated the Strait remains "fully" under its control.
- **"Go get your own oil"**: Trump told allies who depend on Hormuz shipping to "build up some delayed courage, go to the Strait, and just TAKE IT." He's explicitly shifting Hormuz reopening responsibility away from the US and onto oil-dependent nations, primarily the UK, France, Japan, South Korea, and India.
- **NATO threat**: Trump said he is "strongly considering" pulling the US out of NATO, calling it a "paper tiger." He told Britain's Telegraph he is "beyond reconsideration." In tonight's address, he plans to discuss "my disgust with NATO" alongside the Iran war update.
- **Address to the nation**: Trump will speak at 9 PM ET tonight (Wednesday). He is expected to reiterate the two-to-three-week timeline, declare that the military is meeting or exceeding all operational benchmarks, and address NATO.

**What it means:** This is potentially the most consequential shift in the war. Trump is signaling the US will end its military campaign unilaterally, with or without a deal. If the market believes this, the entire war trade unwinds: oil collapses, defence sells off, tech rallies, consumer discretionary recovers. That's exactly what started happening yesterday with Brent falling from $118 to ~$102 in 24 hours. But the execution risk is enormous: leaving without a deal means Hormuz may stay closed, Iran's proxies stay active, and the next crisis comes faster. Tonight's address will determine whether this is a negotiating tactic or actual policy.

### Iran: Denial, Defiance, and Destruction

Tehran's messaging is maximally confusing, and that may be intentional:

- **Ceasefire denial**: Iran's Foreign Ministry denied asking for a ceasefire, calling Trump's claim "false and baseless." This contradicts Monday's Pezeshkian "necessary will" statement; or more precisely, it's diplomatic positioning. Iran wants to negotiate without appearing to have blinked.
- **Pezeshkian's appeal**: President Pezeshkian urged Americans to "question your government's war motives," a direct appeal to US public opinion, bypassing the White House. This is a sign Iran sees anti-war sentiment as leverage.
- **Araghchi backchannel**: The Witkoff-Araghchi message exchange continues. Both sides have confirmed contact. Iran frames it as "not negotiations," but messages are being exchanged, which is what matters.
- **Mobarakeh Steel struck**: US-Israeli strikes hit the Mobarakeh Steel Company complex in Isfahan and the Khouzestan Steel Company in Ahvaz, two of Iran's largest industrial facilities. Initial assessments indicate massive damage to production units. This marks an expansion from military/nuclear targets to core industrial infrastructure.
- **4,700+ Iranian security forces killed**: Per Iranian state media (likely understated). The domestic political pressure to end the war is real.

**What it means:** Iran is doing three things simultaneously: denying Trump's narrative (pride), appealing to American public opinion (leverage), and keeping the backchannel open (pragmatism). The steel plant strikes are significant: hitting civilian industrial infrastructure crosses a line that strengthens Iranian hardliners' argument against negotiation. The tension between Pezeshkian's peace signals and the IRGC's escalatory threats reflects a genuine split within Iran's leadership.

### IRGC: Threatens 17 US Tech Companies Starting Today

The most alarming escalation of the past 24 hours:

- **The threat**: The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps announced it will target 17 US technology companies with operations in the Middle East, starting Wednesday at 8 PM Tehran time (12:30 PM ET today). The stated rationale: "From now on, for every assassination, an American company will be destroyed."
- **Companies named**: Apple, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Amazon, IBM, HP, Intel, Tesla, Boeing, JPMorgan, Nvidia, Palantir, and others.
- **Precedent**: Iran struck AWS data centers in the Middle East in early March, causing app and service outages across the UAE. This isn't an empty threat; they've done it before.
- **IRGC warning**: The Guard called on employees of American tech firms in the region to "immediately distance themselves from their workplaces" and urged residents near US company offices to evacuate.

**What it means:** This introduces a new attack vector: targeting corporate infrastructure rather than military or energy assets. If the IRGC follows through, the implications extend beyond physical damage. Cyber attacks on US tech companies could disrupt cloud services, financial systems, and communications across the Middle East. Tech stocks are rallying today on peace hopes, but the IRGC threat at 12:30 PM ET could reverse intraday sentiment if any attack materializes. Watch AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, and META closely after noon.

### Houthis: Fresh Attacks Continue

## ISM Manufacturing — 52.7: Expansion Continues. No Recession Signal.

The March ISM Manufacturing PMI, released at 10 AM today, was the most-watched data point of the week:

- **Headline**: 52.7 (vs. 52.3 consensus, up from 52.4 in February). Third consecutive month of expansion.
- **New Orders**: 53.5, healthy demand. No sign of war-induced demand destruction in manufacturing.
- **Production**: 55.1, strongest reading in months. Factories are running.
- **Supplier Deliveries**: 58.9, this is the war signal. Deliveries are slowing because of supply chain disruptions (Hormuz, Red Sea shipping). Higher supplier deliveries = longer lead times = inflationary pressure.
- **Employment**: 48.7, still in contraction. Manufacturers are producing more with fewer workers. Productivity story, but also caution.
- **Inventories**: 47.1, drawdown mode. Companies building to order, not stocking shelves.

**What it means:** The recession scare is off the table, for now. An ISM above 52 with strong new orders and production is not a recession economy. But the supplier delivery spike (58.9) is the canary: the Hormuz blockade is creating supply chain bottlenecks that will feed into producer prices and eventually CPI. The Fed is watching this component closely. ISM says the economy is expanding; supplier deliveries say inflation is coming. Both things can be true.

## ADP Employment — 62,000: Weak but Not Alarming

The ADP National Employment Report for March came in at 62,000 private-sector jobs, down from 63,000 in February:

- **Small businesses (<20 employees)**: +112,000, surprisingly strong. Micro-firms are still hiring.
- **Mid-size (50–249)**: -26,000, this is the weak spot. Mid-market companies are cutting.
- **Healthcare**: +58,000, continues to be the hiring engine.
- **Construction**: +30,000, infrastructure and housing still active.
- **Pay growth**: +4.5% YoY, stable but elevated. Not disinflationary.

**What it means:** 62K is below the ~150K needed to keep up with population growth. The labor market is cooling but not cracking. The divergence between micro-firms (+112K) and mid-size (-26K) suggests the war's economic impact is hitting mid-market companies hardest; they're big enough to feel energy cost pressure but too small to absorb it. Friday's NFP (released Good Friday, markets closed) will be the definitive read.

Conversely, a successful SpaceX IPO would reopen the IPO window that's been frozen since the war began, bullish for venture-backed companies and growth stocks broadly. The timing is extraordinary: filing an IPO in the middle of a war signals Musk's conviction that the conflict is ending.
