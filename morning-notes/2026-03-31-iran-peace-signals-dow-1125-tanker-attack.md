## Trump: "Go to the Strait, and Just TAKE IT"

President Trump escalated his public rhetoric over the past 24 hours while privately keeping backchannel diplomacy alive:

- **"Just TAKE it"**: In a call with allied leaders, Trump told partners to "go to the Strait, and just TAKE IT," referring to the Strait of Hormuz. He singled out the UK and France as "unhelpful," criticizing their reluctance to commit naval forces to a joint Hormuz operation.
- **Destroy-everything threat**: Trump warned publicly that if Iran does not reach a deal, the US will destroy all of Iran's power plants, oil wells, and Kharg Island, Iran's primary oil export terminal handling 90% of crude shipments. This goes well beyond current air operations and signals willingness to pursue economic annihilation.
- **April 6 deadline holds**: The extended strike deadline remains April 6. Trump framed it as Iran's last chance to negotiate before the campaign enters a new, more destructive phase.
- **Behind the scenes**: Defense Secretary Hegseth told reporters talks are "continuing and going well" despite the public posturing. This suggests the escalatory rhetoric is leverage, not a policy break. White House envoy Witkoff has been exchanging messages with Iranian FM Araghchi, confirmed by both sides.

**What it means:** Trump is running a dual-track strategy: maximum public pressure to satisfy hawks and create urgency, while keeping diplomatic channels open through Witkoff. The "just TAKE it" rhetoric is designed to move reluctant allies. The destroy-infrastructure threat sets the ceiling for what happens if April 6 passes without a deal. Markets need to distinguish between the rhetoric (bearish) and the backchannel (bullish).

## Iran: Pezeshkian Signals Willingness, But With Conditions

Monday's biggest market catalyst came from Tehran, not Washington:

- **"Necessary will"**: Iranian President Pezeshkian told EU mediators that Iran has the "necessary will" to end the war but requires security guarantees before formal negotiations. This is the most conciliatory language from Iran's leadership since the war began on February 28.
- **Araghchi confirms contact**: Foreign Minister Araghchi acknowledged messages have been exchanged with Witkoff but insisted these are "not negotiations," framing them as preliminary communications. This is diplomatic positioning, not rejection.
- **Lebanon condition**: Iran demands any ceasefire framework include Lebanon, linking the broader Hezbollah/Israel front to US-Iran talks. This complicates the framework but also signals Iran is thinking about comprehensive settlement, not just buying time.
- **"Complete end to war"**: Araghchi stated Iran seeks a "complete end to war, not just a ceasefire." This is diplomatically sophisticated, telling Washington that Iran won't accept a pause that leaves sanctions and military posture intact.
- **4,700 killed**: Iranian state media acknowledged 4,700 security forces killed since the war began. This is likely understated but still represents significant domestic political pressure on Tehran to find an exit.

**What it means:** Pezeshkian's statement was the trigger for Monday's massive equity rally. The market read it as Iran opening the door to talks, and it is. But the conditions (security guarantees, Lebanon, "complete end") set a high bar. This is the beginning of a negotiating position, not a capitulation. Ceasefire probability ticks up to 35–40% from 30–35% yesterday.

## Israel: 11,000+ Targets. No Ceasefire Interest.

Israel continues to operate on its own timeline:

- **CENTCOM reported 11,000+ targets struck** across Iran since the war began, a staggering operational tempo combining US and Israeli air assets.
- **Nuclear sites**: Israel's March 27 strikes on Arak and Ardakan remain the most consequential Israeli action of the war. Iran's nuclear program has been set back years. Israel shows no interest in joining diplomatic efforts.
- **IDF posture**: Israel has not signaled willingness to participate in any ceasefire framework. Netanyahu's government views the war as an opportunity to permanently degrade Iran's nuclear and military capabilities. Iran's demand to include Lebanon in talks is partly aimed at restraining Israeli operations through a broader framework.

**What it means:** Israel is the wildcard. Even if the US and Iran reach a ceasefire, Israel may continue striking Iranian targets unless explicitly restrained. This creates a scenario where markets rally on US-Iran peace signals but Israeli escalation re-introduces risk. Watch for any Netanyahu statement on the Islamabad/Witkoff track.

## Houthis: Active on Second Front. Tanker Attacked off Dubai.

The Houthi front that opened March 28 remains active:

- **Al-Salmi tanker attack**: Iran-aligned forces struck the Kuwait-flagged tanker Al-Salmi off Dubai carrying 2 million barrels of crude. Fire was contained; no casualties reported. But striking a vessel off Dubai, in the UAE's waters, is an escalation beyond the Strait itself.
- **Red Sea threat persists**: Houthis continue to threaten the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and Red Sea shipping. Major shipping lines (Maersk, CMA CGM, MSC) have suspended Suez services.
- **Yanbu still at risk**: Saudi Arabia's Yanbu bypass (East-West Pipeline at 7M bbl/day) remains within Houthi missile range. A drone struck the Samref refinery last week. Systematic attacks on Yanbu would eliminate the last workaround for Persian Gulf oil.

**What it means:** Two of the world's three critical maritime energy chokepoints remain threatened simultaneously. The Al-Salmi attack extends the threat zone to the UAE coast. This is the structural floor under oil prices. Even peace signals cannot undo the chokepoint risk until Hormuz physically reopens.

## CB Consumer Confidence | March: Weak But Not Collapsing

The Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index for March came in at **91.8**, up 0.8 from February's revised 91.0:

- **Present Situation Index**: 123.3 (up from 120.1). Consumers still see the current economy as okay, largely because unemployment remains low.
- **Expectations Index**: 70.9 (down from 73.2). This is the problem. The forward-looking component fell further below the 80 threshold that historically signals recession risk. Consumers see gas at $4, war on TV, and stocks in correction. They're pulling back on spending plans.
- **Jobs "hard to get"**: Rose to 16.8% from 15.9%. Labor market perceptions are deteriorating at the margin.

**What it means:** The headline number masked divergence. Present conditions are holding because the labor market hasn't cracked yet. But expectations are deteriorating fast. If oil stays above $110 and gas crosses $4.25, the Expectations Index likely breaks below 65, deep recession-warning territory. Watch ISM Manufacturing on Wednesday for the business-side confirmation.
