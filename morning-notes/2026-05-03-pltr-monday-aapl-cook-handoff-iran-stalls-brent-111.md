Sunday before the bell. Friday closed with the S&P at a fresh record of 7,230 and the Nasdaq stretching into new territory, but the geopolitical backdrop hardened in two directions over the weekend. Israeli air force conducted intense strikes across southern Lebanon on Saturday, killing at least 41 people in 24 hours and breaking what had been a fragile two-week ceasefire. In Washington, Trump rejected Iran's revised peace proposal on Friday afternoon, and the IRGC followed with public commentary that renewed conflict is "possible." Pakistan-mediated talks remain the only working channel. Brent stayed bid at $111.20 into the weekend, WTI at $105.40, with the Strait of Hormuz still effectively shut by US and Iranian blockades.

The other major weekend story sits 1,200 miles west of Wall Street. Greg Abel ran his first Berkshire Hathaway annual meeting in Omaha on Saturday with Buffett watching from the audience. Q1 operating profits doubled, the cash pile sits at a record near $385 billion, and Abel ruled out any break-up. The signal that matters for our coverage is Abel's restraint on AI capital allocation. He told shareholders Berkshire is "not going to do AI for the sake of AI," which lands as a veiled critique of the hyperscaler capex that has been driving the index. The crowd was visibly thinner than 2025, which is its own data point on Buffett-as-spectacle.

The week's other set pieces were already in the books before Friday's close. The April 29 FOMC delivered a hawkish hold at 3.50% to 3.75% with four dissents, the most since 1992, and was Powell's last meeting in the chair before he steps back to governor on May 15. Apple printed Q2 on April 30, beat across the board on rev and EPS, and disclosed that Tim Cook hands the CEO seat to John Ternus on September 1. The Cook headline initially overshadowed a strong fundamental quarter, but the stock has worked through it and finished the week green. The market is rewarding execution, ignoring CEO transitions, and pricing oil as supply-bound rather than demand-destructive. That combination is fragile.

Three things into Monday:

1. **PLTR is the marquee print of the week, after the close.** Consensus sits at $0.28 EPS on $1.54B in revenue (revenue +74% y/y), with commercial expected at $771M (+94%) and government at $764M (+57%). Options are pricing a 10.55% straddle, slightly above the four-quarter realized average of 9.28%. The stock has drifted lower into the print despite a recent Oppenheimer initiation at $200, and Burry's Q4 short disclosure remains the loudest fundamental dissent. We are positioned long on conviction in the AIP commercial accel, but sized for the implied move.

2. **AAPL has unfinished business post-Cook.** The fundamental Q2 was clean (rev $111.2B +17%, iPhone $57B +22% record, China $20.5B +28% turning to tailwind), but the succession question now overhangs every product decision through September. Ternus is a hardware lifer, not a services or AI architect, and the WWDC keynote on June 9 becomes a referendum on whether the AI roadmap travels with the new chair. We are flat the name into WWDC after taking off the put-spread post-print.

3. **The bond market is screaming what equities refuse to hear.** The 30Y closed Friday at 5.02%, the first time above 5% since late 2023, and the 10Y at 4.55% sits 37 bps wider than where it printed three weeks ago. Auction tails on the 7Y and 30Y this week were ugly. With four FOMC dissents, a higher CPI risk into May 13, and Brent at $111, duration is the wrong place to be. Equity multiples have not yet rerated for the rates move. They will, and the catalyst is likely CPI.

## Geopolitics — The Ceasefire Is Cracking

The situation is materially worse than it looked one week ago.

**Lebanon.** Israeli air force struck approximately 50 Hezbollah-linked sites Saturday across Tyre, Bint Jbeil, and Marjayoun districts. Lebanon's Ministry of Health put the 24-hour death toll at 41. Local press reported alleged white phosphorus around Seriane and Taybeh, which would be banned in civilian areas. Hezbollah retaliated with rockets and explosive drones at IDF positions in southern Lebanon. One rocket was intercepted, several detonated near forces without injuries. The November ceasefire framework is functionally suspended even if neither side has formally withdrawn. Over a million people remain displaced.

**Iran.** Trump rejected Tehran's revised peace proposal Friday after a week of Pakistan-mediated shuttle diplomacy. Secretary Rubio called the negotiators "very good" but signaled the latest draft was unsatisfactory. The IRGC stated publicly that "renewed conflict is possible" and that Iran is "fully prepared." Hormuz transit volumes remain a fraction of pre-war norms, and OFAC's secondary sanctions on Chinese refiners taking Iranian crude were extended Friday for another 90 days.

**Trump tariffs.** The 25% secondary tariff on countries doing business with Iran (announced January 12) is still in force. Trump separately threatened a 50% tariff on countries shipping arms to Iran. The Trump-Xi summit in Beijing remains scheduled for May 14 to 15, which is the upper bound on tariff escalation in the very near term. Trump is unlikely to torch the China summit by escalating before he gets on the plane.

**Oil tape:** Brent closed Friday at $111.20, WTI at $105.40. The contango/backwardation flip last week into deeper backwardation tells you the prompt market sees physical tightness, not just risk premium. Dated Brent printed near $115 versus front-month $111. UAE quit OPEC last month, which removed a 2.9 mbpd swing producer from the cartel and structurally tightened the spare capacity picture. The next OPEC+ ministerial is May 21.

## Berkshire Hathaway Annual Meeting — May 2

Greg Abel ran his first as CEO. Buffett watched from the audience and offered light commentary on the Apple position (initial $35B basis now worth $185B per Buffett). Q1 operating profit doubled. Cash and Treasurys at a record ~$385B. Abel emphasized capital discipline ("financial independence, flexibility, tax efficiency, an aversion to arrogance, bureaucracy and complacency") and said Berkshire would not "do AI for the sake of AI." He ruled out a break-up. Adam Johnson (consumer and NetJets), Ajit Jain (insurance), and Katie Farmer (BNSF) joined him on stage. Attendance ran roughly half the 2025 crowd, which is the first soft data point on the Buffett premium decaying out of the equity. BRK/B finished Friday at $510.

**Implications:** With BRK sitting on $385B of cash earning ~5% on T-bills, every quarter without a deployment is a 50 bps drag versus the index. Abel's tone suggests no imminent change. The opportunity cost is real and the market may start to price it. Watch BRK/SPY ratio for the first time it breaks the 12-month uptrend.
