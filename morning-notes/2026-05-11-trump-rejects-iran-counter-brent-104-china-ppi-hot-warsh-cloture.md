Monday pre-market, 7:45 AM ET. The Sunday afternoon Truth Social post from President Trump rejecting Iran's counter-proposal as "totally unacceptable" is the read of the morning, and it pulls every line of the book in a consistent direction. Tehran's reply, delivered through the Pakistani back-channel late Saturday and surfaced by state media Sunday, walked the 14-point memo into a wider zone rather than a narrower one. The counter-memo demands recognition of Iranian sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz, war-damages compensation, the release of frozen Iranian assets, the lifting of OFAC sanctions on Iranian oil sales during a 30-day pilot window, an end to the US naval blockade, and a separate negotiating track for the nuclear file. There is no mention of the highly enriched uranium dilution language that the White House team had penciled as a hard line on the original memo. CENTCOM raised the redirected vessel count to 61 from 57 over the weekend, with more than 20 US warships now on station enforcing the blockade. Brent gapped through $103 at Sunday open and is trading $104.20 in early London, WTI is back to $97.88, and the 10Y is six basis points wider at 4.44%. June S&P 500 e-mini futures are down 0.22% at 7,383, Nasdaq 100 futures are down 0.12% at 29,299, Russell 2,000 futures are off 0.42% at 2,856, and the VIX is repricing to 17.85 in pre-market screens. China's April CPI surprised to the upside at 1.2% versus the 0.9% consensus, but the bigger print was PPI, which jumped to 2.8% year over year, the highest since July 2022 and a full 120 basis points above the 1.6% consensus. The NBS attributed the move to the Iran-war energy passthrough, with the domestic petroleum complex up 5.7% year over year. Kospi closed +4.32% on the back of an SK Hynix and Samsung-led memory beat, the Nikkei was soft at -0.47% on a yen wobble, the CSI 300 closed +1.64% with energy and materials leading, the Hang Seng held flat, and the Stoxx 600 opened roughly 0.3% lower with FTSE relatively bid. The information sequence into the Monday close is heavy: Existing Home Sales at 10:00 AM, the rescheduled $42B 10Y auction at 1:00 PM with a clear tail risk on the China PPI shock, Senate cloture on Warsh at 5:30 PM (we model 53 to 56 votes), and the Constellation Energy print AMC against $2.61 EPS / $8.71B revenue consensus. The book opens Monday with memo probability cut to 24% from Sunday's 32%, Brent long sizing held into the OPEC+ window on May 21, and the CEG vertical reinforced ahead of the print.

Three things to internalize before the open:

1. **The counter-memo is structurally worse than no memo at all, and it raises the operational ceiling on the energy long.** Tehran's framing has shifted from "still reviewing" on Saturday to a concrete maximalist counter on Sunday, which the Witkoff and Kushner channel reads as a deliberate stall through the Trump-Xi summit window. The sovereignty-over-Hormuz language is the structural escalator since it would convert the strait into Iranian internal waters under any reading, and the 30-day pilot window construction is the Iranian playbook for unwinding sanctions enforcement without delivering a binding nuclear commitment. We hold the Brent August $95 / $108 strangle at $4.30 entry, mark $5.40 (+26%), and we add 0.05% NAV to the XLE June 19 $99 / $108 call spread at $2.55 mark, bringing total energy long sizing to 0.60% NAV. The right read on the curve is that the September Brent skew is repricing the right wing, with the $110 strike implied vol moving from 39% Friday to 46% in Sunday electronic.

2. **China PPI at 2.8% is the cleanest read on the global goods-inflation passthrough, and it pulls the duration trade tighter into Tuesday's US CPI.** The PPI surprise is 120 basis points above consensus, and it lands the day before the US April CPI print with the Cleveland Nowcast already running at 3.5% to 3.9% versus 3.0% headline consensus. The China import-price channel into US final demand carries roughly a two-month lag on durable goods, so the Q2 read-across is not directly in the April US number, but it is the cleanest forward indicator for May and June. We hold the 2Y June 17 4.10% / 4.30% put spread at 12 ticks entry, mark 17 ticks (+42%), and we hold size at 0.25% NAV into Tuesday rather than adding into the rally. The 10Y at 4.44% is six basis points wider on the day, and the rescheduled $42B 10Y auction at 1:00 PM is the second-largest auction of Q2, with a clean tail risk if the bid-to-cover prints below 2.45.

3. **CEG AMC is the cleanest single-name read on the AI power demand structural thesis until NVDA on May 20, and we are sized for a beat-and-raise.** Q1 consensus is $2.61 EPS on $8.71B revenue, with the EBITDA benchmark at $2.02B confirming Calpine integration pace. The two metrics we watch on the call are first, whether CEO Dominguez updates the PJM regulatory clarity timeline, and second, whether the data center pipeline disclosure includes any new hyperscaler MOU beyond the Microsoft Three Mile Island agreement. The buyside whisper is running $0.13 above consensus, and options markets are pricing a roughly 7% move on the print, which we view as fair. We hold the CEG May 16 $310 / $330 call spread at $4.20 net debit from Wednesday, mark $5.45 (+30%), with PT $330 close.

The separate nuclear track is also a familiar device that Tehran used in the 2014 to 2015 framework, and the partial HEU dilution rather than full ship-out is the gap that ultimately killed the original JCPOA architecture. We mark memo probability at 24% Monday morning, down from 32% Sunday morning, against a market that is now repricing closer to 38% per the Brent September call skew.
