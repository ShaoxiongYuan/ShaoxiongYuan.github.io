## Why the Nuclear Strikes Change Everything

For 27 days, Israel and the US conducted strikes against military targets, air defenses, missile bases, IRGC facilities, and — once the US paused — energy infrastructure. But nuclear sites were the unspoken red line. That line was crossed overnight.

**What was hit:**

- **Arak Heavy Water Reactor:** A key plutonium production facility. Damage here sets back Iran's potential weapons program by years. This was partially disabled under the 2015 JCPOA and had been slowly rebuilt.
- **Ardakan Yellowcake Plant:** Iran's primary uranium extraction facility in Yazd province. Produces the raw material for the entire enrichment chain.
- **Khondab Heavy Water Complex:** Also in the Arak complex. Confirmed damaged by satellite imagery.
- **Steel plants:** Khuzestan and Mobarakeh (Isfahan) steel facilities, both IRGC-linked, were also hit.

**Why this is different from everything before:**

- **Diplomatic contradiction:** Trump extended the US strike pause to April 6 to give talks room. Israel struck nuclear sites the same day. Araghchi explicitly said this "contradicts" the US diplomatic track. Pakistan's mediation is now under strain.
- **Escalation risk:** Iran vowed to "exact heavy price." Nuclear sites are a sovereignty issue in a way that military bases are not. The retaliation risk just spiked dramatically.
- **Oil implications:** The Strait of Hormuz was already blocked. Now Iran has additional motivation to escalate. Brent broke $110 for the first time since the 2022 Russia-Ukraine spike. The $95–$105 equilibrium we identified Monday is gone.
- **Ceasefire probability:** We are downgrading from 45–50% to 30–35%. The nuclear strikes make Iranian hardliners less willing to engage. Pakistan confirmed talks continue, but the political space for compromise has narrowed significantly.

## The Fed's Nightmare | Rate Hike Odds Cross 50%

This is the most important macro development in months. Let's break down the transmission chain:

- **Oil at $110 -> Headline inflation surge:** Every $10 increase in oil adds ~0.3% to headline CPI over 3 months. Oil has gone from $75 pre-war to $110 — a $35 increase that will add roughly 1% to headline CPI by June. The Fed projected 2.7% PCE for 2026. Global forecasters now estimate 4.2%.
- **UMich inflation expectations at 3.8%:** Today's final March UMich reading confirmed the worst fears. The headline sentiment index fell to 53.3 (from 56.6 in Feb), but the inflation expectations number is what matters. At 3.8%, it's the highest since April 2025 and far above the Fed's comfort zone. If expectations become unanchored, the Fed HAS to respond.
- **Consumer cracking:** UMich expectations fell to 51.7 — the lowest since December 2025. Short-term economic outlook plunged 14%. Consumers see both higher prices and weaker growth. That's the stagflation cocktail.
- **Fed funds futures:** 52% probability of a hike by December 2026. April FOMC is 96% hold. The market expects the Fed to sit tight through Q2 while data deteriorates, then face a forced choice in H2.

> **IMPLICATION — The Stagflation Trap Is Closing**
>
> The Fed is caught: raise rates to fight inflation and crush a consumer already at 12-year-low confidence, or hold rates and let inflation expectations spiral. Powell's March presser ("need to see how tariffs and geopolitics feed through") was an attempt to buy time. But at $110 oil and 3.8% expectations, time is running out. Our base case is now: no cuts in 2026, with a 25–30% probability of a hike by September.

**Our view:** The TurboQuant trade is now inseparable from the war trade. MU and NVDA are down because of compounding fear — TurboQuant + nuclear strikes + Fed hike risk — not because any single catalyst justifies a −23% decline from ATH. The thesis from yesterday holds: buy the dip. But acknowledge the dip isn't done.
