Thursday, September 17, 2026, written at 8:50 a.m. Eastern, forty minutes before the open and roughly twenty six hours before the Bank of Japan. The Federal Reserve raised rates yesterday for the first time since July 2023. Everything below is a pre-market mark.

## Top Call: The Hike Was Priced. The Sentence About Financial Conditions Was Not.

The Committee moved the target range up a quarter point to 3.75 to 4.00 percent. That was 92.5% priced and it is not the news. The news is three sentences from the opening statement, which read in full: the Committee is continuing its policy of maintaining ample reserves, inflation remains elevated, and, in Warsh's own words, he would be hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive, a view he said was widely shared by the Committee, so they removed a dose of accommodation.

Take that language seriously, because it is a reaction function and not a mood. A central bank that describes a hike as removing accommodation is telling you it does not believe it has reached neutral, let alone gone past it. A central bank that names financial conditions as the evidence is telling you what it will watch to decide whether to go again. The Summary of Economic Projections agrees: the median 2026 dot moved to 4.10% from 3.80% in June, which is one more hike this year, sixteen of the eighteen participants who submit dots want at least that, four of them want two, and for 2027 eight see another increase against six holds and four cuts. Warsh files no dot of his own, so the median he does not vote in is the market's only map, and the man who can override it spent thirty minutes saying this summer's inflation readings do not tell him underlying trends have meaningfully improved.

The tape yesterday understood the first half of that and not the second. The Dow fell 631.21 points, or 1.2%, to 51,461.90. The S&P lost 0.45% to 7,551.81. The Nasdaq Composite fell 3.15 points, which after rounding is nothing at all, closing at 25,978.42. A 1.2% decline in the Dow against a flat Nasdaq on a day the Federal Reserve tightens is a coherent trade. Rate sensitive industrials, banks and dividend proxies wear the discount rate. Companies whose cash flows are dominated by an order book two and three years out do not, at least not on day one.

This morning the whole thing is being unwound in the other direction. S&P futures are up about 1.2%, the Dow is indicated up around 600 points, Nasdaq 100 futures lead at 1.6%, and Russell 2000 futures are up 1.5%. The instrument that let it happen is not equity at all. It is crude. Brent is down 3.43% to $102.15 and WTI is under a hundred dollars at $99.97, on Saudi guidance that roughly half the East-West pipeline's capacity can be restored within days and full operation inside six weeks. Take the energy line out of the forward inflation path and the second hike stops being automatic. That is the entire argument behind a 1.2% gap up in the S&P the morning after a hawkish tightening.

Here is why that trade is thinner than it looks. It rests on a repair schedule published by the operator of a pipeline that has been offline since September 11 and is being shot at, and it ignores that the physical crude market went the opposite way in the same twenty four hours. Oman crude futures cleared $132 a barrel. The rate to ship physical crude from the Gulf to China reached a record $1.035 million a day. Aramco is rerouting roughly 20 million barrels to Asian buyers by ship to ship transfer near Sohar because the Red Sea route does not function. A flat price that falls 3.4% while the physical differentials and the freight print records is not a market that has been resupplied. It is a market that has been told it will be.

So the honest read of this morning is that a hawkish Federal Reserve is being discounted using an oil price that is being discounted using a press release. If the repair lands on schedule, the rally is right and the second hike genuinely moves out. If it does not, the equity market has just eased financial conditions by 1.2% into a chairman who told you yesterday that financial conditions are the thing he is watching, which is the one mechanism that makes the second hike arrive sooner rather than later. That reflexivity is the position, and very few people are being paid for holding it.

## The Curve Gave Warsh the One Thing He Wanted

Ignore the equity noise and look at what the bond market did, because it is the most informative price on the page. Yesterday the two year note sold off to its highest yield since 2024 on the dots, exactly as the front end should when a median moves up thirty basis points. This morning the two year is at 4.702%, three basis points lower. The ten year is at 4.988%, two lower. The thirty year is at 5.341%, one lower.

That is a parallel shift down, and the important half is the long end. For two weeks the story of this market has been a bear steepening driven by term premium, with the ten year printing 5.04% and the thirty year at 5.37% while the front end sat still, which is the shape a bond market makes when it does not believe the central bank will defend the target. Yesterday the Federal Reserve tightened into an energy shock and told the market it is prepared to do it again, and the long end did not sell off. It rallied.

That is credibility arriving, and it is worth more to the Committee than the twenty five basis points. A hawkish surprise that lowers the thirty year yield is the textbook definition of a successful tightening, because it says the market now expects less inflation later rather than more policy rate forever. We have spent a fortnight arguing that the five handle on the ten year was a term premium event about fiscal supply and energy rather than a repricing of the path. If that was right, then a credible chair is precisely the thing that takes it back, and one session of evidence now points that way.

The caveat is that two of the three legs are borrowed. Brent down 3.4% does a great deal of work in a thirty year yield, and so does a dollar index at 100.11 holding near seven week highs. Strip out the oil move and it is not obvious the curve does the same thing. We would rather watch the long end over the next three sessions than the S&P, because the long end is the only place where the market grades the chair rather than its own positioning.

## The AI Power Trade Split in Two This Morning, and the Split Is the Signal

Two names in the same theme moved violently in opposite directions before the open, and taken together they are more useful than anything the index did.

Generac is indicated up roughly 33% to about $233 after disclosing a long term supply agreement with Amazon for industrial backup generators across Amazon's global data center estate. Initial deliveries are $2.4 billion across 2027 and 2028, the agreement runs to as much as $8 billion over its life, and the company's existing data center backlog was around $1.35 billion, so the first tranche alone is close to twice the entire book. Cantor called it the most significant data center announcement in the company's history and reiterated Overweight at $333.

Fluence Energy is indicated down about 22% to under $7 after cutting fiscal 2026 guidance for the second time in six weeks, taking revenue from roughly $3.0 billion to $2.4 billion and widening the adjusted EBITDA loss from about $10 million to about $200 million on supply chain problems and the Houston ramp. Barclays moved it to Underweight with a $10 target from $16, citing execution delays, higher capital needs and thinning liquidity against a backlog that is still large. Management's stated plan is to right size growth so it does not have to raise capital.

Both companies sell equipment into the same bottleneck. One of them has a purchase order from a hyperscaler balance sheet and the other has a backlog it cannot convert without working capital. At a zero funds rate that distinction is an accounting detail. At a 4.00% funds rate with the median dot at 4.10% and a chairman who says conditions are not yet restrictive, it is the whole investment case, because the cost of financing the gap between an order and a delivery just went up for the fifth time this year and the equity market is no longer willing to fund it on faith. This is the same argument we have been making about SoftBank in a different part of the capital structure, and it is now visible in industrials at single digit share prices.

The detail in the Generac deal that deserves more attention than the headline is the consideration. Amazon received warrants over 1,693,745 shares at $200.93, roughly 3% of the company, worth as much as $340 million. That is a customer being paid in equity to place an order. The structure is familiar from the chip layer, where suppliers, model labs and clouds have spent eighteen months taking positions in one another alongside commercial agreements, and it has now reached the generator supply chain. It is not sinister and it is often rational, since it aligns a supplier to a buildout it could not otherwise justify financing. It does mean that an increasing share of the demand signal in this theme carries an equity claim attached, and that revenue quality and share count have to be read together rather than separately.

## Earnings: Lennar Bought Volume With Price, Which Is the Answer to the Dot Plot

Lennar reported after Wednesday's close, into the decision, and it is the cleanest available measurement of what this policy rate does to the most rate sensitive part of the real economy.

| Metric | Consensus | Actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$8.37bn | $8.05bn | Miss |
| Adjusted EPS | $1.30 | $1.23 | Miss, earnings down ~38% y/y |
| Deliveries | n/a | 20,519, +2% y/y | Volume held |
| New orders | n/a | -4% y/y | Forward book softening |
| Average sale price | n/a | $371k from $389k | Price is the clearing mechanism |

**Our take**: the composition matters far more than the two cent miss. Deliveries rose 2% while the average selling price fell 4.6% to $371,000 and new orders fell 4%. That is a builder paying with price and incentives to keep the machine running, and the cost lands in the earnings line, which is down roughly 38% year on year. It is not distress, and it is not a housing market clearing on its own terms either. It is margin being handed to the buyer to offset a mortgage rate set by a ten year Treasury near 5%.

The reason this is the most useful print of the week is that it dates the transmission. The Committee's median dot says one more hike this year and no cuts until 2028, and the SEP is a forecast of an economy that tolerates it. Lennar is the same economy reporting from inside, six months into a rate backup that predates yesterday's move entirely, already funding 2% delivery growth out of gross margin. Whatever the dots say about 2027, the thing a policy rate can actually reach is already being reached, and it is showing up as price rather than as volume, which is the harder version to see in a macro series and the easier version to see in a cash flow statement. Housing starts and building permits land at 8:30 this morning with permits forecast at 1.400 million against 1.433 million, and the Lennar call at 11:00 will be more informative than either.

FedEx reports after the close, the first quarter in its history without FedEx Freight, which was spun off and began trading on its own on June 1. Consensus is $3.65 on $21.78 billion. With diesel at record levels for most of the quarter the fuel surcharge mechanism is doing an unusual amount of work in the revenue line, and the question worth asking on the call is how much of the yield improvement survives a Brent price that just fell 3.4% in a session. FedEx is the real economy's tell, and this quarter it is also an energy pass through story.

## Geopolitical Risks

Yesterday marked the two hundred and first day of the Iran conflict, and the tenth consecutive operational period without an announced American or coalition strike on Iranian territory. That is the longest such pause of the war, and it is the substance behind the oil move as much as the pipeline schedule is. Iran's foreign minister said Tehran is not interested in continuing the conflict with the United States, China's foreign minister urged reopening the strait and restarting negotiations, and the American president said the war is hopefully toward the end.

Set against that, the mechanics have not improved. A United States contracted vessel carrying American personnel was struck near the strait by four Iranian drones and at least one missile, with minor injuries reported. The Houthi spokesman claimed strikes on the Yanbu Aramco facility and on Khamis Mushait airbase. Transit counts remain unrecognisable against a pre-war baseline of 130 to 140 a day: Reuters recorded four transits on September 15 and Windward detected twelve on September 16, and the divergence between those two numbers is itself the story, since it measures a dark fleet running with transponders off. The energy secretary's claim of 18 million barrels moving in a day is not supported by any independent dataset we can see.

What has changed is the routing. Aramco is moving roughly 20 million barrels to Asian buyers by ship to ship transfer near Sohar, and the American military has carved a corridor along the Omani coast that has let Gulf exporters lift volumes through Hormuz. That is a genuine improvement in the supply picture and it deserves the dollars it took out of the flat price. It is also expensive, slow and reversible, and the cost of it is visible in the two numbers the flat price is ignoring: Oman futures above $132 and Gulf to China freight at a record $1.035 million a day. Barrels are reaching water. They are not reaching it cheaply, and the premium is sitting in differentials and in charter rates rather than in Brent.

Our framing is unchanged. This is a structural transit and export constraint with an event premium on top, and only the event premium breathes. What moved yesterday was the event premium, on an announcement, and the structural leg is intact until Yanbu loads and the transit count has a three digit handle again.

Separately, the United Nations fact finding mission concluded there are reasonable grounds to believe American forces committed war crimes in strikes on a school in Minab, where roughly 120 of more than 150 dead were children, and on a sports centre in Lamerd. That is not a market event this week. It is a constraint on the political room available to prosecute the war for the rest of it, and it belongs in any assessment of how the conflict ends.

## Accountability

Three marks to record, and only one of them is good.

The one that worked is the front end. We wrote yesterday that the two year at 4.65% was the only instrument on the page with asymmetric risk into 2pm, because a median dot above 4.00% would price a second hike the front end had not discounted at all. The median came in at 4.10% and the two year sold off to its highest yield since 2024. The setup was right and the identified instrument was the right one.

The volatility call is now wrong three sessions in a row. We said 17.50 was too low on Monday, 16.86 was too low on Tuesday and 16.67 had not noticed the dot plot on Wednesday. The VIX is 15.44. Three consecutive marks against on the same view is no longer a timing problem, it is a signal that the position is crowded on our side of it, and we are keeping the view into tomorrow's Bank of Japan and Friday's expiry only because those two events are dated, not because the last three days have told us anything encouraging.

The gold call needs the model changed rather than defended. On Tuesday we called the five week low the cleanest tell on the page and argued that a nineteen year high in real yields was outbidding the haven bid. Gold has now risen 0.7% and then 2.43%, and the second of those came on the day the Federal Reserve tightened and signalled another hike, which is the single worst possible day for the mechanism we described. The mechanism is not working. The better explanation, and the one we are moving to, is that gold here is not trading against the level of real yields at all. It is trading against the probability that this Committee tolerates a higher inflation rate for longer than it says, and a hawkish chairman raises rather than lowers the amount of attention that question gets. On that reading yesterday's price action is internally consistent and our earlier framing was simply the wrong model applied confidently. We would rather say that plainly than keep marking a losing view as early.

Everything above is a pre-market mark taken at 8:50 a.m. Eastern. The 8:30 data, the cash open and the Bank of Japan tomorrow morning can each rewrite the page.
