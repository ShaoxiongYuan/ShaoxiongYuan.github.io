Monday, September 21, 2026, written at 9:15 a.m. Eastern, fifteen minutes before the open. Every futures and pre-market level below is indicative and can move before the bell. Tokyo is shut for Respect for the Aged Day, so the Asian session ran without its largest cash market.

## Top Call: Two Things Repriced Overnight and Only One of Them Actually Changed

Brent is down about 3.3% to roughly $100.50 and WTI is down about 3.1% to roughly $97.10, the fourth consecutive decline and the longest losing run since June. The stated reason is that traders are looking toward the United Nations General Assembly this week for a resumption of American and Iranian diplomacy, after the president said he was open to meeting President Pezeshkian. Equities took the gift immediately. S&P futures are up 0.70% at 7,766, Nasdaq 100 futures are up 1.12% at 30,251, Dow futures are up 401 points and the ten year has given back five basis points to 4.95%.

In the same overnight window, South Korea published its customs file for the first twenty days of September. Total exports were $71.4 billion, up 78% on the year. Semiconductor exports were $34.12 billion, up 259.4%, close to half of everything the country shipped, against a trade surplus of $23 billion. That is not a survey, a guide or an executive comment at a conference. It is a customs receipt, and it is the hardest read on artificial intelligence demand anyone has produced this month.

Our view is that the market has the two stories weighted backwards. The peace trade is priced off a meeting that has not been scheduled, between two governments that were exchanging threats over the weekend, in a war that has run two hundred and five days with a fire pause that has already collapsed once this year. The chip number is settled data. And the part of the energy complex that actually reaches the inflation print did not improve at all: the American diesel crack set a record $118.62 a barrel on September 14, distillate stocks stood at 107.9 million barrels on September 11, the lowest for that week since 1982, and on Sunday Ukraine struck the Moscow oil refinery inside the largest drone barrage of the year. We wrote on Saturday that the barrel had stopped being the story and the refinery had become it. Today is the first live test of that framing and it is passing: crude fell three percent and nothing that matters to the consumer price index got cheaper.

So we would take the demand signal and decline the peace trade. The risk to that is not that Iran talks fail. It is that the market has now spent two weeks paying twice for the same Saudi pipeline repair and once for an Omani plan nobody confirmed, and it has given all of it back each time.

## The Tape: A Clean Risk-On Open Built on One Input

The pre-market is uniform in a way that tells you it has one driver. Futures on all four indices are higher, with the Nasdaq 100 leading at 1.12% and the Russell up 0.78% after five consecutive losing weeks. Bond yields are lower across the curve, with the thirty year down about three basis points to 5.30% and the ten year down five to 4.95%. Gold is down 0.9% to $4,386, its first decline in four sessions. Bitcoin is up 4.3% to about $84,670, its highest level since January, on a short squeeze that liquidated more than $600 million of bearish positions.

Every one of those moves is the same trade expressed in a different instrument, and the trade is lower oil. When crude falls three percent, the forward inflation path shortens, the term premium in the long bond compresses, the real rate on cash falls, the hedge against inflation persistence gets sold and everything with duration in its cash flows gets marked up. Gold falling while bitcoin rips four percent on the same morning is the cleanest evidence that this is a positioning event rather than a fundamental one, because those two assets have spent the year being described as the same trade and today they went opposite ways.

The VIX at 14.88 is effectively unchanged and is worth one sentence. A volatility index that did not fall on a three percent decline in the single most important macro input of 2026 is a market that has no hedges left to unwind. It sold them all into Friday's expiry. That is not a warning about anything specific, but it does mean the next genuine surprise has nothing to cushion it.

## The Peace Trade Is Priced Off a Speech That Has Not Happened

Here is what we can actually source about the diplomacy. The president has signalled willingness to meet Pezeshkian at the General Assembly, despite hostile exchanges over the weekend in which Iran's Revolutionary Guards threatened to deploy new weapons and strike additional targets if the United States opens another offensive. No meeting has been scheduled. No mediator has confirmed terms. On Friday, Iran's foreign minister said in Beijing that Tehran had agreed an Omani plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and no Omani official has since confirmed it.

The oil curve is telling you the market knows this. Front month WTI for October sits near $97, the November contract near $94. That is still steep backwardation, which is a physical market saying barrels today are worth materially more than barrels in five weeks. What has changed is that the deferred contracts are being marked down faster than the prompt, so the whole strip is falling while the shape stays tight. Read plainly, that is a market pricing an eventual political resolution while continuing to price a physical shortage right now. Both can be true. Only one of them is observable.

The second leg is Saudi supply. Aramco is working to restore about half of the East-West pipeline's capacity within days and the full four to five million barrels a day inside roughly six weeks, after the line was shut on September 11. Reports of stronger Saudi exports are part of today's move. We would note that the same repair schedule has now been sold to this market three times in ten days, that the line has been offline for exactly as long as the schedule has existed, and that the pipeline remains the only large export route that bypasses Hormuz entirely. Aramco did restore full capacity in about a week after an earlier and smaller strike this year, so the capability is real. The claim is not the same as the flow.

## The Refinery Did Not Get the Memo

The numbers below are the reason we are not buying this morning's energy read, and they are current.

| Distillate indicator | Latest | Context |
|---|---|---|
| US diesel crack | Record $118.62/bbl, Sep 14 | Above the $107.72 record close we carried Saturday |
| US distillate stocks | 107.9mb at Sep 11 | Lowest for the week since 1982; EIA sees sub-100mb |
| ARA hub inventories | 16% below five-year average | July reading, the European buffer |
| Singapore stocks | 8.2mb recently | Against a 2025 average of 9.6mb |
| US retail diesel | Above $6.00 since early September | First time ever; roughly 68% higher on the year |

Bloomberg reported this morning that the shortage is likely to run into at least the first quarter of 2027 as storage tanks drain. The detail worth keeping is a quote from the chief operating officer of a tank brokerage: "Why would you pay for a storage tank when there is no diesel to store?" Leaseable storage capacity in North America and the Caribbean rose to thirteen million barrels for October from eleven million in June, which is a market with empty tanks rather than full ones. Storage capacity becoming available in a shortage is the opposite of what an inventory cycle looks like and it is the signature of a market that cannot source the product at any price it is willing to pay.

Then Sunday happened. Ukrainian drones struck the Moscow oil refinery at Kapotnya inside the largest overnight barrage of the year. Russia's defence ministry said it downed 1,110 drones across nineteen regions and Crimea, eclipsing a previous high of 822 in August. Moscow's mayor put interceptions above 1,600, with 450 headed for the capital. Two people were killed and twenty injured in the Moscow region, and Kyiv confirmed the first combat use of its Pelican ballistic missile. The barrage landed on the final day of Russia's parliamentary elections, which tells you it was timed for political effect as much as industrial effect, but the industrial effect is what prices diesel. Russia has already extended its diesel export restrictions through October, Ukraine has removed better than thirty percent of Russian working refining capacity since August, and this weekend the campaign reached a refinery inside the capital.

So the distillate market this morning has a record crack, the thinnest American inventory cover since 1982, an expanded export ban, an intensifying campaign against the refineries that would have to loosen it, and a new American sanctions law signed Friday that makes the Russian and Iranian pools legally harder to reach. Crude fell three percent. Those are not the same market and they have not been the same market for three weeks.

## Korea's Customs File Is the Print of the Month

The Korean trade data is the sort of number that ends arguments. Exports of $71.4 billion in twenty days against $40 billion a year earlier. Chips at $34.12 billion, up 259.4%, comfortably the largest twenty day tally on record and ahead of the previous high of $61.7 billion for total exports set in June. Imports up 26.7% to $48.4 billion. A $23 billion surplus in under three weeks.

Two weeks ago this desk spent a Monday note on the industry's own executives arguing about whether the frontier should be paced, and watched Asia mark $91 billion off two Korean memory names in nineteen minutes on the strength of an essay. The argument we made then was that pacing the frontier constrains how fast capability advances, not how much silicon gets bought, and that the order book and the narrative are different objects. This print settles it for the quarter. Whatever anyone says at a conference, close to half of a major industrial economy's exports are now semiconductors, and the growth rate has a two hundred handle.

Seoul agreed. The Kospi closed at 7,007.72, up 1.65%, its first finish above seven thousand since September 9, with Samsung Electronics up 4.98% at 274,000 won and institutions buying a net 1.49 trillion won against foreign selling. SK Hynix added only 0.59%, which is the interesting divergence: the name most levered to high bandwidth memory barely moved while the broader manufacturer ran five percent. That is consistent with what the supply data says, namely that this is no longer a story about one accelerator platform. Samsung and SK Hynix finished memory inventory fell below ten days on September 7. Samsung has taken 32GB DDR5 module pricing to $239 from $149, a sixty percent increase, and DDR5 contract prices have more than doubled. Conventional memory is now tight because HBM4 consumes roughly three times the wafer capacity per bit, so the shortage has spread from the AI bill of materials into everything else that needs DRAM.

The unresolved item sits in Taiwan, and today is its deadline. Micron's Taoyuan and Taichung unions, representing about ten thousand of fifteen thousand staff, said that absent a concrete proposal at meetings on September 18 and September 21 they would declare negotiations broken down and hold a formal strike vote. An internal survey in August found more than eighty percent of participating members willing to walk out. Micron reports fiscal fourth quarter results on September 30. A strike vote at the company's largest DRAM and high bandwidth memory base, inside a market with sub-ten-day inventories, is a supply event and not a human resources story.

## Rates: Goolsbee Said the Quiet Part and the Market Bought Anyway

Austan Goolsbee spoke in London this morning at an Official Monetary and Financial Institutions Forum event and was about as hawkish as a regional president gets. The path back to two percent inflation is "unlikely to be painless." The central bank cannot ignore repeated and persistent supply shocks and must respond in a way that may cause hardship. Forecasters have spent more than a year pushing back the date on which inflation was supposed to peak, and "we need evidence that these shocks are actually fading, or it's hard to see a credible path back to 2% inflation." On demand, he said there is "no ambiguity" about what the Committee would do if it is overheating.

That is the first regional Federal Reserve voice since Wednesday's hike to 3.75 to 4.00 percent and a median 2026 dot of 4.10 percent, and it agrees with Kevin Warsh on the substance rather than softening him. Markets are now roughly a coin toss on an October move and price about an 87% chance of at least one more increase by December.

Yields fell anyway, because the energy line moved and the energy line is currently doing more work in the forward inflation path than any Federal Reserve speech. The two year is unchanged at 4.74%, at its cycle high, while the ten year is down five and the thirty year down three. That is a bull steepening of the front against the back, the mirror image of last week's bear flattening, and the mechanism is clean: the policy path is unchanged because Goolsbee did not change it, while the inflation risk premium at the long end compresses with crude.

The reflexivity we flagged on Wednesday is live for a third session. Warsh named financial conditions as the evidence he would watch. Equities have now eased them on Thursday, on Friday into the expiry and again this morning, with the Nasdaq 100 up better than one percent and credit untroubled. Every one of those sessions raises the probability of the October move that the front end is only half pricing. The market is buying a rally that pays for the hike that would end it, which is not a contradiction so much as a bill that arrives later.

## Corporate: The Services Layer Just Got Paid to Spend Money

Accenture is up about 6% pre-market, and the reason is worth unpacking because it inverts a rule this desk has been leaning on for two weeks. Anthropic and Accenture announced a partnership under which Accenture, through its Faculty artificial intelligence unit, will embed a team of evaluators alongside Anthropic's internal safety teams to red team models, run alignment assessments and test safeguards. Both companies plan to invest at least a billion dollars each over five years, roughly two billion in total, and the arrangement is not exclusive on either side.

The rule we have been applying since September 12 is that the tape pays the layer receiving the cheque and charges the layer writing it. Oracle beat on every line and closed down on capital expenditure guidance while Dell and Hewlett Packard Enterprise added twelve percent. CoreWeave fell four percent on its third convertible in ten months while memory names rallied. On that rule Accenture, which has just committed a billion dollars of its own capital, should be down. It is up six percent instead, and we think the market is right. The reason is that this is not capital expenditure, it is the creation of a recurring services annuity with a named anchor client in a category, independent model evaluation, that is about to be written into regulation in several jurisdictions. Accenture is building capacity it can sell to every other frontier developer, and Anthropic has said explicitly that it will use other providers too. The layer being paid here is expertise, and expertise does not depreciate on a three year schedule the way a GPU does.

Accenture reports on Thursday, which makes the timing awkward in a useful way. The stock is now carrying a narrative premium into a print that will be judged on bookings in the traditional consulting business, where the question for a year has been whether clients defer projects to fund their own technology bills.

Elsewhere the optical layer got its second upgrade of the month. Evercore ISI raised Ciena to Outperform with a price target of $550 from $375, arguing that connectivity between and inside data centres is becoming a gating factor for frontier model monetisation. Separately, Lumentum, Qualcomm and Corning are planning a joint optical interconnect demonstration for artificial intelligence systems. Two independent datapoints in one morning saying the bottleneck is moving from compute to the light that connects it.

In media, Warner Bros Discovery is up 7.9% and Paramount Skydance 6.8% on reports that settlement talks with state attorneys general over the $110 billion acquisition have reached an advanced stage. The concessions under discussion include a $1.5 billion California production commitment, an undertaking not to sell either studio lot, thirty theatrical releases a year with penalties attached, and independent monitoring of CNN's editorial output. California's attorney general has been pushing to close it; New York and Connecticut and at least two other states are not yet aligned. That last clause is why we would not treat a seven percent move as the deal clearing.

Also on the tape: Apollo is taking a 16% stake in the New York Yankees through a $2.6 billion injection that values the club above $12 billion; Oura has filed to raise $2.2 billion at $40 to $44 a share for a valuation up to $14.1 billion; DeepSeek is prioritising domestic silicon with Huawei deliveries expected in the fourth quarter; Ireland's Data Protection Commission fined Google $463 million over location data; and Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly are facing suits alleging their obesity drugs caused vision loss, with Novo separately targeting pipeline sales above $23 billion by 2035.

## Earnings: A Thin Monday Into Three Prints That Matter

Fourteen companies report today and none of them move an index. The week's substance is concentrated on Thursday, with the month's most important print still nine days out.

| Company | When | Consensus | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accenture | Thu | ~$3.70 on $17.3bn | Corporate tech budgets, now with an AI safety annuity attached |
| Costco | Thu, after close | $6.48 to $6.55 on ~$94.8bn | Cleanest read on a consumer paying $6 diesel |
| Micron | Sep 30 | Record quarter guided | Sub-ten-day memory inventories and a live strike vote |

**Our take**: these three are the only prints of the next ten days we would change a position on, and they test three different things. Accenture tells you whether enterprises are still funding discretionary transformation work or have diverted the budget into their own compute bills, and the six percent move this morning raises the bar it has to clear on Thursday. Costco is the counterweight to the consumer data, which has gone in two directions at once: August retail sales ran 1.2% on the month against a 0.2% call, while the University of Michigan sentiment index printed 47.8 in September, the second lowest reading in a series that starts in 1952, with inflation expectations at 4.6%. A membership retailer with pricing discipline is the best single window into whether households are absorbing fuel or substituting away from everything else to pay for it. Micron is the one that carries tail risk in both directions, because the demand side is now confirmed by Korean customs data and the supply side has a labour dispute at its largest fab with a strike vote scheduled to be called this week.

Last week's prints, for the record, all missed and all came from the rate sensitive economy rather than the buildout: Lennar at $1.23 on $8.05 billion against $1.30 on $8.37 billion, Nucor guiding to $5.55 to $5.65 against $5.99, Steel Dynamics to $5.34 to $5.38 against $5.60, and Fluence cutting fiscal 2026 revenue to $2.4 billion with the loss widening to $200 million. Nothing this morning changes that read. Construction inputs are contracting while the electrical and silicon layers are not.

## Asia and Europe

Tokyo was closed. That matters more than usual, because the Nikkei finished Friday at 65,018.95 in record territory on a currency trade in a handful of exporters while the Topix fell, and a holiday means that divergence goes untested for another day. The yen sits at 157.22, weaker again, which is now four sessions of a currency failing to strengthen after a Bank of Japan hike to 1.25%.

Seoul carried the region, as above. Hong Kong closed up about 1.2% at 25,042 and Shanghai up about 0.9% near 3,949, with the People's Bank of China leaving loan prime rates unchanged for a sixteenth consecutive month at 3.00% and 3.50%. The yuan is at its strongest in three and a half years. That is the single clearest expression of a policy choice: Beijing is using currency appreciation as a disinflationary tool and as a goodwill gesture into Thursday's summit, while declining to cut the rates that would actually stimulate domestic demand. It is a stance you can hold for a fortnight, not a year.

Europe opened firmly. The Stoxx 600 is up about 0.7% and the Euro Stoxx 50 about 1.1%, the DAX is up 283 points at 25,587 and the FTSE is up roughly one percent near 10,756. Technology led, with ASML, Infineon and STMicroelectronics each gaining about 2.8% on the Korean chip data, and Societe Generale added about 3.6% after raising its profitability target. Germany's Ifo survey is due with expectations of 89.3 and current conditions of 89.0.

We owe a correction here. On Saturday we attributed Europe's two day decline, led by autos and telecoms, to diesel exposure and called it the top call in sector form. Today Europe is rallying on a three percent fall in crude with technology leading and autos not driving it. The mechanism we described cannot explain both sessions. The more honest reading is that European equities are trading the global growth and rates impulse like everyone else, and that Friday's sector leadership was a European carmaker problem with its own causes rather than a clean expression of our distillate thesis. The distillate thesis stands on the American data. It does not need the European tape and we should not have recruited it.

## Geopolitics

The Iran war is at day two hundred and five with the fire pause in its eleventh day. The market's optimism rests on General Assembly week producing a meeting between Washington and Tehran that neither side has scheduled and that the Revolutionary Guards spent the weekend threatening to make irrelevant. Hormuz transits remain a fraction of the pre-war 130 to 140 vessels a day. Iran's domestic position continues to deteriorate, with the rial past 2.3 million to the dollar, which is the strongest reason to think Tehran wants an exit and no guide at all to when it takes one.

Russia held parliamentary elections through Sunday and Ukraine marked the final day with the largest drone barrage of the year, discussed above. The Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, signed Friday, authorises tariffs of up to 100% on the largest purchasers of Russian energy. Nothing has been imposed under it yet. When something is, it will be the first test of whether an administration seeking an Iran deal at the General Assembly is willing to open a second energy front with China and India in the same week.

Germany delivered the weekend's other political result. In Mecklenburg-Vorpommern the Alternative for Germany more than doubled its share to 38% and finished first, ahead of the Social Democrats at 35%, though a firewall coalition will keep it from governing. In Berlin, Die Linke won with 25.7% against 18.8% for Chancellor Merz's Christian Democrats, with the AfD third at 16.3%. Two weeks after the AfD took 43.8% in Saxony-Anhalt, Merz called the results a disaster. Markets are ignoring it, which is defensible on a one month view and less so on the view that matters, because the German federal government is the swing vote on European fiscal capacity and on Ukraine funding, and it has now been weakened in three state elections in a month.

Xi Jinping arrives in Washington on Thursday for his first visit to American soil since 2015. Treasury Secretary Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng met all day Sunday at JPMorgan's Manhattan headquarters, joined by Trade Representative Greer, and Bessent called the talks "very successful" with the two sides agreeing to meet again on artificial intelligence, including an American proposal for an incident notification mechanism. What he did not address publicly was the trade truce expiring November 10 or American complaints that Chinese rare earth and critical mineral flows remain insufficient. An AI dialogue is the cheapest possible deliverable and the one that was announced. The expensive items are still open with fifty days on the clock.

## Accountability

The crack call is working and it is three days old, so we will not oversell it. We wrote on Saturday that watching flat price to judge the energy contribution to inflation had become the wrong instrument. Since then crude has fallen four consecutive sessions, the American diesel crack has been confirmed at a record $118.62, distillate cover is at a forty four year low for the date, and Bloomberg has published a shortage running into 2027. The framing is being validated by events rather than by price, which is the weaker of the two forms of confirmation, and we will keep marking it.

The European sector attribution from Saturday is wrong and is corrected above. We used a one session move in European autos and telecoms as evidence for a distillate thesis that stands perfectly well on American inventory data, and today's rally in the same index on the same input shows the attribution did not hold.

The volatility call remains dead. We spent four sessions arguing hedges were too cheap. The VIX is 14.88 this morning, flat, having fallen through a quarterly expiry on Friday. We are not reviving it.

The Micron labour item, which we raised on Saturday as the unpriced element inside the memory trade, resolves today one way or the other. We will mark it tomorrow whichever way it goes.
