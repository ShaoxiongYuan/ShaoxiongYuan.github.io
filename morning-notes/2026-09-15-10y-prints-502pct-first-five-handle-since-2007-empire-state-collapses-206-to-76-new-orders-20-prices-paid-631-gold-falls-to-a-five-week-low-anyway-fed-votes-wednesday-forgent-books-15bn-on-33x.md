Tuesday, September 15, 2026, written at 9:20 a.m. Eastern, forty minutes before the open. Asia has closed, Europe is mid session, and the American cash market has not opened, so every futures mark below can move before the bell.

## Top Call: The Ten Year Has a Five Handle and the Fed Votes Tomorrow

The ten year Treasury traded to 5.02% this morning. That is the first five handle since July 2007, and it clears the October 2023 peak that has capped this market for three years. The thirty year is at 5.37%. The two year is at 4.66% and slightly lower on the day.

Read those three numbers together, because the shape carries all of the information. The front end is anchored, because tomorrow's hike to a 4.00% upper bound is priced at 92% to 93% and there is nothing left to discount. The long end is doing the entire move. A two year to ten year spread near 34 basis points that is widening on long end weakness is not a market changing its view of the policy path. It is a market raising the price of holding duration at all, which is inflation risk premium and fiscal supply rather than anything Kevin Warsh says at 2:30 tomorrow.

It is worth stating plainly where this came from. The ten year was 3.97% before the Iran conflict began. Roughly 105 basis points of the move to 5% has been delivered by a war over a shipping lane. The Federal Reserve has been a passenger on most of that, and tomorrow it becomes a participant.

Then at 8:30 this morning the New York Fed published the September Empire State survey, and it is the most important number on this page after the yield itself. General business conditions fell from 20.6 to 7.6. New orders collapsed from 17.3 to 2.0. Shipments went negative at minus 3.2. Prices paid accelerated from 58.6 to 63.1, and prices received printed 28.1. Responses were collected between September 2 and September 10, which makes this the first hard American activity read covering the weeks after Brent cleared $100.

That is the textbook signature of an energy shock arriving in the real economy. Input costs accelerate, orders stop. It is not a demand boom that needs cooling and it is not a wage spiral. It is an imported cost passing through a manufacturing base that cannot raise prices as fast as it is being charged, and the gap between prices paid at 63.1 and prices received at 28.1 is that squeeze expressed in a single number.

The committee raises rates into it tomorrow. The case for doing so rests on an August headline CPI of 3.4% against a core of 2.4%, a hundred basis point wedge that is effectively all energy, with gasoline up 27.4% over the year and the national average now $4.33 a gallon against $4.08 a month ago. We have argued for a week that this is a relative price shock rather than a monetary one. This morning a regional survey says the same shock is already destroying orders. Tightening into that is buying insurance against a cost the instrument cannot reach while adding to the cost of the demand it can.

So the move itself is spent and the information is entirely in the dot plot and the framing. If the median dot shows a second hike, the two year at 4.66% is wrong and the front end has to move. If it shows one and done, then the problem was never the front end and the thirty year at 5.37% keeps going. Warsh declined to submit his own projections in June, which means his press conference carries more weight than the median he did not vote in.

## Gold Falling Is the Cleanest Tell on the Page

Saudi civil defence issued danger alerts this morning for six cities including Yanbu, the kingdom's main Red Sea oil port and the destination of the East-West pipeline that has been shut since Friday, then cleared them. The Houthis claim to have hit aircraft hangars, radar, runways and ammunition depots at King Khalid airbase. Monday's attacks injured thirteen people. Brent is near $107 and WTI added a dollar to $103.61.

Gold fell. It is trading around $4,300, down between 0.6% and 0.8%, at a five week low.

On a naive reading that is a contradiction, and it is not. Gold pays no coupon, and the real yield available on a risk free alternative just reached its highest level in nineteen years. The haven bid is being outbid by the carry. What that tells you is that the marginal buyer is pricing this as an inflation and rates event rather than a systemic risk event, and that the same buyer believes the Federal Reserve will validate the tightening. It is the single most honest sentence the market has produced this week, and it is more useful than any commentary about safe havens.

The other half of the same sentence is the VIX at 16.86, down 1.4%, on the day before an FOMC with a dot plot, four days before a quarterly expiry, three days before a Bank of Japan meeting that could deliver the highest Japanese policy rate in roughly thirty one years, with a shooting war over the world's largest crude exporter. We flagged this index as too complacent at 15.84 on Saturday and at 17.50 yesterday. It has now gone the other way. We are keeping the flag.

## The AI Complex Found a Bid Overnight and It Came From the Wrong Place

SoftBank rose 9.1% in Tokyo to ¥6,372, recovering most of Monday's 10.7% loss. Kioxia added 2.3%. The Nikkei finished effectively flat at 63,484.10 after being up sharply in the morning. On the surface that looks like Monday's panic about the pace of frontier development being unwound.

Look at what actually caused it. SB Energy, SoftBank's data centre and power infrastructure unit, filed updated paperwork for a United States listing, offering up to $500 million of new shares against a reported valuation near $50 billion. Kioxia rose because Bloomberg reported it is weighing at least $10 billion through a US ADR listing, with Bank of America, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan in the conversation. And SoftBank repays the remaining $25.9 billion of its $40 billion OpenAI bridge loan today, months ahead of a March 2027 maturity, with a high yield bond sale of up to $20 billion planned to term the exposure out.

Every one of those is a financing event. Not one is a demand event. The overnight rebound in AI linked equities was purchased with the announcement that three of them intend to sell more securities. That works for exactly as long as the window stays open, and the window is priced off the same 5.02% ten year sitting at the top of this note.

The window is visibly narrowing. Brian Moynihan told the Barclays financial services conference that Bank of America's third quarter investment banking fees will land at $1.6 billion to $1.8 billion against $2.0 billion a year ago, a decline of 10% to 20%, with sales and trading roughly flat on $5.4 billion. The stock fell about 5% and dragged the sector with it. Dealogic has the whole investment banking market down roughly 10%. So the largest arranger in the market guided fees lower in the same week that SoftBank, Kioxia and SB Energy all queued up to issue into it.

This is the structural fact that matters most right now and it requires no view whatsoever on artificial general intelligence. The buildout has migrated from cash flow funding to capital markets funding. Capital markets funding has a price. That price is rising faster than the equity market is discounting, and it is being set by a pipeline in the Saudi desert rather than by anything in a hyperscaler's guidance.

## Earnings: The Order Book Answers Monday

**Forgent Power Solutions** (FPS), fiscal fourth quarter and full year to June 30, reported this morning. The company makes electrical distribution equipment, the switchgear and busway that stands between a grid connection and a rack, and it listed on June 29.

| Metric | Prior year | Q4 actual | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $238m | $462m, +94% | Beat, above high end |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $43m | $113m, +163%, 24.4% margin | Beat |
| Adjusted EPS | n/a | $0.25 (GAAP $0.21) | Beat, est. $0.24 |
| Bookings | $316m | $1,503m, +375%, 3.3x book to bill | Exceptional |
| Backlog | $843m | $3.0bn, +256%, all time high | Exceptional |
| FY27 guidance | n/a | Rev $2.4-2.6bn, adj EPS $1.26-1.40 | Raised, +76% and +95% |

**Our take**: this is the print that answers Monday's argument. A single quarter of bookings, $1.5 billion, exceeded the entire fiscal year's revenue of $1.42 billion. Book to bill at 3.3x means orders arrived more than three times faster than the company could ship, backlog tripled to $3.0 billion, and management guided fiscal 2027 revenue up 76% with adjusted earnings roughly doubling. Two days ago the market sold the whole semiconductor complex on an essay about the pace of frontier capability. This morning a company whose revenue is a direct function of how many megawatts actually get energised told you its order book quadrupled. Those two things cannot both describe the next twelve months. Our position is unchanged from yesterday: what got repriced on Monday was the terminal multiple, not the order book, and the order book keeps printing records.

The discipline point is that 3.3x book to bill is not repeatable and nobody should model it as though it were. A company guiding 76% growth against a $3 billion backlog will be judged on conversion, and conversion is a supply chain, transformer lead time and skilled labour question rather than a demand question. It is also a June listing with a short float and a narrow analyst base, which cuts both ways: the order book is uncontaminated by narrative precisely because almost nobody has a long model on it, and the shares will be volatile for the same reason.

Trip.com reports after the close tonight against consensus of roughly $0.87 on $2.29 billion, and it is the cleanest available read on whether the Chinese consumer weakness in this morning's data extends to travel. Lennar reports tomorrow after the Fed, with consensus at $1.30 on about $8.3 billion, earnings down roughly 35% year on year, against a thirty year mortgage at 6.76% and a ten year that just printed 5%. That report is the best single read available on what a five handle does to the real economy.

## Overnight and Pre-Market Developments

- **China**: August data diverged hard. Retail sales grew 0.4% year on year against 0.8% expected, the second consecutive slowdown. Industrial production accelerated to 5.2% from 4.5%, beating a 4.8% call. Fixed asset investment contracted 7.2%, worse than expected. Supply is accelerating while domestic demand and investment shrink, which makes China a deflationary exporter into a world that has an inflation problem. That is a mild disinflationary offset for goods prices and a poor advertisement for the commodity demand leg of the oil trade.
- **Japan**: the Nikkei closed at 63,484.10, effectively unchanged after giving back a strong morning. SoftBank rose 9.1% on the SB Energy filing, Kioxia rose 2.3% on the ADR report, while Advantest fell about 3% and Furukawa Electric 3.3%. The Bank of Japan meets Friday with the yen and the ten year JGB both at multi year extremes.
- **Korea and Hong Kong**: the Kospi fell 0.9% to 6,627.26, a second consecutive decline after Monday's 3.3% rout. The Hang Seng lost 1.0% to 24,667.24, a fifth straight loss. Shanghai fell 0.5% to 3,864.28. Australia's ASX 200 lost 0.9% to 8,672.50.
- **Europe**: indices opened between 0.4% and 1.2% lower and recovered part of it by midday, with the FTSE MIB weakest on Italian banks, the FTSE 100 down about 0.4%, the CAC 0.2% and the DAX 0.1%. Energy is the only sector consistently bid, with Shell, TotalEnergies and BP all higher. Tech remains defensive after Monday.
- **Banks**: the Bank of America guidance is the sector story and it is not idiosyncratic. Fee income guided down 10% to 20% with a pipeline management describes as strong but stuck says the constraint is closing rather than origination, which is what a 5% ten year does to deal maths.
- **Memory**: the structural backdrop under Kioxia's financing plan has not changed. Suppliers are effectively sold out for 2026, data centres consume roughly 70% of global memory output, and recent sequential price increases ran around 30% for SK Hynix DRAM and above 40% for Samsung, with NAND faster still. Storage remains the tightest physical constraint in the buildout and the one most likely to surprise on pricing.
- **Calendar**: the FOMC meeting begins today. August retail sales and import prices land tomorrow morning, before the decision.

## Geopolitical Risks

The war moved closer to the export terminal overnight. Alerts covering Yanbu, Jeddah, Abha, Jazan, AlUla and Taif were issued and then cleared, but the fact that the kingdom's principal Red Sea loading port required an air raid alert at all is the escalation. The East-West pipeline that feeds Yanbu remains shut after Friday's drone strikes, Yanbu's own export stock runs five to seven days at current rates, and repair estimates still span days to six weeks. The Houthis hold Mokha, Perim and Zuqar, which is territorial control on both approaches to Bab el-Mandeb, and nearly 94,000 people have been newly displaced inside Yemen.

The distinction we have been drawing for a week holds. Previous spikes this year were event premium that decayed because the physical system absorbed the shock, and the absorption mechanism was the pipeline. With the pipeline down and the port under threat, the correct model is a structural transit constraint with an event premium layered on top, and only the second layer breathes. Brent adding 0.3% to 1.3% on a morning that included air raid alerts over Yanbu is not a market that has priced the first layer.

Elsewhere, Canadian tariffs on more than 700 American goods remain live with a federal procurement ban due September 29, and the Xi and Trump meeting later this month still sits on unresolved rare earth and optical transceiver disputes.

## Accountability

We wrote yesterday that the VIX at 17.50 was still too low. It closed lower and is at 16.86 this morning. One session is not a verdict on a volatility call, but the mark is the mark and it has gone against us so far.

We also wrote yesterday that the tape had conflated pacing the frontier with buying less compute, and that the order book was intact while the multiple was not. Forgent's bookings this morning are the strongest evidence for that view we have seen, and they arrived from a company with no narrative attached. The SoftBank recovery is consistent with the same call but for the wrong reason, and we would not claim it.

On Saturday we said the operating leverage inside this theme had moved to the suppliers of physical infrastructure. Electrical distribution equipment booking at 3.3x with a tripled backlog is that thesis in its purest available form, and it is now the part of the AI complex we would most want to own and the part the index pays least attention to.

Everything above is a pre-market mark. The cash open, the Saudi repair timeline and tomorrow at 2pm can each change the entire picture.
