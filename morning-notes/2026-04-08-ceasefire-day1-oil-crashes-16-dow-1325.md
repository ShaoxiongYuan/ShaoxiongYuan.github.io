At 6:30 PM ET on Tuesday, 90 minutes before he planned to destroy Iran's entire civilian infrastructure, Donald Trump agreed to a two-week ceasefire brokered by Pakistan. The Dow surged 1,325 points (+2.85%). Oil crashed 16.4% to $94.41, the largest single-day drop since the war began. Airlines rallied 12%. The global market celebrated its best day in a year: KOSPI +6.87%, Nikkei +5.39%, DAX +5.06%. But the ceasefire is already in trouble. Israel launched the largest strike on Lebanon since the war began, hitting 100 Hezbollah targets in 10 minutes and killing 250+, within hours of the truce. Iran says the ceasefire has been violated on three counts. Hormuz is "open" but only 4 dry cargo ships transited on Day 1, zero oil tankers, versus 130 per day pre-war. The English and Persian versions of Iran's 10-point plan disagree on uranium enrichment. VP Vance heads to Islamabad Saturday for the most consequential diplomatic talks of the 21st century with a two-week window to achieve what decades of diplomacy couldn't. Ceasefire probability of holding through the two-week period: 55–60%. Probability of a permanent peace deal: 25–30%.

## The Day the War Paused: A Timeline of the Most Dramatic 12 Hours Since February 28

### Morning: "A Whole Civilization Will Die Tonight"

The day began with Trump's most extreme rhetoric of the entire war. On Truth Social, he warned that "a whole civilization will die tonight" and will "never be brought back" if Iran does not fully open the Strait of Hormuz by his 8 PM ET deadline. The message was unambiguous: simultaneous destruction of all power plants, bridges, and civilian infrastructure.

At the same time, video emerged from Iran showing hundreds of civilians forming **human chains around the Kazerun combined cycle power plant** in Fars province, near the Persian Gulf, answering a government call to serve as human shields. Additional human chains formed around bridges in Tehran and Isfahan. Trump called the tactic "totally illegal" in an NBC interview.

The world was watching what appeared to be the countdown to the most destructive night of the war.

### Midday: Markets Paralyzed

US markets spent the day in a narrow range, unable to price the binary outcome. The S&P 500 drifted between -0.3% and +0.3% all session. Oil was volatile but anchored near $113. The VIX hovered at 25, elevated but not spiking. Nobody wanted to take a position ahead of 8 PM.

### Late Afternoon: Pakistan Makes Its Move

Behind the scenes, Pakistan's Field Marshal Asim Munir and PM Shehbaz Sharif worked the phones "all night long" (Pakistani time) with VP JD Vance, envoy Steve Witkoff, and Iranian FM Araghchi. Pakistan proposed a **two-week ceasefire extension**, giving both sides time to negotiate in Islamabad rather than escalate in the field.

This was the diplomatic breakthrough. Pakistan converted Trump's deadline from a destruction trigger into a negotiation accelerator.

### 6:30 PM ET: Trump Announces Ceasefire

At approximately 6:30 PM ET, Trump posted on Truth Social: he agreed to **suspend the bombing and attack of Iran for two weeks**, "based on conversations with Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir, of Pakistan." The suspension was "subject to the Islamic Republic of Iran agreeing to the COMPLETE, IMMEDIATE, and SAFE OPENING of the Strait of Hormuz."

Trump added that Iran has proposed a "workable" 10-point peace plan. Both sides will send delegations to **Islamabad for face-to-face negotiations starting Saturday, April 11.**

Iran's **Supreme National Security Council** confirmed acceptance of the two-week ceasefire.

### What the Ceasefire Includes

- **US suspends all strikes on Iran** for two weeks (through approximately April 21).
- **Iran agrees to "complete, immediate, and safe opening"** of the Strait of Hormuz, though the definition of "complete" and "immediate" is already in dispute.
- **Face-to-face talks in Islamabad** beginning Saturday. VP Vance leads the US delegation, alongside Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Vance is the most senior US official to visit Pakistan since 2011.
- **Iran's 10-point plan** forms the basis for negotiation. Key demands: permanent end to war, safe Hormuz passage under Iranian sovereignty, lifting sanctions, ending regional hostilities including Lebanon, reconstruction assistance.

### What the Ceasefire Does NOT Include

**Lebanon is not included.** Trump and Netanyahu explicitly stated the ceasefire does not cover Israel's operations against Hezbollah in Lebanon. Iran and Pakistan say it does. This is the fault line that threatens to collapse the entire framework before Saturday's talks even begin.

## The Ceasefire's First Crisis: Within Hours

### Israel Launches Largest Lebanon Strike of the War

Within hours of the ceasefire announcement, Israel launched what it called its **largest attack on Lebanon since the war began**:

- **100 Hezbollah targets struck in 10 minutes.** The operational tempo was extraordinary, a clear signal that Israel intended to exploit the US-Iran ceasefire to escalate against Hezbollah without constraint.
- **250+ killed, 1,160 wounded** in Lebanon. The country declared a national day of mourning.
- Israeli officials justified the strikes by asserting the US-Iran ceasefire "does not include a pause in its fight against Hezbollah."
- **Vance later said** Israel had "offered to restrain" Lebanon strikes during the Islamabad talks, but offered, not committed.

### Iran Says Ceasefire Already Violated

Iran's parliamentary speaker accused the US of violating three provisions of the 10-point ceasefire proposal within the first day:

1. **Israel's attacks on Lebanon**, which Iran considers part of the ceasefire. The US and Israel do not.
2. **A drone entered Iranian airspace**, suggesting surveillance operations continued.
3. **Iran denied the right to enrich uranium**, as the English and Persian versions of the 10-point plan diverge on this critical issue.

President Pezeshkian warned that continued Israeli attacks on Lebanon would make the peace talks "meaningless."

### The English vs. Persian Problem

A potentially deal-breaking discovery: Trump wrote that the 10 points Iran published publicly were "different than the 10 that were given to the U.S." The Persian version of Iran's plan reportedly diverges from the English version on uranium enrichment, the single most sensitive issue. If the two sides can't even agree on what the framework says, Saturday's talks face a foundational problem.

### Hormuz: "Open" in Name, Closed in Practice

Despite Iran's agreement to "completely and immediately" open Hormuz:

- **Only 4 ships** passed through on the first day of the truce, all dry cargo, no oil or gas tankers.
- Pre-war average: 130 ships per day.
- The Strait remains "effectively closed" for energy trade.
- Iran is charging **tolls** on ships that do pass.

The market's oil reaction is pricing a gradual reopening over the two-week ceasefire period. But if oil tankers aren't moving through Hormuz within 48–72 hours, the ceasefire premium evaporates.

## Vance to Islamabad: The Most Important Talks Since the War Began

VP JD Vance will lead the US delegation to Islamabad starting Saturday, April 11:

- **US delegation**: Vance, Witkoff, Kushner. The VP-level representation signals this is serious, not performative.
- **Iran delegation**: FM Araghchi + senior officials. The composition will signal whether Iran's military (IRGC) or diplomatic (Foreign Ministry) wing is driving.
- **Location**: Serena Hotel, Islamabad. Pakistan has reserved the entire property.
- **Framework**: Iran's 10-point plan as the starting position. But the English and Persian versions differ on uranium enrichment.
- **Timeline**: Two-week ceasefire gives talks until approximately April 21. That's 10 days from Saturday, extremely compressed for a peace deal.

**The four obstacles:**
1. **Lebanon**: Iran says included, US/Israel say not. This could collapse talks before they start.
2. **Uranium enrichment**: The English vs. Persian discrepancy is a dealbreaker if not resolved privately.
3. **Sanctions**: Iran wants them lifted. The US hasn't offered this. The gap is enormous.
4. **Hormuz**: "Open" but not really. If tankers aren't flowing by Saturday, Iran's credibility is questioned. If they are, the US loses its leverage.
