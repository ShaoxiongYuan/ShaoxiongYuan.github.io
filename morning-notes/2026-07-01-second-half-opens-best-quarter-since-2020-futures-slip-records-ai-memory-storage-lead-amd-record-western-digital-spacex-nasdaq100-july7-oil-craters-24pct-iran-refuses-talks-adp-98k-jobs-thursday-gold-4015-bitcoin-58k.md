Wednesday, July 1, 2026, written pre-bell, and the second half opens the morning after the strongest quarter US equities have posted since 2020. Tuesday sealed it with another set of record closes, the Dow finishing at 52,319.20 for a second straight all-time high, the S&P 500 at 7,449.36, and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,213.72. Now the tape exhales. Futures are slipping this morning, with the S&P down about 0.3 percent and the Nasdaq 100 off roughly 0.4 percent, the first real give-back after a run that took the index from last week's financing scare to fresh records in barely three sessions. Three things frame the day. The AI complex is still doing the leading, with memory, storage, and the space names setting the pace. Oil is in an outright collapse that is quietly doing the Fed's disinflation work. And a labor market that is visibly cooling now hands the whole story to Thursday's jobs report, which has been pulled forward into a holiday-shortened week.

## Top Call: Records Into a Payrolls Trap

The one thing PMs need to carry into the open is that this is a market priced for perfection walking into the single number that can break the spell. The quarter that just closed was extraordinary, and the leadership underneath it is real, not froth, with memory pricing power and data-center demand that keep validating themselves. But the melt-up has left the S&P at an all-time high with the VIX asleep in the mid-17s, and this morning's ADP print already told you the labor market is softening faster than the tape wants to admit. Private payrolls rose just 98,000 in June against a 118,000 estimate, the second straight soft read. That sets up Thursday's official jobs report as a two-sided trap. A soft number revives the rate-cut story and the bulls get to keep the party going. A number that is soft in the wrong way, weak hiring alongside sticky wages, hands the stagflation crowd its evidence and the long-duration winners that led this quarter give back the most. Our read is to respect the trend and disrespect the complacency. We would not chase records two days ahead of a payrolls print this loaded, and we would rather own the cash-generative memory and compute leaders than the funding-dependent tail if the tape turns.

## AI Leadership: AMD Prints a Record and the Memory-Storage Trade Broadens

The clearest sign this is still a genuine bull leadership and not a defensive rotation is where the money is going. AMD ripped to an intraday record of 579.73 on Tuesday and closed up more than 7 percent near 577.61, with Wells Fargo lifting its target to 615 from 505 on the read that AMD is finally taking credible share in AI accelerators. The move matters because it widens the leadership beyond Nvidia and the memory names into the second credible compute vendor, which is what a durable theme looks like rather than a single-stock mania.

The louder story is storage, the least glamorous corner of the build-out and suddenly one of the best trades in the market. Barclays raised Western Digital to 620 from 450, and the stock, hovering just under 550, is up more than 950 percent over the trailing twelve months as the same AI capacity crunch that made memory scarce spreads into high-capacity drives. Seagate is riding the same wave. The mechanism is simple and powerful. Producers keep redirecting output toward higher-margin data-center customers, which starves phones and PCs and pushes pricing up across the whole stack. Micron remains the anchor after guiding to roughly 50 billion dollars of revenue next quarter against a 43.6 billion consensus, with its 2026 high-bandwidth memory capacity already sold out. Our take is that the memory and storage complex is the highest-quality way to stay long AI right now because these names are self-funding on real pricing power, which is exactly the quality the market rewarded last week when it started punishing debt-funded ambition elsewhere.

## Space: SpaceX Joins the Nasdaq-100 on July 7 and Rocket Lab Builds the Number Two

The space trade has graduated from a story into an index event. SpaceX is set to join the Nasdaq-100 before the open on July 7, one of the fastest inclusions in the exchange's history, and J.P. Morgan estimates the rebalancing alone forces roughly 4.3 billion dollars of passive buying as QQQ and every fund tracking the benchmark has to own it. That is mechanical demand into a name with a small public float, and it is why the stock keeps grinding higher. This morning SpaceX is up about 2 percent to 174.32 pre-market after Wedbush initiated coverage at Outperform with a 190 target, a clean round trip and then some from the panic lows two weeks ago.

Underneath the headline name, the sector now has a genuine second player. Rocket Lab's 8 billion dollar cash-and-stock acquisition of Iridium, announced Monday, hands it a 66-satellite constellation, globally licensed L-band spectrum, and roughly 2.55 million subscribers, positioning it as the clear number two in commercial launch and satellite communications. Our read is that the space complex has become a two-tier investable group rather than a single dominant private-turned-public name, and the July 7 inclusion is a near-term, high-conviction catalyst with a known buyer on a known date. The risk is that a passive-flow event is precisely the kind of setup that tops out on the day the forced buying finishes, so the inclusion is a reason to own it into July 7, not a reason to chase it after.

## Oil: A 24% Quarterly Collapse as the Peace Dividend Floods the Market

Crude is the quarter's other big story and it runs in the opposite direction. WTI is down another 1.1 percent this morning to 68.71 and Brent is off to 72.25, and the bigger number is the quarter, with WTI down roughly 24 percent over the three months and about 19 percent in June alone. The entire spring war premium has been wrung out and then some. The driver is a supply picture that is loosening fast on both the geopolitical and the fundamental side. Iran says it has shipped more than 40 million barrels since the US naval blockade came off, Russian exports have surged to records, and OPEC keeps adding barrels into a market that no longer fears a Hormuz closure. Analysts are now openly warning about a glut in the back half of the year.

For portfolios the implication is the friendliest crosscurrent in the whole setup. Cheap energy is a direct tax cut for the consumer and a powerful disinflationary force feeding straight into the numbers the Fed is watching, and it is a big part of why European inflation prints came in soft this week. That is the bull case for the rate-cut narrative surviving Thursday. The catch is that oil this low is also a growth signal worth respecting. Some of this drop is peace and rising supply, but some of it is softer demand, and a market cheering disinflation should at least ask whether crude is also telling it the economy is cooling faster than equities at record highs are priced for.

## Iran: A Ceasefire Extension Within Reach, but Tehran Is Walking Away From the Table

The geopolitical file is the swing factor that keeps a floor under oil and a bid under gold, and it took a mixed turn overnight. The good news is that Washington and Tehran are reported to have reached an initial understanding to extend the ceasefire and keep the Strait of Hormuz open, building on the 60-day memorandum the two presidents signed in mid-June, with Oman delivering a fresh Hormuz proposal through the mediators. Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were in Doha meeting the Qatari prime minister to push it forward.

The bad news is the tone out of Tehran. Iran is publicly refusing further US talks, Qatar has said no high-level meetings are actually taking place, and Iranian officials are calling the strait situation sensitive and complex, which is diplomatic language for leverage. So the framework exists on paper and the strait is open in practice, but the process is stalling exactly where last weekend's flare-up showed it is most fragile. Our read is that the market is right to treat open shipping lanes as the base case, since that is the only variable that moves the real economy, but wrong to treat the deal as done. The cheapest hedge in the market is still the one nobody wants to pay for with the tape at records, and a second breakdown before the ceasefire window closes is the headline that would put a fast bid back into crude and gold.

## Macro: The Labor Market Cracks Just as the Fed Turns Hawkish

The tension at the center of this market is that the data is softening while the Fed is talking tougher. This morning's ADP miss at 98,000 is the second soft private-payrolls read in a row, and it lands two days before the official June jobs report, which has been pulled forward to Thursday because Friday is an early close ahead of the Independence Day holiday and the market is shut. ISM Manufacturing is also due today, expected to slip to about 53.8 from 54.0, still in expansion but losing momentum, alongside construction spending and the weekly oil inventories. The setup is delicate. Core PCE ran hot at 3.4 percent for May and at least one Fed official has floated a 2026 hike, yet the labor and growth data are cooling and oil is collapsing. Thursday's payrolls is where those two stories collide, and it is unusually dominant this week because earnings are light and the calendar is thin. Treasuries are quiet ahead of it, the ten-year near 4.40 percent and the two-year around 4.13 percent, a curve that is content to wait for the number rather than pick a side.

## Asia and Europe: Records Pause on Both Sides

The overnight tape was a mirror of New York, a pause rather than a reversal after a strong run. Asia was mixed to slightly lower, with Japan's Nikkei giving back a fraction of Tuesday's surge to sit near 70,410, Hong Kong's Hang Seng easing about half a percent, and mainland China drifting modestly lower, the region consolidating record-adjacent levels rather than selling off. Europe opened softer after closing at all-time highs on Tuesday, when the Euro Stoxx 50 and the Stoxx 600 both set records on the back of soft June inflation prints across Germany, France, and Italy that all but confirmed the war-driven inflation spike has crested. This morning the Stoxx 600 is down about 0.2 percent and the DAX off around 0.4 percent, a gentle give-back from records rather than any change in trend. The pattern across all three regions is the same and worth naming. After a powerful quarter that ended at highs almost everywhere, the world's equity markets are collectively taking a breath and waiting on the same catalyst, Thursday's US payrolls.

## Geopolitical Tracker

**US-Iran:** A framework to extend the ceasefire and keep the Strait of Hormuz open is reportedly within reach, with Oman delivering a fresh proposal and US envoys meeting Qatari mediators in Doha. But Tehran is publicly refusing further talks and Qatar says no high-level meetings are happening, so the process is stalling even as shipping lanes stay open. This remains the single largest swing factor for oil and gold, and last weekend proved how quickly it can break.

**Ukraine-Russia:** Structurally unresolved and overshadowed by the Gulf, with Russian oil exports at record levels adding directly to the global supply glut now pressuring crude. Combat continues along the eastern axes and a pending US security package is the live headline risk.

**Taiwan Strait and South China Sea:** PRC coast guard pressure near the Pratas Islands continues at a low simmer with no major escalation. The pattern of incremental activity persists but stays contained.
