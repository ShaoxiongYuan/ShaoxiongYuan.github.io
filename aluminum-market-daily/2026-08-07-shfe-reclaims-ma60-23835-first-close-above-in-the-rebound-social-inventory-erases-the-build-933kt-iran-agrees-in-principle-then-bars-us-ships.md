It was decided, and decisively. AL2609 opened at 23,785, ran to 23,905 and closed at 23,835, which is 94.00 yuan above an MA60 that fell again to 23,741 — the first close above the 60-day in this entire rebound, and the second consecutive session in which the line was touched, except that this time it was kept. That qualification matters and is recorded, but it does not change the classification: a level that was pierced and surrendered on Wednesday was pierced and held on Thursday, and the analysts producing the underlying caliber raised their own stated core range in response, from 23,500–24,000 to 23,500–24,100 on SHFE and from $3,150–3,300 to $3,200–3,350 on LME. Raising a core range is the cleanest available admission that the prior range no longer contains the market. But the composition inverted for a second straight session, and this is the part that deserves more scepticism than the technical read. On August 5 the physical market led — A00 up 230, the basis flipping to premium, three regions destocking — while the board stalled at +20. On August 6 the exact reverse: the board gained 70 against settlement and 90 against Wednesday's close, while SMM A00 fell 40 yuan to 23,690 and quotes slipped back to a discount of 10 to parity. In two sessions the leadership has changed hands twice. The honest reading is not that physical has confirmed the board or that the board has dragged physical; it is that neither is leading, and the two are alternating around a centre that is rising anyway. What is actually carrying the move is the third fact, and it is the one that dismantles three sessions of this brief's own bearish column: China's aluminum ingot social inventory printed 933,000 mt against the 958,000 mt carried since August 3. Last week's +5,000 mt build — which this brief repeatedly identified as the first accumulation of the cycle and treated as the turn — has been erased at five times its own magnitude in a single week. The correct inference is not that the build was wrong but that it was noise inside a destocking trend that remains intact, and SMM's own framing supports the more cautious version: destocking has SLOWED as the price centre rose, and the cast aluminum alloy series destocked for a tenth week but by only 200 mt. Against that, the newest demand print is bad — the downstream processing operating rate fell to 60.1% from 61.1%, so the drawdown remains supply-composition-led, driven by the liquid-aluminum proportion rising above expectation in July with a further increase expected in August, rather than by consumption. Fewer ingots are being cast; that is a real reason for ingot inventory to fall and it is not the same thing as demand. The geopolitical column did something genuinely unusual on August 6: the deal advanced and its terms retreated in the same session. Iran said the arrangement with Oman is agreed in principle, which by the logic this brief has applied for three days should be the largest single bearish event of the cycle. Yet what accompanied it points the other way. Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi said the agreement would not mean full reopening of the sea lane. Tehran insisted the United States is not a party to it. An Iranian parliamentary committee began reviewing a draft bill barring US, Israeli and other 'hostile' vessels outright, with fines up to 20% of cargo value and restrictions extending to any country that has 'caused damage to Iran' until compensated. Washington rejected any impediments. And Iranian forces fired on 'hostile targets' at the entrance to the Strait, with two explosions reported on Qeshm Island Thursday night. Crude adjudicated immediately: WTI, which had collapsed 11% across three sessions to near $75 while pricing the deal, went back above $78 on a near-3% gain, with Brent at $83.46 and rising 1.18% again into Friday. That reversal is the most useful confirmation available, because oil is the instrument with the most direct exposure to the Strait and the least aluminum-specific noise. The structural point underneath all of it is simple and worth stating plainly. The market discounted a reopening. What has been agreed in principle is a bilateral traffic-management arrangement between two coastal states, explicitly described by one of them as not constituting a reopening, from which the principal counterparty is absent by that same party's insistence, and alongside which the same state's legislature is drafting a statutory ban on that counterparty's shipping. The two physical preconditions this brief has tracked — mine clearing actually beginning and the US naval blockade actually lifting — remain unmet, and Thursday night added a third adverse fact by putting live fire back at the Strait's entrance.
