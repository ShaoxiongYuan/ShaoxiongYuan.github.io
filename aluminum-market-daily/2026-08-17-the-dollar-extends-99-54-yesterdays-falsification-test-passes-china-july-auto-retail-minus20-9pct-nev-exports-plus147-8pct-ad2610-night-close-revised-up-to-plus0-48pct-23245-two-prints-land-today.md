SECOND, THE REVISION, WHICH IS SMALL BUT POINTS THE RIGHT WAY. The weekend wrap put the cast-aluminium-alloy main contract at +0.24% in the Friday night session. SMM's cast-alloy morning comment at 08:30 today closes it out properly: AD2610 opened 23,170, sold off early, and then rebounded to finish 23,245, +0.48% — twice the carried figure, and the shape of the session (dip, then recovery into the close) is the more useful detail. That makes the Friday night ranking within the aluminium complex unambiguous: alloy +0.48%, aluminium flat, alumina −0.19%. The cost end gave ground and the downstream-adjacent end held, which is the ordering a floor looks like rather than the ordering a breakdown looks like. Against that, the same article records registered cast-alloy warrants building 333 mt to 18,311, and repeats that ADC12's own outlook is range-bound with off-season demand capping it. THIRD, THE ITEM FROM OUTSIDE THE METAL, AND IT IS THE HEAVIEST NEW FACT OF THE WEEKEND. CPCA's July passenger-vehicle data, carried by SMM on August 16, has nationwide retail at 1.461 million units, −20.9% year on year. Internal-combustion retail fell 41%. NEV retail fell 3.9% and NEV penetration reached 65.1%. Automotive is one of the demand pillars this brief is required to track, and a fifth of the domestic leg disappeared in a single month. The offsetting line is genuine and large: NEV exports rose 147.8% to 540,000 units, which is aluminium content leaving the country rather than aluminium content not being consumed — the units still get built. But the composition is the point. A demand column that increasingly depends on exports is a demand column exposed to tariffs, to the narrowing China-overseas price spread that SMM has already flagged as eroding billet export momentum, and to precisely the trade-policy risk that sits unresolved in the geopolitics column. Read alongside the July credit contraction confirmed on Saturday and the 42.2% processing PMI with all seven sub-sectors contracting, the domestic demand thesis for aluminium is now weak in credit, weak in processing, weak in property, and weak in autos, with billet named by its own analyst as having peaked in July. FOURTH, THE CROSS-METAL COMPARISON, WHICH THIS BRIEF HAS BEEN CIRCLING FOR A WEEK AND SHOULD NOW STATE PLAINLY. Copper closed Friday at $14,172.50 on SMM's caliber, +0.26%, a seventh consecutive weekly gain, with LME stocks down for a 42nd consecutive day to 204,975 mt, an August–September spread near $370 and a cash-to-3M backwardation near $434. The comparison is not an argument that aluminium should trade like copper; the feedstock stories are different, and copper has a concentrate crisis with negative treatment charges for a nineteenth month that aluminium does not have. It is an argument about what a tight curve is worth when the market believes the tightness. Copper's curve is being paid for. Aluminium's is being noted. Until aluminium's own backwardation widens beyond a rounding error, the LME stock series should be treated as a floor argument rather than a rally argument, and this brief has been carrying it slightly too far up the bull column. FIFTH, AND THIS IS WHY THE EDITION IS DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED: the two facts that would actually settle the direction both land today and neither is in this brief. If destocking continues from that base, the inventory column has moved from seasonal to structural and the bulls keep the one leg they have. If it turns to a build, then the August 13 print gets re-read as a one-off caused by high prices deferring arrivals, and the bulls have nothing unweakened left. THE SHAPE OF THE MORNING, THEN: a dollar that passed its test, an alloy print revised the bulls' way, an auto print that removes the last downstream pillar anyone was still leaning on, a curve comparison that says the market does not yet believe aluminium's own tightness, and two pending numbers that decide the week.
