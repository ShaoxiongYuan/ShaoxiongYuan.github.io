Aluminium's domestic demand thesis has been running on a single leg — inventory — for three weeks, with the processing PMI at 42.2% and all seven sub-sectors contracting; the credit data says the financing channel that would fund a second-half recovery is not merely weak but shrinking. THIRD, the billet column, which until this weekend was the one downstream series still pointing up, has now been dated. SMM's August 15 note puts July's operating rate at 58.3% — a year-to-date high, a three-year high for the period, +5.2 pp year on year — and forecasts a retreat to about 56.9% in August. A peak that is named as a peak by the analyst who computes it is a different object from a high print. Worse, the mechanism SMM identifies is structural rather than seasonal: processing fees have already fallen below the industry average production cost, and some extruders have switched to remelted-ingot substitutes, which puts a ceiling on the fee no matter what the operating rate does. The one offsetting detail is real and should be recorded: both social and in-factory billet inventories remain low versus the same period in recent years, which preserves some pricing power for holders. FOURTH, Alunorte. The August 12 edition treated the cut to 50% at a 6.3 mt/yr refinery as the single largest supply-side item of the cycle, and it is now partially mitigated: Alunorte has procured a spot LNG cargo from a European supplier — notably, one that had previously supplied New Fortress for the Barcarena terminal — and has requested direct access to the regasification terminal itself. The cut has not been reversed and Hydro's USD 75–100 million Q3 estimate is unchanged, but the tail scenario in which the refinery stays at half rate for a quarter because it has no route to gas is now materially less likely. That is bearish for the alumina cost floor that has been quietly supporting aluminium's own floor. FIFTH, and this one is a non-event correctly identified as a non-event, which is itself worth recording: Indonesia's magnitude-7.7 Flores earthquake would in most weeks have generated a supply scare. SMM's assessment is that the aluminium-chain impact is near zero — bauxite, alumina and the overwhelming majority of smelting capacity sit more than 1,400 km west of the epicentre, the nearest smelter is 624 km away and outside the 300 km damage radius, and the tsunami wave was under 0.4 m. A brief that carried this as a bullish supply headline would be wrong, and it is listed here so that it cannot be. Set against all of that, the bull column's two load-bearing facts are unchanged because nothing happened to change them, and both remain formidable: the 898,000 mt Thursday social-inventory print, the first below 900,000 mt and the steepest Thursday draw of the series, and the LME complex, where stocks fell for a 34th consecutive session to 248,300 mt and the cash-to-3M curve stayed in backwardation at +$2.00 while the flat price was falling. The oil and geopolitics column is also unchanged and still bullish at the margin: Brent gained 5–6.3% on the week, Hegseth has called the blockade indefinite, CENTCOM has now redirected 59 vessels since July 13, Bessent has flagged unprecedented isolation measures for this week, and the fee-free Hormuz window expires around today. The honest summary of the weekend is that the price's nearby evidence — inventory, curve, spot premium in South China — did not weaken at all, while the evidence about what happens NEXT — credit, billet, alumina cost, the dollar — weakened in four places at once. That is the configuration in which a market holds its floor and cannot reclaim its averages, which is precisely what the Friday night session did in miniature. Two events resolve the ambiguity and both land Monday: China's July activity data and the Monday social-inventory print, on the same day."
