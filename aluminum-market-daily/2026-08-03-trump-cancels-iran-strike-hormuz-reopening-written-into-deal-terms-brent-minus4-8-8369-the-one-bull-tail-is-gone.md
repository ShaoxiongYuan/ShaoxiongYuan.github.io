## 5 · Fundamentals

<span class="lbl lbl-data">Data — overseas supply, the section that changed most today</span>

- **Gulf structural facts (carried): the Gulf is about 9% of global primary supply; EGA Al Taweelah (1.50 Mt/yr) and Alba (1.60 Mt/yr) were struck by Iranian missiles and drones on March 28; the region also holds Ma'aden, Qatalum and Sohar.**
- **The critical distinction: Alba's Lines 1, 2 and 3 — roughly 311,000 mt/yr, 19% of its total capacity — were placed in a controlled shutdown because of Strait of Hormuz transit disruption, not because of physical damage.** <span class="lbl lbl-view">View</span> **That means the restart condition for this capacity is logistics, not engineering — if the Strait genuinely opens, it comes back materially faster than damaged capacity does.**
- **Quantified return potential: roughly 700,000 mt of aluminium has been identified as capable of flowing into the market on a Hormuz reopening.**
- **EGA: as of July 2, 89 of Al Taweelah's 1,262 pots were operating, anode removal complete and pot cleaning about 90% done; its alumina refinery restarted in July after a three-and-a-half-month outage, produced 2.4 Mt in 2025 covering 46% of EGA's needs, and is expected to be technically capable of full output by year-end; repair progress has been reported as ahead of schedule.**
- **July ex-China daily average output +1.6% m/m, the first monthly recovery since the March strikes, with the total still −6.7% y/y; Slovalco 75,000 mt in Q4, Magnitude 7 Metals 75,000 mt/yr by end-2026, Balco 168,000 mt in FY27 Q2 (+17% y/y), Alcoa 636,000 mt in Q2 (+5% q/q), Rio Tinto raising Kitimat and NZAS.**
- <span class="lbl lbl-view">View</span> **Overseas supply recovery had two binding constraints: engineering repair progress, and Hormuz transit.** **The first was already easing — EGA ahead of schedule, July output positive m/m. The second is the one that changed today.** **Both loosening at once is a first for this cycle.**

<span class="lbl lbl-data">Data — domestic supply and cost (newly published August 2–3)</span>

- <span class="lbl lbl-view">View</span> **A 6,999-yuan margin is extremely wide by any historical standard.** **A 1,146-yuan narrowing sounds negative, but at that absolute level cost imposes no constraint whatsoever on output — no operating smelter cuts production because its margin fell from 8,145 to 6,999.** **So "rising costs" cannot be converted into a bullish supply argument here; it is an income-statement event, not a supply event.**
